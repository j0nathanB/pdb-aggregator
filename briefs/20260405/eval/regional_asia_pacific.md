## Regional Lead (post-editorial chain)

Cheng Li-wun, chair of Taiwan's main opposition KMT party, accepted Xi Jinping's invitation to visit China this week — the first such trip by a KMT leader in a decade — even as American defense cooperation with Taiwan stalls in Congress. The timing shows how China and the United States are pulling Taiwan in opposite directions: Beijing draws Taiwan's opposition closer while Washington struggles to deliver promised military support.

Three regional powers coordinated their response to the Middle East energy crisis, working together more closely than their treaties require. Japan activated energy reserves — over four months of naphtha supplies and eight months of oil — while negotiating rare earth deals with France. South Korea mobilized a 26.2 trillion won supplementary budget for Middle East crisis response. Australia joined talks with over 40 countries on reopening Strait of Hormuz shipping lanes while rolling out fuel crisis spending. The similar responses across different alliances — US-Japan, US-ROK, AUKUS — show countries acting on the same fears without coordinating their plans.

Indonesia collected major investment commitments from both Japan and South Korea during Prabowo Subianto's state visits this week. South Korea alone committed $10.2 billion in expanded deals, while Japan offered competing packages across multiple sectors. The timing suggests competition where each country must match or exceed offers to keep access to Indonesian markets. Indonesia's position between competing powers lets it demand bigger economic payoffs for continued cooperation.

Countries can coordinate crisis responses bilaterally but regional institutions remain absent. The rapid, large-scale economic mobilization happened despite different frameworks, while Indonesia lost three UN peacekeepers in Lebanon with little regional response.

## Gap Paragraphs

Regional institutions failed to respond to the Middle East crisis through ASEAN, CPTPP, or other multilateral frameworks. The gap suggests Asia-Pacific regional institutions lack the ability to respond to crises beyond the region, with countries turning to bilateral and alliance frameworks rather than regional bodies.
