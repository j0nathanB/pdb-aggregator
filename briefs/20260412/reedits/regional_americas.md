Political trouble hit three of the Americas' largest countries this week, each facing a different crisis.

Canada's Liberal government gained a majority through floor-crossings while the Conservative opposition turned on their leadership, with up to 40 MPs questioning their leaders. Brazil's 2026 election is now tied between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro, prompting a cabinet reshuffle and party scrambles. Chile's governing coalition fractured as José Antonio Kast's approval rating dropped to 36% and a cabinet minister was physically attacked.

Mexico stood apart, showing stability while working on economic projects including a $540 million development project in Tlaxcala and reversing its fracking policy to address natural gas dependence.

The political crises did not spill over into foreign policy. Despite domestic upheaval, none of the three countries pulled back from international commitments or turned inward.
