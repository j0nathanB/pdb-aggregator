Three of four big countries in the Americas face domestic political crisis this week. Canada's Liberal government gained a majority when MPs switched parties, while the Conservative opposition is in revolt — up to 40 MPs are challenging their leadership. Brazil's 2026 race is a dead heat between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro, spurring cabinet changes and party reshuffles. Chile's governing coalition has split between Chile Vamos and Partido Republicano as José Antonio Kast's approval fell to 36% and someone attacked a cabinet minister.

Mexico is different. While its neighbours grapple with political crisis, Mexico is pressing ahead with big economic projects, including the $540 million Tlaxcala Economic Development Pole and a shift on fracking policy to cut natural gas dependency.

The domestic turmoil in Canada, Brazil and Chile has not led to less international engagement or isolationist policies. Each country's foreign ministry works independently of its domestic political crises.
