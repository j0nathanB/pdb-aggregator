Pakistan deployed fighter jets and troops to Saudi Arabia this week while hosting 21-hour US-Iran mediation talks in Islamabad. The deployment is the first military use of Pakistan's Strategic Mutual Defense Agreement with Saudi Arabia, moving beyond symbolic cooperation. But Pakistan's mediation role proves that military alliances need not block diplomatic flexibility.

Saudi Arabia called in Pakistani military support while talking directly to Iran in the first foreign minister call since the war began. Iranian forces fired 2,819 projectiles at the UAE, causing the first direct casualties at the Habshan gas facility, while the UAE hosted S Jaishankar for his first Gulf visit since the war began and sent Mohammed bin Zayed, the president, to lead a delegation to China.

Countries are pursuing military action and diplomacy together rather than choosing between them. The timing suggests either coordination or at least agreement that the Iran crisis demands both diplomatic engagement and military preparation. Each country keeps multiple channels open.

The key is separation. Pakistan provides military support to Saudi Arabia while mediating between Iran and the United States. Saudi Arabia accepts military support while calling Tehran directly. The UAE frames Iranian attacks as "economic terrorism" through ADNOC while maintaining its investment activities and hosting Indian diplomatic visits. Regional powers are managing multiple relationships at once.

The Gulf Cooperation Council did not respond as a group despite Iranian attacks on multiple member states. This suggests either that the GCC is too weak to act during emergencies or that member states prefer to handle crises bilaterally rather than through the regional body.
