Pakistan deployed fighter jets and troops to Saudi Arabia's King Abdulaziz Air Base this week while hosting US-Iran mediation talks in Islamabad. The deployment marked the first time the two countries' mutual defence pact moved beyond symbolism, while Pakistan tried to broker peace between Washington and Tehran. Saudi Arabia's foreign minister made his first call to Iran since the conflict began, keeping diplomatic channels open while requesting Pakistani military support.

The pattern is regional. The UAE hosted S. Jaishankar, India's external affairs minister, while Mohammed bin Zayed, the UAE's president, led a delegation to China. The timing suggests coordination rather than coincidence.

Iran fired 2,819 projectiles at UAE infrastructure this week, killing two workers in a strike on the Habshan gas facility—the first confirmed deaths from what ADNOC, the state oil company, calls "economic terrorism." The UAE now describes the attacks as targeting the country's economic lifelines while maintaining investment partnerships abroad.

Countries across the region are escalating and de-escalating simultaneously. Saudi Arabia activated its defence pact with Pakistan while keeping diplomatic channels open to Tehran. The UAE sharpened its rhetoric about "economic terrorism" while preserving foreign investment deals. Pakistan mediated between American and Iranian negotiators while deploying warplanes to defend Saudi airspace. Each country is hedging its bets as Iran tests how much pressure they can handle.

The Gulf Cooperation Council did not coordinate a joint response despite Iranian attacks on multiple member states this week. This suggests either that the organisation is too weak to act in a crisis or that countries prefer to handle Iran bilaterally.
