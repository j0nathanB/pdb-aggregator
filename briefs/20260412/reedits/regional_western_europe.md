EU countries responded to Viktor Orbán's defeat with coordinated celebrations that revealed both their deep frustration with Hungary's leader and their ability to move quickly once he was gone. Germany congratulated the opposition winner, Spain welcomed the result, Norway called it a democratic breakthrough, Sweden said it would improve how the EU works.

Three NATO allies have clashed with Trump without breaking their defence partnerships. Britain shelved the Chagos handover after he withdrew support, then faced his comparison to Chamberlain over energy policy. Norway called his threats to blockade the Strait of Hormuz "problematic" while backing international alternatives. Ulf Kristersson, Sweden's prime minister, disagreed with his NATO approach while continuing defence integration.

Europe is adjusting both how it works internally and how it deals with Washington. Coalition governments across the region face pressure from corruption trials, energy policy failures, and parliamentary defeats — signs that strain runs deeper than the coordinated messaging.

Economic policy coordination among EU members facing Iran war cost pressures did not appear this week. Despite shared exposure to energy price shocks from Middle East conflict, European countries are not coordinating economic responses, suggesting either different impact levels or preference for national over collective solutions.
