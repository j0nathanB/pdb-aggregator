Ukrainian combat drones struck civilian targets across four frontline countries this week, bringing the Russia-Ukraine conflict to their doorsteps. Estonia's Auvere power plant took a direct hit from a stray Ukrainian drone, while Latvia had its first wartime explosion on home soil. Lithuania and Finland faced similar incidents, exposing air defence gaps against low-flying threats and forcing diplomatic protests even as these countries back Ukraine. The incidents force each country to balance support for Kyiv with protecting infrastructure, testing systems built for external threats, not friendly fire.

Even as drones cross borders, governing coalitions are cracking under pressure from war, economic stress and domestic political splits. Three countries face crises that go beyond routine friction into formal breakdown. Andrej Babiš, the Czech prime minister, "screamed with rage" at coalition partners over policy breaches, Lithuania's Social Democrats threaten "political actions" against allies over defence spending priorities, and Romania's Social Democratic Party scheduled a vote on whether to stay in government. These crises threaten steady support for Ukraine and economic crisis response just when regional coordination matters most.

Escalation in the Middle East added another strain as four countries cut fuel prices within days of each other. Poland introduced emergency fuel price cuts, Latvia approved a 15% diesel tax reduction, Romania declared a six-month fuel market emergency with price controls, and Finland prepared broad responses to spillover from the Iran conflict. Conflicts beyond Ukraine create multiple economic shocks forcing immediate government action, but also expose the limits of EU coordination when countries turn to solo measures for domestic price stability.

The timing of these crises — drone spillover, coalition breakdown and economic intervention — means frontline democracies can no longer manage multiple external pressures with current systems. Ukraine itself added to the strain with unprecedented public disputes with the United States over territorial concessions and war strategy, even as its forces continue making territorial gains, creating more uncertainty for allies already juggling war support with domestic stability.

EU co-ordination on economic crisis response was absent this week despite shared frameworks for managing Middle East conflict effects on energy markets. NATO Article 5 consultations were also missing as spillover military incidents were handled bilaterally rather than through Alliance channels, suggesting either that the drone strikes fall below the Article 5 threshold or that NATO procedures are too slow for managing tactical military spillover from partner operations.
