### Columnist warns Labour electoral stance could enable Reform victory

LONDON, Britain — Guardian columnist George Monbiot warned that Prime Minister Keir Starmer's refusal to reform Britain's electoral system could enable the far-right Reform UK party to take power with a minority of votes. Monbiot said Labour's insistence on maintaining the first-past-the-post voting system, despite party conference support for proportional representation, stems from self-interest rather than principle. He cited analysis suggesting Reform UK could win 48% of parliamentary seats with just 27% of the vote under the current system. Monbiot said Starmer previously criticized the electoral system as unfair while seeking party leadership but abandoned reform efforts after taking office. The columnist argued that under proportional representation, Labour would face near elimination based on current Welsh polling data, explaining the party's resistance to change.

### German Chancellor Merz prioritizes economy as unemployment hits 12-year high

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Friday that boosting the economy would be his main focus this year after unemployment surged past 3 million to a 12-year high. Labor Office data showed 177,000 more people out of work in January than December, bringing the total to 3.08 million and pushing the unemployment rate to 6.6%. "The rise in the number of unemployed to more than three million is an alarm signal," Merz wrote on social media platform X. The jobless figures highlighted the lag from economic stagnation despite fourth-quarter GDP growth of 0.3%, which beat forecasts. Economy Minister Katherina Reiche said Germany must pivot toward new "growth engines," arguing that traditional export strengths "no longer carry our growth."

### German chancellor calls Europe alternative to imperialism, autocracy

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Thursday that the European Union represents an "alternative to imperialism and autocracy" as great power rivalries intensify globally. Speaking to parliament about foreign policy, Merz said Europe can forge partnerships with democracies based on "mutual respect, trust and reliability" in an increasingly competitive world. He emphasized that while Germany seeks continued cooperation with the United States, it will not act as a "subordinate" in the relationship. Merz also defended Germany's military deployment in Afghanistan after U.S. President Donald Trump suggested non-American NATO troops avoided front-line combat, noting that 59 German soldiers died during the nearly 20-year mission.

### UK's Starmer arrives in China, first PM visit since 2018

BEIJING, China — British Prime Minister Keir Starmer arrived in China on Wednesday for the first visit by a UK leader since 2018, encouraging businesses to seize opportunities while acknowledging security concerns. "Eight years since a British prime minister stepped on Chinese soil. So on this delegation you're making history," Starmer told more than 50 business leaders accompanying him, according to Reuters. The prime minister said he hoped to make "progress" on visa-free travel between the countries and insisted Britain could strengthen economic ties with China without angering the United States. Starmer is scheduled to meet President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang on Thursday as part of efforts to mend UK-China relations after years of tensions over Hong Kong, China's support for Russia, and espionage allegations.

### Starmer defends China engagement after Trump criticism

SHANGHAI, China — Prime Minister Keir Starmer said Friday it would be "foolhardy" for Britain to ignore China, rejecting President Donald Trump's assertion that closer UK-China ties are "very dangerous." Starmer's comments to the BBC came after three hours of talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping that resulted in China agreeing to cut tariffs on UK whisky and relax visa rules. Trump, responding to questions about the closer UK-China relationship, said "it's very dangerous for them to do that" but did not elaborate. Starmer said the UK-U.S. relationship remains "very close" and that Washington had been made aware of his visit, adding that "for the UK to be the only country that refuses to engage would not be in our national interest."
