### France's National Assembly backs social media ban for under-15s

PARIS, France — France's National Assembly voted 116 to 23 on Monday to approve legislation banning children under 15 from social media platforms, amid growing concerns about online bullying and mental health risks. President Emmanuel Macron said he wants the government to fast-track the legal process to ensure the ban takes effect in September at the start of the next school year. "The brains of our children and our teenagers are not for sale," Macron said in a video released Saturday by French broadcaster BFM-TV. The bill now moves to the Senate before a final vote in the lower house, and would require platforms to block access through age-verification mechanisms compliant with European Union law. Centrist lawmaker Laure Miller, who presented the bill, said "with this law, we are setting a clear boundary in society and saying social media is not harmless."

### France adopts 2026 budget boosting military spending

PARIS, France — France adopted its delayed 2026 budget Monday, clearing the way for higher military spending promised by President Emmanuel Macron to confront threats linked to Russia's war in Ukraine and Middle East conflicts. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu used his constitutional power to pass the bill without a parliamentary vote, surviving two no-confidence votes Monday evening after Parliament proved unable to reach a compromise during a monthslong process. The government is targeting a budget deficit of 5% of gross domestic product, down from 5.4% in 2025, as France faces pressure from the European Union and credit rating agencies to reduce its debt.

### Macron says France preparing to resume Putin dialogue

PARIS, France — French President Emmanuel Macron said Tuesday that France is preparing to resume dialogue with Russian President Vladimir Putin nearly four years after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, though he said Moscow shows no "real willingness" to negotiate a ceasefire. "It is being prepared and so discussions are taking place at a technical level," Macron said during a visit to northeastern France, without providing a specific timeframe. Macron said the preparations are being done "transparently" in consultation with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and European allies, adding that contact with the Kremlin is needed to negotiate security guarantees after the war. "We continue to support Ukraine, which is under bombardment, in the cold, with attacks on civilians and on Ukraine's energy infrastructure by the Russians, which is intolerable and does not demonstrate a genuine willingness to negotiate for peace," Macron said. Dialogue between the European Union and Russia has been virtually frozen since Russia's 2022 offensive, with the bloc imposing extensive sanctions and travel restrictions.
