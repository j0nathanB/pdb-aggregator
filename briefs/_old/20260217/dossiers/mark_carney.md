### Carney Launches Canada's First Defence Industrial Strategy

MONTREAL, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney on Tuesday released Canada's first Defence Industrial Strategy, a plan to prioritize domestic weapons manufacturing and reduce reliance on foreign suppliers. The strategy aims to award 70 per cent of federal defence contracts to Canadian firms within a decade, up from the current 43 per cent, and create 125,000 jobs, senior defence officials said in a technical briefing. The $6.6-billion plan, which includes a "build, partner, buy" framework and a new Defence Investment Agency to streamline procurement, positions Canadian industry to access $180 billion in defence procurement opportunities over the next 10 years, according to the Prime Minister's Office. The release comes days after U.S. President Donald Trump signed an executive order to accelerate American arms sales to allies, though former NATO assistant secretary general for defence investment Wendy Gilmour said she does not believe the two strategies will collide directly.

### Canada Names Charette as Chief Trade Negotiator to U.S.

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney on Monday appointed former top civil servant Janice Charette as Canada's chief trade negotiator to the United States, his office said, ahead of a mandatory joint review of the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement set for a July 1 deadline. Charette, who twice served as clerk of the Privy Council, will act as a senior adviser to Carney and Canada-U.S. Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc on the CUSMA review and the broader trade relationship, the Prime Minister's Office said. The appointment comes a day after Mark Wiseman took over as Canada's ambassador to Washington, replacing Kirsten Hillman, and as President Donald Trump has called CUSMA "irrelevant" and his trade representative has floated breaking the three-way pact into separate deals. Conservative deputy leader Melissa Lantsman said in a statement that Canada does not need "another bureaucrat or negotiator" but rather "results for the thousands of auto, lumber, and steel making jobs lost to the United States."

### Carney Says Bridge Dispute With Trump Will Be Resolved

OTTAWA, Canada — Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Tuesday that he and U.S. President Donald Trump spoke about a range of topics including the $4.7 billion Gordie Howe International Bridge connecting Detroit and Windsor, Ontario, and that the issue will be resolved. Trump had threatened to bar the bridge from opening, citing Canada's financing and control of the project, its tariffs on dairy products and its trade talks with China. Carney said he told Trump that ownership of the bridge is shared between the state of Michigan and the government of Canada, though White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said Trump found it "unacceptable" that Canada would control what crosses the bridge and owns the land on both sides. The New York Times reported that billionaire Matt Moroun, whose family owns the rival Ambassador Bridge, met with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Monday, hours before Trump's statement.

### Carney, Starmer Discuss Trade, Defence Financing in Call

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney spoke with United Kingdom Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer about expanding economic and security ties between the two countries, the Office of the Prime Minister said. Carney outlined efforts to establish a Defence, Security and Resilience Bank, or DSR Bank, which would provide multi-year, low-cost financing for defence investments and unlock private capital for defence and security firms across the supply chain, according to the readout. The two leaders also discussed progress through the Canada-UK Economic and Trade Working Group on digital trade, critical minerals, and sovereign AI infrastructure, as well as ongoing discussions between the European Union and the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership. Carney also thanked Starmer for his condolences following shootings in Tumbler Ridge, British Columbia, last week, the statement said.

### Canada's Supreme Court Annuls Terrebonne Election, Weakening Carney Majority

OTTAWA, Canada — Canada's Supreme Court on Friday annulled the federal election result in the riding of Terrebonne, where Liberal Tatiana Auguste had won by a single vote over Bloc Québécois candidate Nathalie Sinclair-Desgagné, requiring Prime Minister Mark Carney to call a by-election within six months. Chief Justice Richard Wagner said a majority of the court voted to grant the appeal, though the reasons for the non-unanimous decision will be released at a later date. The ruling reduces the Liberal caucus to 168 seats, four short of the 172 needed for a majority, after a postal code error prevented a Bloc supporter's ballot from being counted in the April 28 vote. Liberal spokesperson Matteo Rossi said Auguste would run again, while Sinclair-Desgagné said she had not yet decided whether to seek the nomination, calling the ruling "a great victory for democracy."
