### Carney Joins Opposition Leaders at Tumbler Ridge Shooting Vigil
{: #carney-joins-opposition-leaders-at-tumbler-ridge-shooting-vigil}

TUMBLER RIDGE, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney told residents at a Friday candlelight vigil that Canadians "will always be with you" as the remote British Columbia town mourned nine dead and more than 25 injured in one of Canada's worst mass shootings. Carney was joined by Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre, Bloc Québécois Leader Yves-François Blanchet, NDP Leader Don Davies and Green Party Leader Elizabeth May after the mayor invited them to the town of 2,400, where an 18-year-old shooter on Tuesday killed her mother and half-brother at home before shooting dead a teacher and five students at Tumbler Ridge Secondary School, Royal Canadian Mounted Police said. Carney ordered flags on federal buildings to half-mast for seven days, cancelled a planned trip to the Munich Security Conference and said King Charles and leaders from the U.K., France, Norway, Finland, India and the United Arab Emirates had reached out with condolences. RCMP Deputy Commissioner Dwayne McDonald said the shooter, identified as Jesse Van Rootselaar, was "hunting" and "engaging anybody and everybody" rather than targeting specific individuals before dying by suicide inside the school.

### Carney Launches Canada's First Defence Industrial Strategy
{: #carney-launches-canada-s-first-defence-industrial-strategy}

MONTREAL, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney on Tuesday released Canada's first Defence Industrial Strategy, a plan to prioritize domestic weapons manufacturing and reduce reliance on foreign suppliers. The strategy aims to award 70 per cent of federal defence contracts to Canadian firms within a decade, up from the current 43 per cent, and create 125,000 jobs, senior defence officials said in a technical briefing. The $6.6-billion plan, which includes a "build, partner, buy" framework and a new Defence Investment Agency to streamline procurement, positions Canadian industry to access $180 billion in defence procurement opportunities over the next 10 years, according to the Prime Minister's Office. The release comes days after U.S. President Donald Trump signed an executive order to accelerate American arms sales to allies, though former NATO assistant secretary general for defence investment Wendy Gilmour said she does not believe the two strategies will collide directly.

### Carney Faces Calls to Aid Cuba Amid US Restrictions
{: #carney-faces-calls-to-aid-cuba-amid-us-restrictions}

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney faces growing pressure from opposition parties to send humanitarian aid to Cuba or speak out against the United States, which has tightened restrictions on fuel supplies to the island. NDP interim leader Don Davies urged the government in the House of Commons to "support Cuba in the face of aggressive U.S. imperialism," saying Carney "has remained silent" as President Donald Trump threatens Cuba and the Western Hemisphere. Bloc Québécois leader Yves-François Blanchet said he raised the need for Canada to send humanitarian aid to Cuba during a meeting last week with Carney. Foreign Affairs Minister Anita Anand did not say whether Canada would criticize Washington or offer fuel or humanitarian assistance, saying only that the government is "deeply concerned by the deteriorating situation in Cuba" and that its priority is the safety of Canadians.

### Canada Names Charette as Chief Trade Negotiator to U.S.
{: #canada-names-charette-as-chief-trade-negotiator-to-u-s}

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney on Monday appointed former top civil servant Janice Charette as Canada's chief trade negotiator to the United States, his office said, ahead of a mandatory joint review of the Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement set for a July 1 deadline. Charette, who twice served as clerk of the Privy Council, will act as a senior adviser to Carney and Canada-U.S. Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc on the CUSMA review and the broader trade relationship, the Prime Minister's Office said. The appointment comes a day after Mark Wiseman took over as Canada's ambassador to Washington, replacing Kirsten Hillman, and as President Donald Trump has called CUSMA "irrelevant" and his trade representative has floated breaking the three-way pact into separate deals. Conservative deputy leader Melissa Lantsman said in a statement that Canada does not need "another bureaucrat or negotiator" but rather "results for the thousands of auto, lumber, and steel making jobs lost to the United States."
