*Covering 28 countries across five regions. 27 countries received full analytical treatment this week; 0 were held at maintenance.*### Global Alliance Architecture Under Systematic Stress Test

The Trump administration's systematic coercive diplomacy campaign has triggered the most significant Western alliance stress test since the Iraq War, producing coordinated institutional resistance across European countries while catalysing parallel sovereignty assertions throughout the Americas. European coordination succeeded in forcing suspension of threatened tariffs, demonstrating collective leverage against American pressure.

This represents a fundamental test of alliance architecture limits and reveals that institutional coordination mechanisms remain functional under extreme pressure from the hegemon. The success of collective resistance establishes precedent for allied constraint on American unilateral action, potentially reshaping transatlantic power dynamics and creating template for future coordination against coercive ally behaviour.

*Moderate-high confidence. Strong evidence across three regions with confidence 4 assessments for most countries. Weakest link is Romania's single-source EU-NATO coordination reporting, but pattern holds across 14 other countries with robust confidence.*

*What to watch: American escalation or de-escalation responses, durability of European institutional coordination under sustained pressure, Americas countries' capacity to maintain sovereignty positions without bilateral retaliation, participation of major European allies not yet clearly positioned*### External Pressure Fragmenting Democratic Politics Globally

External pressure from hegemons is producing domestic political fragmentation rather than rally-around-the-flag unification across Western democracies. Seven major democracies simultaneously show acute political instability through different mechanisms—coalition tensions, minority governance, constitutional workarounds, and party system breakdown.

This challenges fundamental assumptions about external threats producing domestic unity and suggests that democratic institutional complexity makes Western allies more vulnerable to foreign pressure rather than more resilient. Internal fragmentation constrains capacity for international co-ordination and bold strategic positioning at precisely the moment when alliance architecture faces systematic stress.

*Moderate-high confidence. Strong country-level assessments (mostly confidence 4) across seven democracies showing similar patterns through different institutional mechanisms. Mexico's confidence 3 assessment is weakest link but pattern holds robustly.*

*What to watch: Electoral outcomes in fragmented systems, capacity for international co-ordination under domestic constraints, potential for external actors to exploit internal divisions, cascade effects to other Western democracies*### Economic Sovereignty Investment Surge Confronts Fiscal Constraints

Asia-Pacific states are mobilising unprecedented economic sovereignty investments totalling hundreds of billions of dollars—Taiwan in semiconductors, Japan in rare earths, South Korea in artificial intelligence systems, Indonesia in infrastructure—while European frontline states face fiscal constraints from defence commitments. Finland has entered European Union deficit procedures partly because of F-35 costs.

This reveals a global tension between security spending ambitions and fiscal sustainability that will constrain middle-power strategic autonomy. Asia-Pacific mobilisation represents a potential model for European responses, while European fiscal constraints preview sustainability limits that Asia-Pacific programmes may encounter, creating system-wide resource allocation pressures.

*Moderate confidence. Based on strong country-level evidence (mostly confidence 4) but linkage assessment limited by different mechanisms and regional contexts. South Korea and Latvia assessments at confidence 3 represent weakest elements.*
