## Top Stories

### Carney announces $3.8B nature protection plan for Canada

WAKEFIELD, Quebec — Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Tuesday a $3.8 billion strategy to protect Canada's natural environment, aiming to conserve 30% of the country's lands and waters by 2030. The plan includes creating two immediate conservation sites: the Wiinipaakw Indigenous Protected Area and National Marine Conservation Area in eastern James Bay off Quebec and the Seal River Watershed National Park Reserve in Manitoba, Carney said during a news conference. The government will also establish up to 14 new marine protected areas and at least 10 new national parks, seeking private sector investment alongside public funding. Conservative opposition spokesperson Sam Lilly criticized the announcement as creating "a whole new bureaucracy to burn taxpayer money while continuing to get the same poor results."

### Carney, Poilievre forge working relationship despite political divide

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney and Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre have developed a working relationship based on "mutual respect" behind the scenes despite their public confrontations in Parliament, according to Poilievre. ## International

### Carney condemns Israel's 'illegal invasion' of Lebanon

WAKEFIELD, Quebec — Prime Minister Mark Carney condemned Israel's military operation in Lebanon as an "illegal invasion" and violation of territorial sovereignty, using stronger language than his government has previously employed. "It's an illegal invasion," Carney said Tuesday at a news conference, calling for a ceasefire as Israeli ground troops move deeper into Lebanese territory. The Centre for Israel and Jewish Affairs said it was "deeply concerned" by Carney's comments, noting that Hezbollah is a "terrorist proxy of the Iranian regime." Hundreds of thousands of Lebanese have been displaced by the escalating conflict.

## Domestic

### Carney rules out proroguing Parliament after upcoming byelections

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney said Tuesday he is "absolutely not" considering proroguing Parliament if his Liberal Party wins a majority following three upcoming byelections. "It has never even entered my thinking, the possibility of [proroguing]," Carney said during a news conference, responding to a Globe and Mail report citing government sources that suggested prorogation was under consideration. The Liberals could form a thin majority government if they win two of three byelections scheduled for April 13 in Toronto ridings University-Rosedale and Scarborough Southwest, and Quebec's Terrebonne riding. Prorogation would end the current parliamentary session and reset the House agenda.

*Sources: (CBC News [1](https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/mark-carney-not-proroguing-parliament-9.7148703))*

### Carney launches Supreme Court judge selection process

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney launched the process to select the next Supreme Court of Canada judge to fill the vacancy created by Justice Sheilah L. Martin's upcoming retirement, his office said Tuesday. Applications from qualified candidates will be accepted until April 27, 2026, according to the Prime Minister's Office. A non-partisan Independent Advisory Board will review applications and submit a shortlist to Carney for consideration, focusing on candidates from Western and Northern Canada. Justice Martin will retire effective May 30, 2026, after serving on the court since 2017.

*Sources: (Office of the Prime Minister [1](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/03/30/prime-minister-carney-launches-process-select-next-judge-supreme))*

### Alberta Premier says energy deal deadlines with Carney missed

EDMONTON, Canada — Alberta Premier Danielle Smith said Tuesday that two April 1 deadlines for agreements connected to her energy deal with Prime Minister Mark Carney will be missed. Smith told reporters that negotiations continue on a carbon pricing system and a memorandum of understanding with the Oil Sands Alliance, both required before a new bitumen pipeline can proceed. The November agreement between Alberta and Ottawa outlined steps needed for a pipeline connecting Alberta to British Columbia tidewater. Carney said Tuesday he felt good about progress on the "complex, very important set of negotiations."
