## Top Stories

### Latvia PM Demands Explanation After Emergency Website Crashes

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Silina said she will demand an explanation from Latvia's State Fire and Rescue Service after the emergency website 112.lv crashed during a drone threat alert Monday evening. The website became inaccessible after authorities issued cell broadcast warnings about a foreign drone detected near the Latvia-Russia border in Ludza and Balvi regions, according to the Interior Ministry's Information Center. Silina thanked emergency services for timely warnings but said she expects clarification about the website's technical capacity. The drone did not enter Latvian airspace and moved away from the border, prompting authorities to send an all-clear message.

## International

### Opposition Questions Latvia PM Over Russia Border Investment Plans

RIGA, Latvia — Opposition lawmakers questioned Prime Minister Evika Siliņa about a 35.7 million euro investment in modernizing three Latvia-Russia border crossings, citing apparent contradiction with the government's pledge to end economic ties with Russia by 2026. United List faction leader Edgars Tavars said the infrastructure projects at Pāternieki, Terehova and Grebņeva crossings undermine Latvia's stated policy of severing trade relations with Russia. The opposition demanded explanations about why public funds continue flowing to border infrastructure improvements while the government publicly commits to ending economic cooperation with Russia.

## Domestic

### Latvia PM calls airBaltic loan short-term solution

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said Wednesday that a 30 million euro government loan to national airline airBaltic is a short-term solution, while acknowledging the carrier may need additional financial support. Speaking on Latvian Television, Siliņa said the government approved the loan to help airBaltic cope with rising fuel costs caused by Middle East conflicts that have disrupted aviation routes. The airline must prepare a new business strategy by June, including reviewing Middle East flight plans, before the government determines if further state support is needed, she said. Parliament will have the final say on the loan, with debate expected ahead of autumn elections.
