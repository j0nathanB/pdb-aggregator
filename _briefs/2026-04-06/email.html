Week of March 30, 2026
THIS WEEK
This week global leaders grappled with mounting domestic pressures and international tensions as fuel costs dominated policy agendas across multiple regions. North America

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announced a $3.8 billion nature protection plan while condemning Israel's military action in Lebanon as an 'illegal invasion,' demonstrating how international conflicts are shaping domestic policy positions. Carney also launched the Supreme Court judge selection process and ruled out proroguing Parliament following upcoming byelections, though tensions persist with Alberta Premier Danielle Smith over missed energy deal deadlines. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum named a new foreign minister after the previous official resigned for health reasons and met with FIFA President Gianni Infantino to discuss 2026 World Cup preparations, while her ruling party senators rejected a UN report on forced disappearances and she accused opposition groups of seeking U.S. intervention in Mexican affairs.

South America

Brazilian President Lula da Silva announced Vice President Geraldo Alckmin as his 2026 running mate in an unusually early campaign declaration, implementing populist measures including expanded paternity leave to 20 days and vowing to cancel a Petrobras gas auction over pricing concerns. Lula escalated criticism of Brazil's legislature, saying senators with eight-year terms 'think they are God' amid reports his government has paid less than 1% of required congressional amendments. Uruguayan President Yamandú Orsi moved to strengthen his international profile by committing to sign press freedom declarations and accepting an invitation to launch a progressive platform in Spain, though Frente Amplio leaders questioned low public support for his government while approving the controversial Solymar housing megaproject despite local concerns.

Europe

French President Emmanuel Macron faced escalating tensions with Trump over personal remarks and policy disagreements, calling Trump's comments about his marriage 'inelegant' while dismissing military action to reopen the Hormuz Strait as 'unrealistic' amid declining approval ratings and Senate pressure on agricultural pesticide legislation. British Prime Minister Keir Starmer issued a 48-hour ultimatum to doctors over pay deals while launching Labour's local election campaign, facing criticism from Trump over military consultation practices and pushback over King Charles' planned U.S. visit timing. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni extended fuel tax cuts and made an unannounced Gulf states visit amid energy security concerns, holding discussions with Starmer about Middle East de-escalation while pressuring the Italian soccer federation chief to resign. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned Ukraine must prepare for possible U.S. aid halt while reporting the best frontline conditions in 10 months, conducting extensive diplomatic outreach with Syria, Turkey, Egypt, Norway and Finland to strengthen security partnerships. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz projected that 80% of Syrian refugees would return home while facing internal CDU discontent, and Finnish President Alexander Stubb positioned Finland as an active mediator, telling Trump that 'more European NATO' is taking shape while urging Iran's Pezeshkian to reopen the Hormuz Strait.

Baltic States

Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda proposed daily fuel price caps to address domestic economic pressures while pursuing diplomatic initiatives with the United States and Ukraine, setting conditions for potential dialogue with Belarus and calling on his government's ruling coalition to decide on the Nemuno Ausra party's participation. Latvian Prime Minister Evika Siliņa faced opposition criticism over government investment plans for Russia border crossings and demanded explanations for emergency website failures during a drone alert, while defending government loans to struggling airBaltic as a short-term measure. Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal spent the week addressing external security concerns, responding to Trump's criticism and defending Estonia's airspace policies amid allegations of facilitating Russian attacks, coordinating with Foreign Minister Tsahkna to reassure the public that the United States would not leave NATO.
