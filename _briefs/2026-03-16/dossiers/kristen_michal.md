### Estonia cancels planned May fuel excise tax increase

TALLINN, Estonia — Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Thursday the government decided to cancel a planned fuel excise tax increase scheduled for May due to disruptions in global fuel markets. Michal said at a government press conference that the proposal came from the finance ministry, which is preparing legislative changes expected to be ready next week. The prime minister said the electricity excise tax is also included in the package, and Estonia will participate in the International Energy Agency's crisis reserve utilization program as a member country.

### Estonian PM Defends Foreign Policy Statements Amid Criticism

TALLINN, Estonia — Prime Minister Kristen Michal defended his recent foreign policy statements amid criticism from lawmakers who said government officials are taking unnecessary risks with diplomatic relations for domestic political reasons. ### Poll shows majority disapproves of Estonian PM's performance

TALLINN, Estonia — A majority of Estonian citizens remain dissatisfied with Prime Minister Kristen Michal's performance, according to a monthly poll conducted by the Institute of Social Research and polling firm Norstat Estonia. The survey found 27 percent of respondents said the government performs very well or rather well, while 66 percent said it performs rather poorly or very poorly. The approval ratings remained largely unchanged from the previous month, when 27 percent approved and 68 percent disapproved of the government's work.

### Estonian PM says defense spending reaches 5.4% of GDP

TALLINN, Estonia — Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Monday that Estonia's defense spending has reached 5.4% of gross domestic product, requiring cuts in other areas of the budget. Michal spoke at a joint press conference with President Alar Karis following a defense council meeting focused on Estonia's defense industry policy. The prime minister said Estonia's defense industry had sales of approximately 730 million euros in 2025, with about 60% of that coming from exports. President Karis also addressed questions about his potential candidacy for a second term and a pending ambassadorial decision.
