### Carney to visit Norway, UK to strengthen defense ties

OTTAWA, Canada — Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney will travel to Norway and the United Kingdom from March 13-15 to strengthen collective defense and security partnerships, his office announced Monday. Carney will observe the NATO Exercise Cold Response in Bardufoss, Norway, where troops from 14 allied nations will train above the Arctic Circle, and meet with Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre to discuss trade, clean energy and critical minerals. He will then travel to London to meet with UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer to discuss collective defense and the Middle East conflict. "Canada is, and forever will be, an Arctic nation," Carney said in a statement.

### Carney announces $35 billion Arctic defense plan

YELLOWKNIFE, Northwest Territories — Prime Minister Mark Carney announced a C$35 billion plan Thursday to boost Canada's Arctic defenses and reduce reliance on the United States for northern security. The plan includes C$32 billion for military infrastructure at forward operating locations in Yellowknife, Inuvik, and Iqaluit, plus C$2.67 billion for four new operational support hubs across the region, Carney said. "We will no longer depend on any one nation, and instead build a stronger, more independent country," Carney said during a speech in Yellowknife. The announcement comes amid strained relations with the U.S. following President Donald Trump's tariffs and comments about annexing Canada.

### Carney moves closer to majority as NDP MP defects

OTTAWA, Canada — Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney moved closer to a parliamentary majority Wednesday when New Democratic Party MP Lori Idlout joined the ruling Liberals, bringing the party to 170 seats in the 343-seat House of Commons. Idlout, who represents the Arctic territory of Nunavut, said in a statement that Canada needs "a strong and ambitious government" to address threats to sovereignty and pressures in the North. The defection marks the fourth opposition legislator to join Carney's Liberals since November and leaves the party just two seats short of a majority. Special elections scheduled for April 13 in three vacant Liberal-held constituencies could give Carney control of the chamber if his party wins at least two seats.
