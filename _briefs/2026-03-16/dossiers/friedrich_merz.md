## Top Stories

### Merz says state election won't impact Berlin coalition

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Monday that his party's narrow defeat in Baden-Wuerttemberg's state election would not affect his governing coalition in Berlin. The Greens edged out Merz's Christian Democrats with 30.2% of the vote compared to 29.7%, powered by candidate Cem Ozdemir's comeback campaign. Merz called the result "bitter" but said he had spoken with Social Democrat coalition partners and promised to accelerate economic reforms. The chancellor again ruled out cooperation with the far-right Alternative for Germany, which finished third with 18.8% of the vote.

### Merz criticizes US easing of Russia oil sanctions

ANDØYA, Norway — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Friday sharply criticized the United States for temporarily easing sanctions on Russian oil purchases, calling the move wrong amid rising energy prices from the Iran war. "We believe that is wrong," Merz said at a press conference with Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, adding there is currently a price problem but not a supply problem. The U.S. Treasury issued a 30-day waiver allowing countries to buy sanctioned Russian oil stranded at sea, which Merz said received opposition from six of the seven G-7 members during a Wednesday conference call with President Donald Trump.

### Merz visits Norway space defense launch site

ANDENES, Norway — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz visited a Norwegian island that is becoming Europe's space launch facility, according to Süddeutsche Zeitung. Merz joined Defense Minister Boris Pistorius, Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, and Research Minister Dorothee Bär at the Andøya site. The remote fishing village of 2,500 residents is being transformed into what officials described as a strategic space defense hub for European sovereignty.
