## Top Stories

### Latvia PM Siliņa delivers annual report to parliament

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa delivered her annual report to parliament Thursday, outlining government achievements and future plans amid challenging circumstances. Siliņa said the government has worked through difficult conditions including frequent elections, wars in Ukraine and the Middle East, and a changing economic situation. She highlighted that Latvia's defense spending will reach nearly five percent of GDP this year, totaling more than two billion euros, which she called the largest defense funding in the country's history. The prime minister also cited economic growth of 2.1 percent last year and reforms in security, economics, education and healthcare sectors.

### Latvia PM Orders Two Probes Into Rail Baltica Project

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said she will propose two official investigations into the Rail Baltica railway project at Tuesday's cabinet meeting. One probe led by the Finance Ministry will examine a procurement process by RB Rail that excluded a bidder offering a 270 million euro lower price, Siliņa said in a social media post. A second investigation under the Economy Ministry will review unfinished bridge supports over the Daugava River and a railway viaduct in Marupe that are being mothballed until after 2030. The Baltic rail project's first phase costs have ballooned to 14.3 billion euros from an initial 5.8 billion euro estimate in 2017.

## International

### Latvia PM Orders Plan to Cut Economic Ties with Russia

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa will order several ministries to develop a detailed plan by the end of March to sever economic ties with Russia by the end of 2026, her New Unity party said. The decision follows the European Union's failure to approve a new sanctions package due to vetoes from some member states, according to the party statement. The plan will include ending public transport connections, banning Russian-origin goods in retail, and halting railway cargo flows to Russia. Latvian businesses must cease imports and exports with Russia under the national-level measures.

### Latvia PM discusses NATO involvement in Middle East conflict

RIGA, Latvia — Latvian Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said NATO is conducting preliminary consultations about potential involvement in Middle East conflicts but has not reached the level of Article 4 consultations. Speaking on TV24's "Week. Post scriptum" program, Siliņa said NATO members including the United States must be prepared to defend their populations. She said NATO is protecting critical infrastructure in the Baltic Sea and land borders, despite criticism that the government is not taking action.
