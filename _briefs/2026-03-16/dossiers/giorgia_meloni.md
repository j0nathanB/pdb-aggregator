### Meloni criticizes US-Israeli strikes on Iran as dangerous trend

ROME, Italy — Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni on Wednesday criticized U.S.-Israeli military strikes against Iran as part of a "dangerous trend" of interventions "outside the scope of international law," according to remarks she made to parliament. Meloni said Iran must not be allowed to develop nuclear weapons, warning this would have "dramatic repercussions for global security." ### Protesters burn Meloni photos at Rome referendum rally

ROME, Italy — Protesters burned images of Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni and Justice Minister Carlo Nordio during a demonstration against a constitutional referendum on judicial reforms, according to Italian media reports. The march organized by the leftist group Potere al Popolo drew about 20,000 people to central Rome, organizers said. Political leaders across party lines condemned the burning of the officials' photos, with Chamber President Lorenzo Fontana calling for the referendum debate to focus on issues rather than "acts that risk poisoning the climate." The National Association of Magistrates also expressed solidarity with Meloni and Nordio while calling for lowered tensions during the campaign.

### Meloni pushes judicial lottery reform ahead of referendum

ROME, Italy — Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni is campaigning for a judicial reform that would use lottery selection to choose magistrate members of Italy's judicial governing councils, ahead of a March 22-23 referendum. The reform would separate career paths of judges and prosecutors and split the judiciary's self-governing body into two councils, Meloni said at a Milan rally Thursday. Recent polls show the opposition "no" camp leading 53-47 percent, according to ANSA. Meloni said the changes would "fix what's broken" in Italy's justice system, while opponents argue the reforms could weaken judicial independence.

### Italian PM Meloni irritated as aide refuses apology over judiciary remarks

ROME, Italy — Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni expressed irritation after her chief of staff Giusi Bartolozzi refused to apologize for calling parts of Italy's judiciary a "firing squad," according to sources at Palazzo Chigi. Bartolozzi, a magistrate for 27 years, issued only a statement of "deep regret" through the ANSA news agency, saying her words were taken from a broader speech and misinterpreted. Justice Minister Carlo Nordio had announced Bartolozzi would apologize, but she maintained she had nothing to apologize for. The controversy emerged as Meloni campaigns for judicial reform referendum support.

## International

### Meloni says Italy won't join Trump's Gaza peace board

ROME, Italy — Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said Wednesday that Italy will not join U.S. President Donald Trump's Board of Peace for Gaza but will contribute to discussions about the initiative. "We have decided not to join the Board of Peace, but I don't see why we shouldn't contribute to this discussion because I care about Gaza," Meloni told lawmakers in the Lower House during remarks on the Middle East crisis. She added that she does not understand those who exclude themselves from such important discussions.

### Russian dissidents urge Meloni to exclude Russia from Venice Biennale

VENICE, Italy — Russian dissidents from the Platform of Russian Democratic Forces at the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe have directly appealed to Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni to exclude Russia from the Venice Biennale art exhibition. The group said Russia's inclusion creates "a dangerous precedent" that serves "Russian state propaganda," according to Corriere della Sera. The appeal follows previous requests from Ukrainian foreign and culture ministers and a petition with over 1,000 signatures. Biennale President Pietrangelo Buttafuoco said "my Biennale will be the true truce," while the foundation's statutes prevent it from refusing Russia's participation as the country owns a pavilion.

### Italy's Meloni says mobile fuel excise mechanism awaits conditions

ROME, Italy — Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said Italy's mobile excise duty mechanism to reduce fuel prices requires all economic conditions to be met before activation, according to statements made in the Senate. The mechanism, designed to cut excise duties when oil price increases become structural, depends on specific revenue conditions including VAT receipts, Under-Secretary Lucia Albano told lawmakers in response to questions from the 5 Star Movement. The government is monitoring fuel price developments following the Middle East crisis, Albano said.
