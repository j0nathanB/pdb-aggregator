THE MIDDLE POWERS MONITOR
Week of February 16, 2026
THIS WEEK
This week global leaders confronted institutional challenges while pursuing major international agreements, with Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum calling for calm after cartel violence and Brazilian President Lula da Silva vetoing congressional salary increases while criticizing Supreme Court actions. French President Emmanuel Macron sought a significant defense deal with India and requested sanctions relief from President-elect Trump, as Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney strengthened his parliamentary position through Conservative defections. The week highlighted tensions between executive authority and institutional oversight, with leaders from Mexico to Italy asserting federal power over courts and regional governments while managing complex diplomatic relationships.

North America

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney consolidated power as Conservative MP Matt Jeneroux crossed the floor to join his Liberal government, while launching Canada's first Defence Industrial Strategy and directing increased patriotic messaging in government advertising. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum proposed constitutional reforms to cap executive pensions and called for calm following cartel leader violence that sparked road blockades across multiple states, while declining Trump's invitation to join a Gaza peace initiative and advancing electoral reforms allowing political parties to initiate popular consultations.

South America

Brazilian President Lula da Silva vetoed congressional salary increases while publicly criticizing a Supreme Court justice's actions as "high treason," defending a carnival tribute honoring him and using an AI summit in India to criticize big tech business models while seeking equal treatment from the incoming Trump administration. Uruguayan President Yamandú Orsi navigated his first week balancing ceremonial duties with opposition calls for investigations into naval and water project contracts, signing agricultural research agreements with China while considering humanitarian aid to Cuba and ranking 12th in a Latin America leadership poll.

Europe

French President Emmanuel Macron pursued a major Rafale fighter jet deal with India while requesting President-elect Trump lift sanctions on European officials, facing diplomatic tensions with Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni over comments regarding a French activist's death. British Prime Minister Keir Starmer faced Trump's criticism over the Chagos Islands agreement while announcing a £4 billion special needs education overhaul and pledging social media restrictions. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz faced business warnings about China trade risks ahead of his planned trip while questioning the German-French FCAS fighter jet project, as Meloni criticized Italian courts over migrant compensation rulings while raising corporate taxes on energy companies. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy announced new trilateral talks with the US and Russia scheduled within 10 days while rejecting any troop withdrawal from Donbas and sanctioning 225 Russian shadow fleet captains.

Baltic States

Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda was hospitalized following a domestic injury while maintaining his diplomatic agenda, announcing continued Belarus sanctions for 12 months and calling military mobility essential to European security. Latvian Prime Minister Evika Siliņa allocated 10 million euros for Ukraine weapons purchases while adding a military cooperation advisor to her office and proposing to rebrand the Ukraine support coalition as a "coalition of strength." Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal faced majority disapproval in new polling while calling for an aggressive future-oriented national strategy and announcing wind farm incentives for municipalities, amid reports that President Alar Karis advised him to replace his foreign minister.
