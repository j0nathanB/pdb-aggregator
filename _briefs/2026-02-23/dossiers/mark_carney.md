## Top Stories

### Conservative MP Jeneroux crosses floor to join Carney's Liberals

EDMONTON, Canada — Conservative MP Matt Jeneroux crossed the floor to join Prime Minister Mark Carney's Liberal Party on Wednesday, citing a "national unity crisis" and bringing the government within two seats of a majority. Jeneroux, who represents Edmonton Riverbend, said Carney's speech at the World Economic Forum in Davos influenced his decision to leave the opposition. The defection marks the third Conservative to join the Liberals since November, following Nova Scotia MP Chris d'Entremont and Toronto-area MP Michael Ma. Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre said Jeneroux "betrayed the people of Edmonton Riverbend" and accused Carney of pursuing "dirty backroom deals" to secure a majority.

### Carney shifts advertising budget toward patriotic Canadian messaging

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney's government has reallocated federal advertising spending to emphasize patriotic and economic messages, including a $3 million "Choose Canada" campaign, according to Treasury Board Secretariat disclosures. The third-quarter spending shifted resources from programs for seniors and youth to campaigns promoting Canadian economic development and national identity. The Privy Council Office said the government is responding to "increased uncertainty in a more dangerous and divided world" by ensuring communications reach Canadians with information about government priorities. Carney also discussed establishing a Defence, Security and Resilience Bank with UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer to provide financing for defense investments.

### Carney launches Canada's first Defence Industrial Strategy

MONTREAL, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney launched Canada's first Defence Industrial Strategy on Tuesday, a plan to prioritize Canadian suppliers and reduce reliance on foreign defense contractors. The strategy aims to award 70 percent of federal defense contracts to Canadian firms within a decade, up from the current 43 percent, according to senior defense officials. The plan anticipates creating 125,000 jobs and generating $125 billion in economic benefits by 2035, Carney said. The announcement comes days after U.S. President Donald Trump signed an executive order promoting American arms sales to allies, potentially creating competing priorities for defense procurement.

### Canada names Charette as chief trade negotiator to US

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney appointed Janice Charette as Canada's chief trade negotiator to the United States, the Prime Minister's Office said Monday. Charette, who twice served as Clerk of the Privy Council, will advise Carney and Canada-U.S. Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc on the upcoming Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement review scheduled for July 2026. The appointment comes as the federal government prepares for potential changes to the North American trade pact, with U.S. President Donald Trump recently calling the agreement "irrelevant." Charette replaces Kirsten Hillman, who stepped down as both chief trade negotiator and ambassador to allow for a fresh negotiating team.

### Conservative MPs defect to Carney's Liberals, nearing majority

OTTAWA, Canada — Three Conservative MPs have crossed the floor to join Prime Minister Mark Carney's Liberal government in recent months, with Matt Jeneroux becoming the latest defection Wednesday, CBC News reported.
