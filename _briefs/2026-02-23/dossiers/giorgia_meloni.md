### Meloni attacks Italian courts over migrant compensation rulings

ROME, Italy — Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni accused Italian courts of undermining her government's efforts to curb illegal migration after a Palermo court ordered the government to pay 76,000 euros in compensation to charity Sea-Watch for unlawfully detaining its ship in 2019. "What message is being sent with this long series of objectively absurd decisions — that the government is not allowed to try to combat mass illegal immigration?" Meloni said in a video message Wednesday. The criticism comes ahead of a March 22-23 referendum on justice system reforms that polls show could go either way.

### Italy raises corporate tax on energy companies to fund bill cuts

ROME, Italy — Italy approved a two-percentage-point increase in its IRAP corporate tax on energy firms to help fund measures aimed at cutting bills for families and energy-intensive businesses, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said Wednesday. The move is expected to generate around 1 billion euros in extra revenue through 2028, according to a government decree. "The package will have a significant impact, guaranteeing benefits for households and businesses worth over 5 billion euros," Meloni said in a video posted on social media platform X. Under the scheme, the IRAP tax rate will increase from 3.90% to 5.90% for all companies that produce, distribute, and supply energy products.

*Sources: (Reuters [1](https://www.reuters.com/business/italy-hikes-irap-corporate-tax-energy-firms-pm-meloni-says-2026-02-18/))*

### Meloni backs Mattarella remarks on judicial council referendum

ROME, Italy — Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said she supports President Sergio Mattarella's recent comments about keeping Italy's judicial council separate from political disputes ahead of a constitutional referendum on justice reform. Meloni told SkyTg24 that the March referendum should focus on the reform's merits rather than becoming a political battle, stating "at the referendum, people are not voting on me." She said the justice reform is not ideologically driven and has been proposed by various political parties over the years. Meloni also responded to French President Emmanuel Macron's criticism of her comments about a recent death in Lyon, saying she was expressing solidarity with the French people rather than interfering in French affairs.
