## Top Stories

### Polish Opposition Party Seeks to Block EU Defense Fund

WARSAW, Poland — Poland's opposition Law and Justice party is working to block a European Union defense fund that would bring billions of euros to Poland, according to Polish media reports. The party opposes the SAFE program because it requires 65 percent of defense equipment components to come from EU countries, limiting purchases from the United States and South Korea, Rzeczpospolita reported. Prime Minister Donald Tusk criticized President Karol Nawrocki on social media, saying he prepares vetoes "just to cause harm" and "is not a president of Polish affairs." The SAFE program represents a major success for Tusk's administration, with Poland set to receive the largest share of the 150 billion euro fund.

### Polish PM Tusk urges citizens to leave Iran immediately

WARSAW, Poland — Prime Minister Donald Tusk urged Polish citizens to immediately leave Iran, warning that evacuation may become impossible within hours due to the possibility of armed conflict. "Please immediately leave Iran and under no circumstances travel to that country," Tusk said, adding that "the possibility of hot conflict is very real." Foreign Ministry spokesman Maciej Wewiór echoed the warning, citing possible airspace disruptions in the Middle East region. The appeal comes amid reports that U.S. forces could be ready to strike Iran as early as Saturday, according to CBS News.

## International

### Polish PM Tusk criticizes Hungary's block of Ukraine aid

WARSAW, Poland — Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk criticized Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orban's decision to block a 90 billion euro European Union loan to Ukraine, linking it to domestic Polish politics. Tusk said Saturday on social media platform X that "Premier Orban blocked European aid for Ukraine" while "Kaczynski tries to block SAFE," referring to Polish defense funding and the country's arms industry. Hungary announced Friday it would block the EU loan package until Ukraine resumes oil transit through the Druzhba pipeline, which has been suspended since a Jan. 27 Russian attack on energy infrastructure.

## Domestic

### Polish PM Tusk faces declining approval in new poll

WARSAW, Poland — Prime Minister Donald Tusk's government faces declining public support, with 33.2% of Poles saying their living standards have worsened since the 2023 elections, according to a UCE Research poll for Onet. The survey of 1,021 residents in cities over 50,000 people found 28.2% reported improved conditions while 32.2% saw no change. Political scientist Bartosz Rydliński said Tusk's government is losing support faster than the previous administration, citing what he called the "desertification of provincial Poland" due to poor healthcare access and declining public transportation in smaller communities.

### Polish PM Tusk to Release Report on President's Crypto Ties

WARSAW, Poland — Prime Minister Donald Tusk plans to publicly release intelligence materials alleging ties between President Karol Nawrocki and cryptocurrency companies, according to Polish newspaper Gazeta Wyborcza. The government is declassifying materials collected by intelligence services and preparing a special report for public presentation, the newspaper reported. The investigation focuses on alleged connections between Nawrocki's circle and Zondacrypto, a leading Polish cryptocurrency exchange, following Nawrocki's February veto of cryptocurrency market regulation legislation. Tusk's advisers told the newspaper that evidence should be disclosed quickly, with the probe also examining other right-wing politicians including members of the Law and Justice party and Confederation.
