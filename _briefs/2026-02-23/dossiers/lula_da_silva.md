### Brazil's Lula defends carnival tribute after samba school relegated

NEW DELHI, India — Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva said Sunday that a carnival tribute to him by the Acadêmicos de Niterói samba school was "extraordinary," despite the school being relegated from Rio's top carnival division. Lula said he only accepted the school's invitation to be honored and did not participate in creative decisions for the parade, which featured his life story and criticized former President Jair Bolsonaro. The president, speaking during a visit to India, said the tribute also honored his late mother and called the parade's music "wonderful." The school will drop from the Special Group division in 2027 following its performance.

### Lula criticizes big tech business models at India AI summit

NEW DELHI, India — Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva criticized big tech companies' business models during an artificial intelligence summit Thursday, saying they depend on exploiting personal data and amplifying political radicalization. ### Lula seeks equal treatment from Trump in bilateral relations

### Lula vetoes salary increases exceeding constitutional ceiling for Congress

BRASÍLIA, Brazil — President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva partially approved salary adjustments for congressional and federal audit court staff while vetoing provisions that could have raised salaries to 80,000 reais ($13,300), the presidential palace said Tuesday. Lula approved 2026 salary increases but vetoed gradual raises through 2029, retroactive payments and compensatory leave that could be converted to cash exceeding the constitutional salary ceiling of 46,366 reais, officials said. The vetoed provisions violated fiscal responsibility laws prohibiting mandatory spending commitments in a government's final eight months that cannot be fully paid during the current term, according to the palace.

### Lula calls Supreme Court justice's actions 'high treason'

BRASILIA, Brazil — President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has privately described Supreme Court Justice Dias Toffoli's handling of the Master case as 'high treason,' according to allies who spoke on condition of anonymity. The comments have intensified tensions between Brazil's executive and judicial branches ahead of the 2026 election year. Allies said Lula's frustration stems not only from Toffoli's technical decisions in the case but also his prolonged role as the case's rapporteur. The president appointed Toffoli to the Supreme Court in 2009, but their relationship has been marked by periods of closeness and distance.
