## Top Stories

### German business groups warn of China trade risks before Merz trip

BERLIN, Germany — German business organizations warned of economic threats from China days before Chancellor Friedrich Merz's planned trip to the country, according to a new policy paper. The Asia-Pacific Committee of German business said China's industrial overcapacity, massive subsidies and distorted exchange rate policies "undermine fair competition and destabilize global markets," Reuters reported. The group said China remains a central economic and innovation partner with significant opportunities for German firms, but added that competition, systemic risks and market distortions have become "a central challenge for prosperity and security in Europe." The business representatives called for coordinated European "de-risking" measures and potentially "robust defensive measures" where economic and national security risks increase.

### Merz Seeks Party Unity at CDU Convention

STUTTGART, Germany — Chancellor Friedrich Merz addressed the Christian Democratic Union party convention Friday, acknowledging criticism of his government's performance while seeking support from party delegates ahead of his re-election as party leader. Merz said he recognized that his administration had not achieved all promised reforms and called for patience with the coalition government's work with the Social Democrats. The chancellor received more than 10 minutes of applause and was re-elected with 91 percent support from the 1,001 delegates, according to party officials.

### Merz questions German-French fighter jet project FCAS

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz publicly questioned the Future Combat Air System (FCAS), a joint fighter jet project with France, citing incompatible military requirements. "The French need an aircraft in the next generation of fighter jets that can carry nuclear weapons and operate from an aircraft carrier. That's not what we currently need in the German army," Merz said in the German podcast "Machtwechsel." Defense Minister Boris Pistorius told journalists Wednesday he shares Merz's skepticism about the joint fighter jet project. The comments sparked concern in French media about Germany potentially abandoning the European defense program.

*Sources: (Süddeutsche Zeitung [1](https://www.sueddeutsche.de/wirtschaft/fcas-programm-kampfjet-herausforderungen-deutschland-frankreich-li.3389059))*

## International

### Merz seeks EU response to Trump tariff ruling

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he will work toward a coordinated European Union position following a U.S. Supreme Court ruling on President Donald Trump's tariff policies, according to German media reports. The court blocked Trump from imposing tariffs under emergency powers Friday, but Trump responded by announcing new global tariffs of 10 percent, later raised to 15 percent Saturday. Merz said Germany will have "a very clear European stance" on the issue and plans to travel to Washington on March 2. Officials in Brussels and Berlin expressed uncertainty about the ruling's implications for planned EU-U.S. trade agreements.

*Sources: (Handelsblatt [1](https://www.handelsblatt.com/politik/deutschland/handelspolitik-zittern-vor-dem-zollchaos-europa-ringt-um-eine-reaktion/100202338.html))*

## Domestic

### Germany's Merz vows to block far-right AfD party

STUTTGART, Germany — Chancellor Friedrich Merz vowed Friday not to let the far-right Alternative for Germany party "ruin" the country as he addressed his Christian Democrat party's conference. Merz told delegates his party would block the AfD, which aims to win its first state election this year, according to his remarks at the Stuttgart gathering. The chancellor, trailing in polls ahead of upcoming state elections, was re-elected as party chairman with 91% of the vote. Merz also warned fellow conservatives to prepare for a "raw new climate of great-power competition," saying the "rules based order we knew no longer exists."
