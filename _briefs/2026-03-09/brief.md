# Week of March 2, 2026

> Global leaders this week navigated escalating Middle East tensions following Iranian attacks, with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney convening emergency response groups and French President Emmanuel Macron announcing plans to increase nuclear warheads while deploying the aircraft carrier to the Mediterranean. Domestic political pressures mounted as Carney called three federal by-elections for April 13 and Uruguay's President Yamandú Orsi faced a 41% disapproval rating in his first year. Meanwhile, Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum called for U.S. arms controls to combat cartel violence as President-elect Trump's return loomed over bilateral relations. The week highlighted the intersection of regional security crises with domestic political challenges across multiple democracies.

### Carney calls three federal by-elections for April 13
{: #carney-calls-three-federal-by-elections-for-april-13}

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Sunday that by-elections will be held April 13 in three electoral districts, according to a news release from his office. The races in Toronto ridings of University-Rosedale and Scarborough Southwest and Quebec's Terrebonne district could give Carney's Liberals a majority government if they win all three seats. The Liberals currently hold 169 seats in the House of Commons and need 172 for a majority. The Terrebonne by-election follows a Supreme Court ruling that nullified the 2025 federal election result in the Montreal-area riding after a mail-in ballot dispute.

### Italy Considers Mobile Fuel Tax as Latvia Monitors Rising Prices *(Giorgia Meloni, Evika Siliņa)*
{: #italy-considers-mobile-fuel-tax-as-latvia-monitors-rising-prices}

ROME, Italy — Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said her government is evaluating activating a mobile fuel tax mechanism to counter rising gasoline prices, confirming earlier statements by Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti. The mechanism would use increased VAT revenue from higher fuel prices to reduce excise taxes, Meloni said. Meanwhile, Latvian Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said fuel prices have risen sharply due to military tensions in Iran but stressed the country has adequate reserves and supply is not at risk. Siliņa said Latvia has purchased required 15% fuel reserves and noted prices remain below January 2023 levels.
