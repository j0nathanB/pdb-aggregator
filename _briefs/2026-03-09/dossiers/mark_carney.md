### Carney calls three federal by-elections for April 13

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Sunday that by-elections will be held April 13 in three electoral districts, according to a news release from his office. The races in Toronto ridings of University-Rosedale and Scarborough Southwest and Quebec's Terrebonne district could give Carney's Liberals a majority government if they win all three seats. The Liberals currently hold 169 seats in the House of Commons and need 172 for a majority. The Terrebonne by-election follows a Supreme Court ruling that nullified the 2025 federal election result in the Montreal-area riding after a mail-in ballot dispute.

### Carney calls Iran conflict failure of international order

SYDNEY, Australia — Prime Minister Mark Carney said Wednesday that Canada supports U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran "with regret," calling the conflict a "failure of the international order." Carney said the strikes "appear to be inconsistent with international law" because the U.S. and Israel "acted without engaging the United Nations or consulting with allies, including Canada." The prime minister said Canada was not informed of the attacks in advance but supports them in a "limited sense" due to Iran's nuclear threat and "export of terrorism." Carney did not rule out future Canadian military participation, saying "we will defend our allies when it makes sense."

### Carney elevates Australia partnership in minerals, defense, AI

CANBERRA, Australia — Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney signed new agreements with Australia on critical minerals, defense cooperation and artificial intelligence during his first official visit to the country in nearly two decades, officials said Thursday. Carney addressed Australia's parliament, telling lawmakers that "middle powers have a choice: compete for favour or combine for strength," according to his office. Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said Australia would join Canada's G7 critical minerals production alliance, aimed at diversifying supply chains away from China's dominance in processing essential materials for semiconductors and defense applications. The leaders also announced enhanced defense cooperation, including training of Canadian Armed Forces personnel on Australia's Arctic Over-the-Horizon Radar system beginning in mid-2026.
