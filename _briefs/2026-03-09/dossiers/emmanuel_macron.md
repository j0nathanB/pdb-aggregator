### Macron announces France will increase nuclear warheads, deploy aircraft to allies

L'ILE LONGUE, France — French President Emmanuel Macron announced Monday that France will increase its nuclear warheads and allow temporary deployment of nuclear-armed aircraft to allied countries for the first time. Macron said eight European countries including Germany, Poland, Britain and the Netherlands have agreed to participate in what he called "forward deterrence," though France will retain sole decision-making authority over nuclear weapons use. The French president said the new strategy aims to strengthen Europe's security independence amid growing doubts about U.S. commitments to defend Europe. Macron spoke at the L'Ile Longue naval base, home to France's ballistic missile submarines.

### Macron reassures opposition on nuclear deterrence sovereignty

BREST, France — President Emmanuel Macron said Monday there would be no sharing of France's nuclear decision-making authority, reassuring opposition politicians who had criticized his planned speech on nuclear doctrine. Speaking at the Ile-Longue naval base, Macron stated there would be "no sharing of the ultimate decision" to use nuclear weapons and "no sharing of the definition of vital interests," according to Les Echos. Opposition leaders from both the Republicans party and the far-right National Rally had expressed concerns over the weekend about potential changes to France's nuclear sovereignty.
