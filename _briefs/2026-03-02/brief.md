# Week of February 23, 2026

> European and Western leaders marked Ukraine's war anniversary with $2 billion in new aid commitments while responding to escalating Middle East tensions following U.S.-Israeli strikes that killed Iran's Khamenei, prompting emergency diplomatic consultations. French President Emmanuel Macron called for an emergency UN Security Council meeting and updated France's nuclear doctrine, while Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney backed the Iran strikes and announced military aid for Ukraine. Latin American leaders faced domestic political pressures, with Brazilian President Lula da Silva polling in a statistical tie with Flavio Bolsonaro for a potential runoff and Uruguayan President Yamandú Orsi's approval rating dropping to 23% amid labor strikes and classified information leak scandals. The week highlighted shifting global alliances as leaders navigated simultaneous crises in Eastern Europe and the Middle East while managing domestic political challenges.

### European Leaders Visit Kyiv to Mark War Anniversary *(Alexander Stubb, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Evika Siliņa, Gitanas Nausėda)*
{: #european-leaders-visit-kyiv-to-mark-war-anniversary}

KYIV, Ukraine — More than a dozen European leaders gathered in Ukraine's capital Tuesday to mark the fourth anniversary of Russia's full-scale invasion, with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy addressing the milestone from a wartime bunker. Nordic and Baltic leaders, including Finnish President Alexander Stubb, Latvian Prime Minister Evika Siliņa, and Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda, announced nearly 12.5 billion euros in planned military contributions for 2026, according to a joint statement. Stubb called Russia's invasion a "strategic failure for Putin and Russia" during meetings with Zelenskyy, while Nausėda praised Ukraine's resistance in a video message. Zelenskyy invited U.S. President Donald Trump to visit Ukraine and honored volunteers and energy workers with state awards on the anniversary.

{: #world-leaders-react-to-u-s-israeli-strikes-that-killed-iran-s-khamenei}

PARIS, France — French President Emmanuel Macron called for an urgent U.N. Security Council meeting Saturday following U.S.-Israeli strikes that killed Iran's Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, saying France was not warned of the attacks. British Prime Minister Keir Starmer said Sunday his government agreed to let the U.S. use British bases for defensive strikes against Iranian missile sites after Iran launched retaliatory attacks. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called on U.S. and European partners to plan for Iran's future, while Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said his country supports the strikes aimed at preventing Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons. All four leaders emphasized their nations were not involved in the initial offensive operations against Iran.

### Poll shows Lula, Flavio Bolsonaro tied in Brazil runoff
{: #poll-shows-lula-flavio-bolsonaro-tied-in-brazil-runoff}

SAO PAULO, Brazil — Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and Senator Flavio Bolsonaro are virtually tied in a potential runoff for October's election, with 46.2% and 46.3% respectively, according to an AtlasIntel poll released Wednesday. The poll showed Lula's advantage has disappeared after leading Bolsonaro 49.2% to 44.9% in January and holding a 12-point edge in December. Former Workers' Party president Jose Genoino said the results indicate the need to reorganize the government's political strategy. AtlasIntel surveyed 4,986 people between February 19-24 with a margin of error of 1 percentage point.
