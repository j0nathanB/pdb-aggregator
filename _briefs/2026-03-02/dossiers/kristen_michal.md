## Top Stories

### Estonia announces 11 million euros in military aid for Ukraine

KYIV, Ukraine — Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Tuesday his country will provide 11 million euros in military aid to Ukraine for air defense weapons and ammunition through NATO's PURL initiative. Michal made the announcement during a joint visit by Nordic-Baltic leaders to Kyiv marking the fourth anniversary of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy awarded Michal the Order of Prince Yaroslav the Wise, Second Class, for Estonia's support. The Nordic-Baltic countries have collectively provided over 42 billion euros in aid to Ukraine, making them the world's second-largest supporter after the United States, Michal said.

### Estonian PM Blames Officials for Bus Fare Proposal

TALLINN, Estonia — Prime Minister Kristen Michal said officials, not politicians, were responsible for proposing that children and pensioners pay higher bus fares, distancing himself from the controversial idea. Michal said the government had not discussed the transportation reform proposal, which came from the Regional Affairs and Agriculture Ministry led by Minister Hendrik Johannes Terras. "When politicians go to elections and bear that responsibility, then it's politicians' job to decide these things," Michal said in a radio interview, adding that he had already stated publicly that fare increases for children and pensioners would not happen.

### Estonian PM delivers Independence Day speech warning against populism

TARTU, Estonia — Prime Minister Kristen Michal warned against populist influences and defended Estonia's Western orientation in his Independence Day speech Tuesday, saying the country must remain "aggressively future-oriented" amid global uncertainty. Michal said Estonia faces a "time of intensifying value conflicts" and rejected calls to distance the country from Ukraine or seek accommodation with Russia. The speech came as Michal visited Kyiv alongside other Nordic and Baltic leaders, where Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy recorded a video message congratulating Estonia on its 108th independence anniversary. Estonia pledged 11 million euros for Ukraine's air defense during the visit.

### Estonian PM calls for EU support for eastern border regions

BRUSSELS, Belgium — Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Thursday that European Union member states along the eastern border carry responsibilities beyond their own territories as he addressed a high-level conference on border regions. Michal said Estonia spends more than 5 percent of its GDP on defense despite economic challenges including recession and inflation that peaked near 20 percent. He called for additional EU funding and higher co-financing rates for eastern border regions, stating that common funding must reinforce defense posture and support for Ukraine must remain long-term and predictable. Michal said living and investing in EU eastern border countries should be an attractive choice rather than a structural disadvantage.

### Estonian PM discusses defense spending, coalition stability

TALLINN, Estonia — Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Estonia will maintain defense spending at five percent of GDP while suggesting future tax increases could be reconsidered if economic conditions improve. In an interview with Postimees, Michal said his government aims to balance defense priorities with other spending needs, including teacher salary increases. The prime minister also addressed coalition stability, stating he intends to keep the current government together until parliamentary elections in March 2027. Michal warned against Russian efforts to create polarization in Estonian society while emphasizing that citizens don't need to love the government but should love their country.
