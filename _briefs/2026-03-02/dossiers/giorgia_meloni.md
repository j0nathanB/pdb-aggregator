### Italy's Meloni proposes electoral law changes ahead of 2027 vote

ROME, Italy — Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's ruling coalition struck a deal to reform Italy's electoral system, proposing a fully proportional system with bonus seats designed to give stable majorities to coalitions winning more than 40% of votes. The reform would eliminate single-member districts and award 70 bonus seats in the lower house and 35 in the Senate to winning coalitions, according to a coalition statement. Opposition Democratic Party leader Elly Schlein called the move a "strong distortion of representation," while Meloni's Brothers of Italy party said it aims to ensure governmental stability after the 2027 election. Studies suggest the proposed system could help Meloni's center-right coalition secure a comfortable parliamentary majority even with a narrow polling lead.

### Meloni says government role in Monte dei Paschi ended

### Meloni calls energy costs absolute priority for EU

### Italy rejects equal parental leave proposal over costs

ROME, Italy — Italy's Budget Committee rejected an opposition proposal for equal parental leave between mothers and fathers after the State Accounting Office said the measure lacked adequate funding. The center-left proposal would have equalized leave months for both parents and increased coverage from 80% to 100% of salary, but officials estimated costs at 3.7 billion euros in 2026, rising to 4.5 billion euros annually by 2035. Democratic Party leader Elly Schlein called on Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, Italy's first female premier, to reconsider the measure. The accounting office said the proposal "cannot be verified positively" due to insufficient financial coverage.
