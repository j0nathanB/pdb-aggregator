### German Chancellor Merz seeks China trade reset amid deficit concerns

BEIJING, China — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called for strengthened partnership with China during his first visit to Beijing as chancellor, seeking to address a widening trade deficit that reached 90 billion euros last year. Merz told Chinese President Xi Jinping he wanted to deepen the "comprehensive strategic partnership" with Germany's largest trading partner, according to a joint statement. The chancellor said the trade deficit had increased fourfold since 2020 and called the dynamic "not healthy," while announcing China had ordered 120 aircraft from European manufacturer Airbus. Merz was accompanied by executives from 30 German companies including Volkswagen and BMW as European leaders seek to maintain ties with China amid tensions with the Trump administration.

### Merz calls for Iran 'day after' planning following strikes

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called on U.S. and European partners to start planning for Iran's future after U.S. and Israeli strikes killed the country's supreme leader, Reuters reported. Merz said his government agreed with U.S. goals to end Tehran's nuclear program but warned of escalation risks from Iran's potential counterstrikes. The chancellor outlined four aims including ensuring regional stability, ending Iran's nuclear and missile programs, and helping Iranians decide their own fate, according to the report. Merz also said Germany would not tolerate attacks on U.S. or Israeli institutions in the country.

### Merz Government Faces Criticism Over Labor Reform Package

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz's coalition government is drawing criticism for its latest labor reform initiatives, according to a commentary in the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung. The newspaper's Berlin correspondent Dietrich Creutzburg said the government's new collective bargaining loyalty law brings additional bureaucracy and complicates the awarding of state contracts. Creutzburg stated that Merz's administration has failed to deliver on promised economic reforms, instead pursuing what he called a "pension package on credit" that adds billions in social spending to the federal deficit.
