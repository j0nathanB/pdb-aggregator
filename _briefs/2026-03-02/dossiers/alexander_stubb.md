### Finland's first lady skips Melania Trump White House event

HELSINKI, Finland — Finland's first lady Suzanne Innes-Stubb will not attend a March meeting for spouses of world leaders hosted by U.S. First Lady Melania Trump at the White House, citing scheduling conflicts. The President's Office said Tuesday that Innes-Stubb has responded to the invitation for the March 24-25 event focused on children's welfare. The White House gathering, called "Fostering the Future Together," aims to enhance children's well-being through education, innovation and technology initiatives.

### Finnish President Stubb visits Ukraine, calls invasion strategic failure

KYIV, Ukraine — Finnish President Alexander Stubb visited Ukraine on Tuesday, calling Russia's invasion a "strategic failure for Putin and Russia" as the war approaches its fourth anniversary. Stubb met with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and leaders from Nordic and Baltic countries to discuss peace negotiations and continued support for Ukraine, according to the Finnish president's office. The visit included stops at Saint Sophia Cathedral and a damaged power plant hit by Russian missile strikes. Stubb said Ukraine "is certainly not losing the war" and emphasized the need to maintain military and economic support while keeping pressure on Russia.

### Finnish President Says Ukraine Winning War Against Russia

HELSINKI, Finland — Finnish President Alexander Stubb said Europe must convince the United States that Ukraine is winning its war against Russia, according to an interview with Swedish public broadcaster SVT. Stubb said it is important for Americans to understand that Russia is not winning the war, which would give Ukraine a better negotiating position. The president's comments represent a shift in his rhetoric about Ukraine, as he previously had prepared Finns for a difficult peace for Ukraine, according to Finnish newspaper Helsingin Sanomat. A military expert warned that overly optimistic statements could be harmful.

### Finland Known Globally for Racism Controversies, Stubb Coverage

HELSINKI, Finland — Finland gained international attention in 2024 primarily for racism controversies and President Alexander Stubb's diplomatic activities, according to a new report by Finland Promotion Board.
