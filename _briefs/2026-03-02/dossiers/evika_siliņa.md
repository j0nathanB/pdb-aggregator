## Top Stories

### Latvia PM joins Baltic, Nordic leaders in Kyiv visit

KYIV, Ukraine — Latvian Prime Minister Evika Siliņa arrived in Ukraine's capital Monday for a working visit alongside Baltic and Nordic leaders, as well as European Commission President, to mark the fourth anniversary of Russia's invasion. "We have been with Ukraine since the first day of the war," Siliņa said, adding she came to demonstrate continued energy and military support. The visit includes memorial events and high-level discussions about future Ukraine support and peace negotiations with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Siliņa last visited Ukraine in July 2025 when Latvia delivered Patria armored personnel carriers to Ukrainian forces.

## International

### Latvia PM's Cancer Drug Claims Face Fact-Check Review

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa's claim that Latvia has more innovative cancer drugs than Lithuania and Estonia is being examined by fact-checkers, according to Latvian public media. The Re:Baltica fact-checking project is reviewing the assertion made by Siliņa, who leads the New Unity party. The review also examines claims by Riga Deputy Mayor Vilnis Ķirsis about minority children's exposure to the Latvian language.

## Domestic

### Latvian lawmaker urges PM to join military exercises

RIGA, Latvia — A Latvian parliamentarian said Prime Minister Evika Siliņa should participate in military exercises, following Sweden's example of involving politicians in defense training. Igor Rajevs, an independent member of parliament and parliamentary secretary at the Interior Ministry, said on TV24 that ministers should dedicate two days annually to military drills. "We should start with Prime Minister Siliņa participating in exercises," said Rajevs, a reserve colonel in Latvia's National Armed Forces.

*Sources: (Latvijas Avīze [1](https://www.la.lv/silinai-ir-japiedalas-militarajas-macibam-rajevs-iesaka-sekot-zviedrijas-piemeram))*

### Latvia PM Criticized for Olympic Trip, Ice Skating Display

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa faced criticism for her taxpayer-funded trip to the Milan Olympics and a controversial ice skating session at an Olympic hockey arena, according to commentary published in Latvijas Avīze. The article by Armands Puče accused Siliņa of being "bought off" by the Latvian Olympic Committee, which provided funding for officials' Olympic travel and uniforms. Puče criticized Siliņa for skating during time reserved for Latvia's national hockey team training, calling it inappropriate use of resources.
