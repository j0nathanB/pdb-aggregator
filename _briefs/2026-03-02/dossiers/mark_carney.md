### Carney knew of South Bow Keystone XL plans before Trump meeting

CALGARY, Canada — Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney was aware of oil company South Bow's plans to revive parts of the canceled Keystone XL pipeline when he discussed the project with U.S. President Donald Trump in October, a federal government source said Tuesday. South Bow is considering reviving portions of the already-built pipeline in an expansion project aimed at transporting more Canadian oil to the United States, the source told Reuters. During his White House meeting, Carney asked Trump if he would be interested in reviving Keystone XL with Canadian support as part of efforts to ease trade tensions between the two countries. ### Carney announces $2 billion Ukraine military aid package

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney announced approximately $2 billion in military assistance for Ukraine during a virtual Coalition of the Willing meeting marking four years since Russia's invasion, the Prime Minister's Office said Monday. Carney also announced the donation of 400 armoured vehicles to Ukrainian forces and the renewal of Operation UNIFIER, Canada's military training mission, according to the statement. The meeting was co-chaired by British Prime Minister Keir Starmer and French President Emmanuel Macron, with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy participating. Carney said Canada will impose sanctions on an additional 100 vessels in Russia's shadow fleet and provide $20 million to the Ukraine Energy Support Fund, the office said.

### Carney visits India seeking trade deal to reduce US dependence

MUMBAI, India — Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney arrived in India on Friday for his first official visit, seeking to reset relations and negotiate a comprehensive trade agreement by November as part of efforts to reduce Canada's dependence on the United States. Carney told business leaders the two countries are "natural partners" and said the visit "marks the end of a challenging period" following diplomatic tensions over allegations that Indian agents were involved in the killing of a Canadian Sikh activist. Foreign Minister Anita Anand said talks on the trade deal are expected to be completed by November, while officials indicated Carney will sign agreements including a possible uranium supply deal worth C$2.8 billion. After India, Carney will visit Australia and Japan as part of his strategy to forge new global alliances among what he calls "middle-power countries."

### Canada, South Korea sign defence information sharing agreement

OTTAWA, Canada — Canada and South Korea signed a defence agreement Wednesday for exchanging and protecting classified military information, roughly a month after Prime Minister Mark Carney urged middle powers to unite against economic coercion by great powers. The agreement, following negotiations concluded in October, establishes a legal basis for improved collaboration on defence procurement, industrial security and research, Ottawa said. The countries also agreed to update their strategic partnership and begin negotiations on a broader defence cooperation agreement that would include a framework for military cooperation.

### Carney backs U.S. strikes on Iran nuclear program

MUMBAI, India — Prime Minister Mark Carney said Saturday that Canada supports U.S. and Israeli military strikes against Iran aimed at preventing the country from obtaining nuclear weapons. Speaking at a forum in Mumbai, Carney said Canada backs "the United States acting to prevent Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon and to prevent its regime from further threatening international peace and security," though he said Canada is not participating militarily in the operation. The strikes came as U.S. President Donald Trump called for regime change in Iran, with Iranian state media later confirming that Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei was killed in the attacks. ### Carney says Davos speech resonated with middle powers

MUMBAI, India — Prime Minister Mark Carney said his World Economic Forum speech calling for middle powers to unite against "hegemons" resonated because he voiced what many countries were already thinking. ### Carney welcomes Burke as new Official Languages Commissioner

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney welcomed the appointment of Kelly A. ### Canadian passport fees to rise, tied to inflation

OTTAWA, Canada — Canadian passport fees will increase by 2.7 percent on March 31, with Prime Minister Mark Carney's government implementing a new policy tying future price increases to the consumer price index, officials said. A five-year passport will cost $123.24 and a 10-year passport $164.32 for domestic applicants, according to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada. The department said the passport program faces a $121 million revenue shortfall in fiscal year 2024-25 and is conducting a comprehensive fee review that could result in further increases. NDP immigration critic Jenny Kwan said the changes represent "another burden that the Liberal government is putting on everyday Canadians."
