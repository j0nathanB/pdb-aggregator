_Week of February 23, 2026_

> Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva faced mounting political challenges this week as polls showed him tied with Flavio Bolsonaro in a potential runoff and a majority of Brazilians disapproving of his government, according to survey data. ### Poll shows Lula, Flavio Bolsonaro tied in Brazil runoff

SAO PAULO, Brazil — Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and Senator Flavio Bolsonaro are virtually tied in a potential runoff for October's election, with 46.2% and 46.3% respectively, according to an AtlasIntel poll released Wednesday. The poll showed Lula's advantage has disappeared after leading Bolsonaro 49.2% to 44.9% in January and holding a 12-point edge in December. Former Workers' Party president Jose Genoino said the results indicate the need to reorganize the government's political strategy. AtlasIntel surveyed 4,986 people between February 19-24 with a margin of error of 1 percentage point.

### Brazilian Congress panel approves financial probe of Lula's son

BRASILIA, Brazil — A congressional investigative panel approved breaking banking and tax secrecy for Fabio Luis Lula da Silva, President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's eldest son, over suspected ties to a social security fraud scheme. ### Lula visits flood-hit Minas Gerais after 70 deaths

JUIZ DE FORA, Brazil — President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva visited flood-devastated areas of Minas Gerais state Saturday after landslides killed 70 people in the southeastern region. ### Lula revokes waterway decree after Indigenous Amazon protests

SAO PAULO, Brazil — President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's government said Monday it would revoke a decree allowing private concessions for waterways following 33 days of protests by thousands of Indigenous people at a Cargill facility in Santarem. ### Brazil posts lower primary budget surplus than expected

BRASILIA, Brazil — Brazil's central government posted a primary surplus of 86.9 billion reais ($16.87 billion) in January, below the 88.8 billion reais forecast by analysts, Treasury data showed Wednesday. Expenditures rose 2.9% from a year earlier while net revenue increased 1.2%, highlighting President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva's challenge in balancing public accounts through revenue-raising measures, according to the data. The Treasury attributed higher outlays mainly to increased social security payments and higher personnel costs. In the 12 months to January, Brazil's central government primary deficit reached 0.47% of gross domestic product, compared with a 0.25% primary surplus target for the year.
