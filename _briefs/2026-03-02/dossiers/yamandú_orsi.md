### Uruguay senators seek sanctions against Orsi aide over leak

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — Two opposition senators asked President Yamandú Orsi to sanction his deputy secretary Jorge Díaz for publicly disclosing parts of a classified government report about the Cardama shipyard contract dispute. Senators Javier García and Andrés Ojeda said in a letter that Díaz violated administrative rules by posting excerpts on social media from a legal report that the Defense Ministry had declared reserved information. The senators said the disclosure was "institutionally inadmissible" and potentially criminal, noting the same information had been denied to lawmakers through public information requests. Díaz had cited portions of lawyer Cristina Vázquez's report regarding the government's decision to terminate its contract with the Spanish shipyard.

### Uruguay President Signs European Union Trade Agreement Into Law

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi signed into law Friday Uruguay's ratification of the Mercosur-European Union trade agreement, with implementation expected to begin May 1, Foreign Minister Mario Lubetkin said. The Uruguayan Parliament approved the trade deal Thursday with support from all political parties except one, following more than 25 years of negotiations between the two trading blocs. Lubetkin said certain Uruguayan products including beef, wood, oils and wool could enter European markets tariff-free starting May 1 if the European Union accepts Uruguay's documentation. Uruguay and Argentina have ratified the agreement, while Brazil and Paraguay have yet to complete the process.

### Uruguay President Orsi weighs tunnel for Montevideo transport reform

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi is deciding whether to include a tunnel under 18 de Julio Avenue as part of a metropolitan transport reform, according to the Ministry of Transport and Public Works. ### Uruguay's Orsi government completes first year amid agricultural challenges

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi's government completed its first year in office marked by significant developments in Uruguay's agricultural sector, according to a review by El Observador. The administration faced criticism over the National Colonization Institute's $32.5 million purchase of the María Dolores ranch in July, with agricultural producers questioning the investment's necessity. Agricultural exporters expressed frustration with the peso-dollar exchange rate anchored below 40 pesos, while the government declared agricultural emergencies due to water deficits and avian flu outbreaks. Despite challenges, Uruguay achieved record export revenues from meat and dairy products, with the agricultural sector generating 34% of the country's $13.5 billion in export income.

### Uruguay workers strike over unpaid wages, criticize Orsi

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — Workers at Uruguay's Ministry of Social Development launched an indefinite strike Friday over unpaid wages, with their union criticizing President Yamandú Orsi for failing to prioritize the crisis.
