### Polish President Calls Security Council Meeting Over Lawmaker's Russian Ties

WARSAW, Poland — President Karol Nawrocki has called a National Security Council meeting for Feb. 11 to examine allegations about Parliament Speaker Włodzimierz Czarzasty's business connections to Russia, presidential spokesman Błażej Leśkiewicz said. The meeting will address "actions taken by state organs to clarify all circumstances of the eastern business-social contacts of Parliament Speaker Włodzimierz Czarzasty," according to Leśkiewicz. Media reports have alleged that Czarzasty's wife had business relationships with a Russian citizen connected to institutions controlled by the Russian state, claims Czarzasty has rejected as politically motivated. Leśkiewicz said Nawrocki is "basing his actions on findings that appeared in the media space" regarding the allegations.

WARSAW, Poland — President Karol Nawrocki convened a National Security Council meeting to address allegations against Parliament Speaker Włodzimierz Czarzasty, according to Rzeczpospolita newspaper. ### Polish FM to brief Nawrocki on Trump's 'Board of Peace'

WARSAW, Poland — Foreign Minister Radoslaw Sikorski said he will present President Karol Nawrocki with an assessment of opportunities and threats linked to President Donald Trump's proposed 'Board of Peace' that Nawrocki was invited to join. Trump is seeking to establish the international organization as an alternative to the UN with the stated aim of ending global conflicts, Sikorski said at a Washington press conference Wednesday. Nawrocki has called a National Security Council meeting for Feb. 11 to discuss Poland's potential involvement in the US-led body. Sikorski said he would outline concerns including Belarus's membership and Russia's invitation despite its war on Ukraine, plus unclear funding requirements of $1 billion for permanent membership.

### Polish President Nawrocki seeks expanded political control

WARSAW, Poland — President Karol Nawrocki is pursuing a strategy to consolidate political dominance by blocking military nominations and demanding meetings with intelligence chiefs and parliamentary leaders, according to political analyst Michał Szułdrzyński. The president refused to approve graduates from intelligence schools until he could meet with security service heads, a move initially blocked by Prime Minister Donald Tusk, Szułdrzyński wrote in Rzeczpospolita.
