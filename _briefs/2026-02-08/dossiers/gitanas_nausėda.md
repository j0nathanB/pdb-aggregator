## Top Stories

### Lithuania president vows to push for Belarus sanctions extension

VILNIUS, Lithuania — Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda said Tuesday he will push firmly for the European Union to extend sanctions on Belarus when the bloc decides in late February. "I will be among those who most firmly demand the extension of these sanctions," Nausėda told Žinių Radijas radio. He cited Belarus's continued alignment with Russia, imprisonment of political prisoners and what he described as hybrid attacks using smuggling balloons that have repeatedly forced temporary suspensions at Vilnius Airport. The most recent balloon incident occurred last week, prompting what Nausėda said was a strong U.S. response warning Minsk that such actions could undermine efforts to improve bilateral relations.

### Lithuanian authorities to inspect president's property over land complaint

VILNIUS, Lithuania — Lithuania's State Territorial Planning and Construction Inspectorate will inspect President Gitanas Nausėda's private residence in February following a complaint alleging he may have unlawfully occupied state-owned land, the Delfi.lt news portal reported Thursday. The inspection concerns Nausėda's home in Pavilniai Regional Park, where he has continued to live instead of moving to the official presidential residence in Turniškės after taking office. The Presidential Office told Delfi.lt that Nausėda is aware of the planned inspection and said it is up to authorities to determine whether the complaint is well-founded.

### Lithuanian President Calls for Investigation into Epstein Files

VILNIUS, Lithuania — President Gitanas Nausėda called on Lithuanian law enforcement to thoroughly examine communications from deceased U.S. financier and sex offender Jeffrey Epstein that mention Lithuanians, according to an interview broadcast Tuesday on Žinių radio. Nausėda said authorities should use legal assistance agreements with the United States if needed to investigate the matter. "I hope that institutions will evaluate in principle what they receive," Nausėda said, adding that while much information has been redacted, Lithuania has a legal assistance treaty with the U.S. The president said the available information "raises, to put it mildly, quite a few doubts."

### Poll shows majority of Lithuanians view president negatively

VILNIUS, Lithuania — More than half of Lithuanians said their opinion of President Gitanas Nausėda had worsened last year, according to a survey published by public broadcaster LRT. The poll showed 54% of respondents reported a more negative view of the president, while only 5% said their opinion had improved, according to pollster Baltijos tyrimai. The survey of 1,063 adults was conducted from Jan. 8-24 with a margin of error of up to 3.1%. Men, people over 50, rural residents, managers and the unemployed were more likely to report worsened views of Nausėda, the poll said.
