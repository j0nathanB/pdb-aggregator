# Week of February 9, 2026

> European leaders this week pushed competing visions for the continent's economic future, with French President Emmanuel Macron advocating for EU joint borrowing through eurobonds while facing resistance from other member states on competitiveness measures. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney unveiled a defense strategy targeting 125,000 jobs amid diplomatic tensions with the United States over infrastructure funding claims. In Latin America, Uruguay's President Yamandú Orsi scaled back his universal school bonus campaign promise to target only lower-income families, while Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva maintained public appearances despite facing a 52.5% disapproval rating. British Prime Minister Keir Starmer announced plans for a crackdown on young people's smartphone use as his national security adviser reportedly considers leaving Downing Street.

### Starmer promises crackdown on young people's smartphone use
{: #starmer-promises-crackdown-on-young-people-s-smartphone-use}

LONDON, Britain — Prime Minister Keir Starmer said Sunday he will seek broader powers to regulate internet access and crack down on addictive elements of social media within months. Starmer said the government will consult on an Australian-style social media ban for children under 16 and create new legal powers to take immediate action following the consultation. The prime minister said technology is moving fast and the law must keep up, adding that if confronting big social media companies is necessary, "bring it on." The measures will include amendments to existing legislation to cover AI chatbots and prevent children from using virtual private networks to access restricted content.

*Sources: (Reuters [1](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/uks-starmer-seeks-greater-powers-regulate-online-access-2026-02-15/); BBC [1](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cvg38x13x5yo))*

### Carney tells Trump Canada paid for Gordie Howe bridge
{: #carney-tells-trump-canada-paid-for-gordie-howe-bridge}

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney said Tuesday he spoke with U.S. President Donald Trump about the $4.7 billion Gordie Howe International Bridge after Trump threatened to block its opening. Carney told reporters he explained to Trump that Canada paid the full construction cost and that the bridge connecting Detroit and Windsor, Ontario, is jointly owned by Michigan and Canada. Trump had criticized Canada's financing of the bridge and incorrectly suggested it would be owned entirely by Canada, according to Carney. The bridge is set to open in the coming months with costs to be recovered through tolls over 30 years.

*Sources: (Reuters [1](https://www.reuters.com/world/canadas-carney-says-issue-bridge-that-trump-threatened-will-be-settled-2026-02-10/); National Post [1](https://nationalpost.com/news/canada/carney-gordie-howe-bridge-trump-threat); CBC News [1](https://www.cbc.ca/news/politics/trump-carney-gordie-howe-bridge-9.7082658))*

### Carney unveils defence strategy targeting 125,000 jobs, domestic investment
{: #carney-unveils-defence-strategy-targeting-125-000-jobs-domestic-investment}

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney unveiled a defence-industrial strategy aimed at creating 125,000 jobs and generating more than $500 billion in investment over a decade, according to government documents. The plan seeks to boost the share of defence acquisitions awarded to Canadian firms to 70 percent, a significant shift for a country that has long relied on U.S. military contractors. Carney had planned to announce the strategy last week but postponed it following a mass shooting in British Columbia. The initiative comes as Canada embarks on its largest military buildup in decades, driven by NATO spending commitments and concerns about Russian activity in the Arctic.

*Sources: (National Post [1](https://nationalpost.com/news/canada/carney-buy-canada-defence-strategy-domestic-investment))*

### Starmer's national security adviser considers Downing Street exit
{: #starmer-s-national-security-adviser-considers-downing-street-exit}

LONDON, Britain — Prime Minister Keir Starmer's national security adviser Jonathan Powell is considering leaving his position at the end of the year, according to sources familiar with the matter. Powell, who served as chief of staff under Tony Blair, rejected overtures to replace Morgan McSweeney as Starmer's chief of staff after McSweeney's resignation, the Guardian reported. Downing Street sources denied Powell had plans to step down, saying he would continue as national security adviser. Powell, credited as a key architect of the controversial Chagos Islands deal with Mauritius, reportedly advised against appointing Peter Mandelson as ambassador to Washington.

*Sources: (The Guardian [1](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/feb/15/jonathan-powell-rejects-replacing-morgan-mcsweeney-starmer-chief-of-staff))*

### EU Leaders Push Competitiveness Measures, Split on Eurobonds *(Giorgia Meloni, Emmanuel Macron, Gitanas Nausėda, Friedrich Merz)*
{: #eu-leaders-push-competitiveness-measures-split-on-eurobonds}

BRUSSELS, Belgium — European Union leaders called for urgent measures to boost the bloc's economic competitiveness during meetings this week, though they remained divided on financing mechanisms. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said she supports eurobonds despite acknowledging the issue remains divisive, while French President Emmanuel Macron called for joint borrowing to fund strategic investments in green technology and defense. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz proposed sweeping deregulation and automatic project approvals, saying Europe must close its growth gap with the United States and China. Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda said the EU should pursue expanded free trade agreements with Australia, the Philippines and other regional countries.
