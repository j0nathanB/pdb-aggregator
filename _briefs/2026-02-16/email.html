THE MIDDLE POWERS MONITOR
Week of February 9, 2026
THIS WEEK
This week European leaders clashed over financing mechanisms for economic competitiveness, with Macron pushing eurobonds while facing resistance from other member states. Canadian Prime Minister Carney unveiled a major defense strategy targeting 125,000 jobs while managing diplomatic tensions with Trump over infrastructure funding. In Latin America, Uruguay's Orsi scaled back his universal school bonus promise while Brazil's Lula maintained public appearances despite a 52.5% disapproval rating. British Prime Minister Starmer announced smartphone restrictions for youth as his national security adviser considers departing Downing Street.

North America

Carney dominated headlines with his defense strategy targeting 125,000 jobs and $500 billion in investment while defending Canada's full payment for the Gordie Howe bridge to Trump amid U.S. diplomatic tensions. The Canadian leader received praise from China's foreign minister at international forums and announced new Luxembourg partnerships. Meanwhile, Mexico's Sheinbaum announced cinema laws requiring 10% domestic content in theaters, offered to serve as an air bridge for Cuba aid, and rejected U.S. congressional accusations calling her a 'puppet' while demanding explanations for El Paso airspace closures.

South America

Orsi faced early criticism for scaling back his campaign promise of universal school bonuses to target only lower-income families, with political analysts characterizing his administration as operating with limited ambition. The Uruguayan president also received the Mercosur-EU trade agreement for parliamentary ratification and met with soccer officials regarding the 2030 World Cup hosting role. In Brazil, Lula maintained an active schedule attending Carnival celebrations across three cities and inaugurating a Rio hospital emergency center despite facing a 52.5% disapproval rating, while signing legislation providing free cooking gas to poor families and undergoing a minor medical procedure to remove skin lesions.

Europe

Macron intensified his push for EU joint borrowing through eurobonds at conferences in Munich and Belgium while defending European spending against Musk's criticism and signaling potential diplomatic shifts including planned India visits and possible Putin talks on Ukraine. Starmer faced mounting pressure over personnel controversies including Epstein scandal fallout and his national security adviser's potential departure, while announcing smartphone restrictions for youth and pursuing closer UK-EU energy alignment. Merz announced nuclear deterrent discussions with France and led German business leaders to China, while Meloni backed eurobonds and approved controversial migration legislation allowing naval blockades. Zelenskyy conducted intensive Western diplomatic meetings including weapons production talks with Scholz while denying election announcement plans, and other leaders from Stubb to Tusk navigated various domestic and international challenges ranging from Nokia promotion to coalition pressures.

Baltic States

Nausėda said Taiwan should decide its own representative office name in Lithuania while participating in European forums and linking EU competitiveness to security concerns. Siliņa faced coalition pressure threatening her position amid government stability concerns while considering dismissing her agriculture minister over a timber probe, though she maintained active media presence and attended Munich meetings with Scholz. Michal coordinated foreign policy with President Karis ahead of Brussels summits while criticizing potential MAGA foreign policy approaches that would favor Orban over traditional allies.
