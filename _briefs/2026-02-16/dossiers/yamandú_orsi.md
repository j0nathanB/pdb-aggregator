### Uruguay's Orsi scales back universal school bonus promise

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi's administration will limit a promised $2,500 school bonus to students from lower-income families rather than providing universal coverage as pledged during his campaign, education officials said. The National Public Education Administration cited "budgetary management problems" for the scaled-back implementation, according to interim president Elbia Pereira. Orsi had promised during presidential debates that "all students" starting primary school would receive the bonus, but the program will now cover only the three lowest income quintiles in 2026, expanding gradually to universal coverage by 2028. The bonus affects more than 310,000 primary school students nationwide.

### Uruguay Parliament receives Mercosur-EU trade agreement for ratification

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — Uruguay's Parliament received the official Mercosur-European Union trade agreement document Tuesday for ratification, making it the last Mercosur country to begin the legislative process. President Yamandú Orsi returned from China as the document arrived via diplomatic pouch from Paraguay, according to El Observador. The governing coalition plans to establish special committees in both chambers to review the agreement, while opposition lawmakers prefer using existing International Affairs committees. Meanwhile, negotiations continue between government and opposition parties to potentially cancel a scheduled Feb. 19 interpellation of Foreign Minister Mario Lubetkin.

### Uruguay President Orsi to decide on Montevideo tunnel project

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi is expected to decide in coming days whether to approve a tunnel under 18 de Julio Avenue as part of a metropolitan transport reform, the Ministry of Transport and Public Works said Thursday. ### Uruguay's state vehicle fleet grows to 18,480 units

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — Uruguay's state vehicle fleet has grown to 18,480 units in 2025, continuing an expansion that began under previous administrations and has persisted into President Yamandú Orsi's government, according to data obtained by weekly publication Búsqueda. The fleet increased from 16,155 vehicles when former President Luis Lacalle Pou took office to 18,148 by the end of his term, then reached 18,480 under Orsi's administration. Despite the growth in vehicles, fuel consumption decreased by 3 million liters from 57.6 million liters in 2024 to 54.6 million in 2025, state oil company Ancap said. The Interior Ministry operates the largest fleet with 4,947 vehicles, followed by state utility UTE with 1,724 units.
