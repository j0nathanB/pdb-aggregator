### Carney tells Trump Canada paid for Gordie Howe bridge

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney said Tuesday he spoke with U.S. President Donald Trump about the $4.7 billion Gordie Howe International Bridge after Trump threatened to block its opening. Carney told reporters he explained to Trump that Canada paid the full construction cost and that the bridge connecting Detroit and Windsor, Ontario, is jointly owned by Michigan and Canada. Trump had criticized Canada's financing of the bridge and incorrectly suggested it would be owned entirely by Canada, according to Carney. The bridge is set to open in the coming months with costs to be recovered through tolls over 30 years.

### Carney unveils defence strategy targeting 125,000 jobs, domestic investment

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney unveiled a defence-industrial strategy aimed at creating 125,000 jobs and generating more than $500 billion in investment over a decade, according to government documents. The plan seeks to boost the share of defence acquisitions awarded to Canadian firms to 70 percent, a significant shift for a country that has long relied on U.S. military contractors. Carney had planned to announce the strategy last week but postponed it following a mass shooting in British Columbia. The initiative comes as Canada embarks on its largest military buildup in decades, driven by NATO spending commitments and concerns about Russian activity in the Arctic.

### International group urges Carney to protect endangered fish species

MONTREAL, Canada — The International Union for Conservation of Nature sent a letter to Prime Minister Mark Carney and Quebec Premier François Legault expressing "serious concern" about the survival of the copper redhorse fish amid Montreal port expansion plans at Contrecœur. The organization said the expansion project poses "high risks" to the endangered species, which exists only in a small portion of the St. Lawrence River and Richelieu River in southern Quebec. Fisheries and Oceans Canada granted authorization for the work on Jan. 8, accepting a compensation plan that includes planting seagrass habitats covering 3.29 hectares starting in summer 2026. The IUCN said no comparable examples exist worldwide demonstrating that compensatory measures for such specialized fish species have been beneficial.

### Carney announces new partnerships with Luxembourg during PM visit

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney announced new economic, security and educational partnerships with Luxembourg during a three-day visit by Luxembourg Prime Minister Luc Frieden from Feb. 7-9, the Prime Minister's Office said. The leaders launched a 2026 Canada-Luxembourg Financial Sector Policy Dialogue and welcomed establishment of the McGill Luxembourg Centre for Finance, according to the statement. Carney and Frieden also committed to expanding bilateral trade in advanced manufacturing, aerospace and related technologies, and agreed to deepen cooperation on a proposed Defence, Security and Resilience Bank to provide financing for defense initiatives. The prime ministers reiterated support for Ukraine against Russian aggression and discussed strengthening defense supply chains, the office said.
