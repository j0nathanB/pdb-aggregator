## Top Stories

### Merz announces nuclear deterrent talks with France at Munich conference

MUNICH, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Friday he has begun confidential talks with French President Emmanuel Macron about European nuclear deterrence, speaking at the opening of the Munich Security Conference. Merz said the discussions are "strictly embedded within our nuclear sharing in NATO" and that Germany will adhere to its legal obligations prohibiting nuclear weapons development. Macron, speaking at the same conference, said Europe must become "a geopolitical power" and strengthen its security architecture given the Russian threat. The talks come as European leaders seek greater independence while maintaining their alliance with the United States.

### Merz calls for European deregulation at industry summit

BRUSSELS, Belgium — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called for sweeping deregulation across the European Union and automatic approval of delayed projects during a speech at the European Industry Summit on Tuesday. Merz said Europe must close its economic growth gap with the United States and China, citing figures showing the EU has grown only 1 percent annually over the past 20 years compared to 2 percent for the U.S. and 8 percent for China. The chancellor proposed implementing a "28th regime" of unified European business rules and said any project not approved within weeks or months should be automatically regarded as approved. Merz said European leaders will negotiate a European Competitiveness Agenda at meetings in Alden Biesen, with first decisions expected at the March European Council meeting.

*Sources: (Bundeskanzleramt [1](https://www.bundeskanzler.de/bk-en/news/speech-at-the-european-industry-summit-2406966))*

### Merz calls for EU carbon trading system reform

BERLIN, Germany — Chancellor Friedrich Merz called for potential reform or suspension of the European Union's carbon emissions trading system during an industrial summit in Antwerp, triggering a sharp decline in carbon certificate prices. The carbon trading system price fell to its lowest level since August 2025, dropping about 7 percent after Merz said the system should be overhauled or postponed if it fails to achieve carbon-free production, according to German financial newspaper FAZ. The reference contract for CO2 certificates traded at around 71 euros per ton on Friday, losing nearly 20 percent of its value since the beginning of the year. Representatives from other EU states have called for intervention to lower carbon prices to boost European competitiveness.

### Euronext CEO urges European business integration without waiting for Merz

PARIS, France — Euronext CEO Stéphane Boujnah said European businesses must pursue integration projects without waiting for political leaders including German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, according to an interview published Saturday in Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung. Boujnah, who leads Europe's largest stock exchange, said private companies should drive capital market union efforts rather than wait for direction from Merz, French President Emmanuel Macron or European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. The French executive expressed interest in meeting with Merz to discuss creating a European "super exchange" but said businesses cannot afford to wait for political action amid global economic pressures.

### Merz strengthens ties with Italy as France relations cool

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz is deepening cooperation with Italy while relations with France deteriorate, according to German officials and foreign policy experts. Merz praised unprecedented closeness with Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni during January government consultations in Rome, while tensions with French President Emmanuel Macron have returned to levels seen under former Chancellor Olaf Scholz. A senior Union party foreign policy expert said Germany sees more potential in cooperation with Italy, Europe's third-largest EU member, than in the traditional Franco-German partnership. The shift reflects disputes over defense projects, trade agreements and Macron's unilateral diplomatic initiatives, including sending an envoy to Moscow without prior coordination with Germany.

### German Chancellor Merz Takes Business Leaders to China

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz will lead a large business delegation on his first trip to China, according to German business publication Handelsblatt. The delegation includes chief executives from major German companies including Volkswagen's Oliver Blume, Mercedes-Benz's Ola Källenius, BMW's Oliver Zipse, Siemens' Roland Busch, and Bayer's Bill Anderson. Handelsblatt reported that business interest in joining the trip exceeded available spots. The visit comes as German companies face growing competition from Chinese rivals offering innovative products at low prices, creating what Germany calls a "China shock" in global markets.
