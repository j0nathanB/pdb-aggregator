# Week of March 16, 2026

> European leaders remained divided this week over military responses to Iran's strait blockade, with British Prime Minister Keir Starmer resisting pressure from former President Trump to join potential military action while French President Emmanuel Macron named the country's new aircraft carrier 'Free France' amid Mediterranean operations against Russian vessels. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni faced a domestic test with judicial reform voting as Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said his country helped five nations defend against Iranian drone attacks. In the Americas, Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva criticized the UN Security Council as 'omissive' on global conflicts while announcing Finance Minister Fernando Haddad's gubernatorial candidacy, and Uruguayan President Yamandú Orsi assumed the CELAC presidency calling for regional anti-drug cooperation amid diplomatic pressures from Argentina and European Parliament members.

### Italians vote on judicial reform in test for Meloni
{: #italians-vote-on-judicial-reform-in-test-for-meloni}

ROME, Italy — Italians began voting Sunday in a referendum on Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's contested judicial reform, a key test for her right-wing coalition ahead of next year's general election. The constitutional changes would separate career paths of judges and prosecutors and split Italy's judicial self-governing body into two entities, Reuters reported. Opposition parties led by the Democratic Party warned the reform would undermine judicial independence and allow political interference, while Meloni's government said the changes are needed to curb politicized appointments after scandals exposed backroom deals. Pre-vote polls showed the two camps in a tight race, with analysts saying a victory for either side could significantly impact the political landscape.

### European Leaders Split on Military Response to Iran Strait Blockade *(Friedrich Merz, Gitanas Nausėda, Alexander Stubb, Giorgia Meloni)*
{: #european-leaders-split-on-military-response-to-iran-strait-blockade}

BRUSSELS, Belgium — European leaders offered mixed responses to Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, with Italy and Germany ruling out military participation while Finland backed international condemnation efforts. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said Wednesday her country would not join military missions to force open the strategic waterway, while German Chancellor Friedrich Merz told lawmakers Germany would not participate in U.S.-led operations against Iran. Finnish President Alexander Stubb said Friday his country joined an international statement condemning Iranian attacks on commercial vessels, while Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda praised German-Baltic security cooperation during separate remarks Tuesday. The strait handles about one-fifth of global oil shipments.

### Starmer resists Trump pressure to join Iran war
{: #starmer-resists-trump-pressure-to-join-iran-war}

LONDON, Britain — Prime Minister Keir Starmer said he will not allow Britain to be drawn into a wider conflict with Iran, resisting pressure from President Donald Trump to send warships to help reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Trump criticized Starmer's leadership as "terrible" and said the British leader is "no Churchill" after Starmer told him he needed to consult his team before committing military assets, according to Trump's comments to reporters. Starmer said he wants to see the Middle East conflict end "as quickly as possible" and warned that prolonging the war makes the situation more dangerous. European allies including Germany and France have similarly declined Trump's calls to form a coalition to protect the strategic waterway, which handles a fifth of the world's energy supplies.

### Macron names new French aircraft carrier 'Free France'
{: #macron-names-new-french-aircraft-carrier-free-france}

INDRE, France — President Emmanuel Macron said Wednesday that France's new aircraft carrier will be named France Libre (Free France), referencing the World War Two resistance movement. Macron announced the name during a visit to the construction site in western France, where state-owned shipyard Naval Group has begun work on the 10.2 billion euro ($12 billion) vessel. The nuclear-powered warship will replace the aging Charles de Gaulle and become operational in 2038, measuring 310 meters in length with two nuclear reactors. Macron said the naming reflects that "our seas and oceans are new arenas of contemporary conflict."

### Ukraine helps 5 countries defend against Iranian drone attacks
{: #ukraine-helps-5-countries-defend-against-iranian-drone-attacks}

KYIV, Ukraine — Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said his country is providing drone defense expertise to five Gulf nations to counter Iranian attacks, with Ukrainian units already achieving results in the region. Zelenskyy said in his evening address that Ukraine is working daily with Gulf countries to provide expertise and support, adding that Russia is irritated by Ukraine's global assistance role. The president previously confirmed that three teams of dozens of Ukrainian drone experts had been deployed to Qatar, the United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia. Defense Secretary Rustem Umierov said the units are protecting civilian and critical infrastructure from Iranian drones.
