## Top Stories

### Union leader criticizes Carney, Poilievre auto strategies as flawed

WINDSOR, Ontario — A prominent auto union leader said both Prime Minister Mark Carney's electric vehicle strategy and Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre's tariff-focused plan are inadequate for Canada's struggling automotive sector. James Stewart, president of Unifor Local 444 representing Stellantis assembly plant workers, told CBC Radio that neither approach addresses the core challenge of trade barriers with the United States, which buys 90 percent of Canadian-made vehicles. Stewart said Carney's push to diversify beyond the U.S. market ignores the reality that other regions don't purchase Canadian cars, while Poilievre's proposal to revive a modern Auto Pact won't work with dozens of companies now operating in the market.

### Carney meets UK Prime Minister Starmer in London

LONDON, United Kingdom — Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney met with British Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer during a bilateral visit to the UK, the Prime Minister's Office said. The leaders discussed the Middle East situation, condemning Iranian missile and drone attacks on civilian and energy infrastructure while expressing concern over regional escalation risks and global economic consequences, according to the statement. Carney and Starmer also reaffirmed support for Ukraine and agreed to deepen Canada-UK economic and security cooperation, including through initiatives like a proposed Defence, Security and Resilience Bank.

## International

### Carney's extensive foreign travel marks shift in PM role

OTTAWA, Canada — Prime Minister Mark Carney spent 84 days traveling internationally in his first year in office, nearly double the pace of his predecessor Justin Trudeau and more than twice that of Stephen Harper, according to Global Affairs Canada records. The extensive travel included visits to 23 countries across Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas, with political analysts debating whether such international engagement reflects changing demands of the prime minister's role. Former Harper chief of staff Ian Brodie said international travel was 'a net negative politically' for prime ministers, while pollster Dan Arnold argued Carney's global engagement reinforces perceptions he can handle the world stage amid concerns about U.S. President Donald Trump. Former diplomat Kerry Buck said Canada needs to view international engagement as 'existential' rather than optional given increasing global threats.
