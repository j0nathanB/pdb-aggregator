### Lithuanian president warns against easing Belarus sanctions

BRUSSELS, Belgium — Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda said Thursday that the European Union should maintain its independent sanctions policy on Belarus despite recent U.S. moves to ease restrictions. Nausėda told reporters the EU recently extended sanctions against Belarus for another 12 months and has no intention of reconsidering them, stating "there are no possibilities and no political will to review sanctions on Belarus." His comments came after Belarus released 250 political prisoners following talks with Washington, prompting the United States to lift sanctions on key Belarusian entities including potash producer Belaruskali. Nausėda warned that such prisoner exchanges could create harmful incentives for Minsk to detain more people for future leverage.

### Lithuanian President calls 'Lithuania for Lithuanians' slogan inappropriate

VILNIUS, Lithuania — President Gitanas Nausėda said Wednesday the slogan "Lithuania for Lithuanians" is inappropriate and evokes troubling historical associations with 1930s Germany. Nausėda made the comments after authorities opened a pretrial investigation into an incident during March 11 Independence Day commemorations, when Vilnius University professor Konstantinas Andrijauskas removed a banner bearing the slogan. "To me, such slogans sound like a kind of relic of the past. They are alien to me," Nausėda said on LRT TV, urging citizens to express patriotism in more inclusive ways. Police said Andrijauskas is under investigation for suspected disturbance of public order after removing what authorities described as an extremist banner.

### Lithuanian President Praises German-Baltic Chamber Role in Relations

VILNIUS, Lithuania — Lithuanian President Gitanas Nausėda praised the German-Baltic Chamber of Commerce's role in developing economic and security relations between Vilnius and Berlin during the organization's 30th anniversary celebration Tuesday. "Your presence here is an act of solidarity. It reminds us that we are not alone in defending freedom, stability and the European way of life," Nausėda said in remarks cited by the presidential office. The president highlighted Germany's brigade deployment in Lithuania and Rheinmetall's ammunition factory construction as examples of Berlin-Vilnius cooperation, noting that 500 companies participate in the chamber community. Germany plans to deploy about 5,000 troops in Lithuania by the end of 2027, with 1,700 German brigade soldiers already serving in the country as of March.

### Lithuanian president calls low birth rates 'self-destruction'

VILNIUS, Lithuania — President Gitanas Nausėda said Wednesday that Lithuania's declining birth rates represent a path toward "peaceful self-destruction" and warned the nation could cease to exist within 50-80 years without action. "By doing nothing we would be moving on the path of 'peaceful self-destruction'," Nausėda said in an interview with public broadcaster LRT. The president plans to submit proposals to Parliament addressing demographic challenges, including zero personal income tax for parents with multiple children and corporate tax incentives for employers hiring such parents. Nausėda met with parliamentary leadership last week and identified solving demographic issues as a priority.

### Lithuania Constitutional Court dismisses case against presidential decrees

VILNIUS, Lithuania — Lithuania's Constitutional Court dismissed a case challenging presidential decrees that approved incomplete government cabinets, court chairman Gintaras Goda said Tuesday. The court ruled the case became moot after President Gitanas Nausėda appointed missing ministers to complete both cabinets led by Social Democrats Gintautas Paluckas and Ingrida Ruginienė. Opposition lawmakers had challenged the constitutionality of approving governments with only 12 of 14 required ministers, but the court said reviewing the decrees would be pointless since they are no longer in effect.

### Lithuanian President Considers Daily Fuel Price Cap Proposal

VILNIUS, Lithuania — President Gitanas Nausėda said he is considering proposing daily fuel price caps to address frequent price increases throughout the day. Speaking on a business podcast, Nausėda said he has tasked his advisory team to study Belgium's experience, where the government has set daily fuel price limits based on objective criteria like current oil prices for more than 50 years. The president said price caps like those implemented by Hungary and Croatia are not sustainable solutions.
