### Latvia's Siliņa says no fuel supply crisis amid Hormuz tensions

RIGA, Latvia — Latvian Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said the country faces no fuel supply crisis despite rising prices linked to conflicts in the Middle East, according to statements reported by Delfi Latvija. Siliņa said Latvia would seriously evaluate any NATO request for assistance regarding the Strait of Hormuz blockade, though no such request has been received. The prime minister said Latvia has sufficient fuel reserves and receives supplies from Norway, where there is no conflict.

### Latvia PM highlights digital development at AI forum

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said Monday that Latvia is one of Europe's most digitally developed countries as she opened an international forum on artificial intelligence's impact on the future of work. Siliņa stated that AI already affects the economy, public services, security and competitiveness, but emphasized that human judgment remains crucial for ethical decisions. The forum brought together 600 participants from 20 countries to discuss European workforce development amid rapid AI implementation. ## International

### Latvia PM rejects business ties with Russia over security

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said Latvia's government will not support restoring business relations with Russia, prioritizing national security over economic gains. Speaking on TV24's "Nedeļa.Post Scriptum" program, Siliņa warned that some political parties might try to change their rhetoric on Russia cooperation. "Security is something we must not sell. We must not risk our people's safety for easy money," Siliņa said, adding she would not work with parties supporting such policies. The prime minister emphasized Latvia should focus on technology and digitalization rather than looking back to cooperation with Russia.

## Domestic

### Latvia PM Siliņa defends government record amid criticism

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa delivered her annual government report to parliament March 12, defending her administration's achievements while facing sharp criticism from opposition lawmakers. Siliņa told the Saeima that her government had accomplished many of its strategic goals, saying the priorities "have become specific government decisions" that are "necessary to be adopted today so that in the long term we can all live better in Latvia." Opposition deputy Edgars Tavars criticized the report's optimistic tone, questioning whether it reflected public perception, while Linda Liepiņa called it "a self-promotional document" lacking specifics. Even coalition member Antonina Nenaševa acknowledged the government's struggles, saying "old political instincts prevailed" with "hesitation" and "avoidance of responsibility."
