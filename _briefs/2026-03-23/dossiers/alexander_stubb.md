# 🇫🇮 Finland
## Alexander Stubb, President
_Week of March 16, 2026_

> Finnish President Alexander Stubb called for preserving salvageable ties between the United States and Europe while describing China as a stable global power during diplomatic engagements this week. ## Top Stories

### Finland's Stubb urges saving salvageable US-Europe ties

LONDON, Britain — Finnish President Alexander Stubb said Europe and the United States must try to save what can be salvaged from the transatlantic relationship, according to an interview published Sunday in The Telegraph. "Don't throw the baby out with the bathwater. Save what can be saved from the transatlantic relationship, like NATO and defense, and then politely disagree on tariffs, climate change and other issues," Stubb said. The Finnish leader acknowledged contacting U.S. President Donald Trump after the Iran conflict began Feb. 28, stating he tries to "choose my battles" from Finland's perspective. Stubb expressed concern that Trump's decision to lift Russian oil sanctions benefits Russia, as does rising oil prices.

### Finnish President Says China Appears Stable Global Power

LONDON, Britain — Finnish President Alexander Stubb told students at the London School of Economics that China appears to be a stable global power that is easy to work with, during a two-day working visit to Britain. Stubb said the world is experiencing a period of disorder and that global power dynamics are shifting, according to remarks reported by Helsingin Sanomat. The president also met with King Charles III during his visit and appeared alongside Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and British Prime Minister Keir Starmer. Stubb said it would take about five years for the current global situation to stabilize.

### Finland joins statement condemning Iran's Strait of Hormuz attacks

HELSINKI, Finland — President Alexander Stubb said Friday that Finland joined an international statement condemning Iran's recent attacks on commercial vessels and attempts to block shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. The statement, issued with partner countries, calls on all nations to respect international law and maintain international security principles, Stubb said on social media platform X. The countries signing the statement expressed readiness to support efforts to ensure safe passage through the strait, according to Stubb. Iran has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, through which about one-fifth of the world's oil shipments passed before the conflict.

### Finland to host JEF Leaders' Summit in Helsinki

HELSINKI, Finland — President Alexander Stubb will host a Joint Expeditionary Force Leaders' Summit at the Presidential Palace on March 26, the Office of the President said Tuesday. The summit will include leaders from the United Kingdom, Lithuania, Norway, Sweden, Latvia, Estonia, Iceland and the Netherlands, with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney participating remotely, according to the presidential office. The discussions will focus on support for Ukraine and Europe's security situation, the office said. The JEF is a UK-led multilateral defense cooperation framework involving 10 Northern European and Baltic nations.

### Finnish President Says Finland Unaware of Denmark's Greenland Defense Plans

RASEBORG, Finland — Finnish President Alexander Stubb said Finland had no knowledge of Denmark's military preparations in Greenland during January's crisis involving U.S. interest in the territory. Stubb told reporters that Finland sent two officers to Greenland for what it considered normal exercise preparation, unaware that Denmark had deployed troops with explosives to potentially destroy runways if the U.S. attacked. Danish broadcaster DR reported that Denmark feared a U.S. invasion and sent forces with blood bags in case of serious conflict. Stubb said the crisis was resolved peacefully between Denmark and the United States, with NATO agreeing to strengthen Arctic defense.

### Finnish President Awards Rare Sports Honors to Two Officials

HELSINKI, Finland — President Alexander Stubb awarded Finland's Grand Cross of Sports and Physical Culture to two veteran sports officials Friday, according to the presidential office. The recipients were Risto Nieminen, former chairman of the Finnish Olympic Committee and longtime CEO of Veikkaus, and Pirjo Wilmi, who became the first woman elected to the International Olympic Committee in 1981. The honor recognizes exceptional service to Finnish sports culture and can be held by a maximum of 12 living Finns simultaneously. Nine people now hold the distinction, including Olympic champions Lasse Virén and Marja-Liisa Kirvesniemi.
