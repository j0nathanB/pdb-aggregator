## Top Stories

### Macron names new French aircraft carrier 'Free France'

INDRE, France — President Emmanuel Macron said Wednesday that France's new aircraft carrier will be named France Libre (Free France), referencing the World War Two resistance movement. Macron announced the name during a visit to the construction site in western France, where state-owned shipyard Naval Group has begun work on the 10.2 billion euro ($12 billion) vessel. The nuclear-powered warship will replace the aging Charles de Gaulle and become operational in 2038, measuring 310 meters in length with two nuclear reactors. Macron said the naming reflects that "our seas and oceans are new arenas of contemporary conflict."

### France boards Russian tanker in Mediterranean shadow fleet operation

PARIS, France — The French Navy boarded a Mozambique-flagged oil tanker in the Mediterranean Sea on Friday that President Emmanuel Macron said belonged to Russia's shadow fleet used to circumvent Western sanctions. The vessel Deyna, which had sailed from the Russian port of Murmansk, was intercepted near Spain's Balearic Islands with British support, according to French maritime authorities. Macron said the ships "circumvent international sanctions and violate the law of the sea" and are "war profiteers" that help finance Russia's war effort. The operation marks France's third interception of a suspected Russian shadow fleet vessel in recent months as European nations intensify efforts to enforce sanctions imposed over Moscow's invasion of Ukraine.

### Macron announces mediation to salvage European fighter jet project

PARIS, France — French President Emmanuel Macron announced a mediation mission to reconcile Airbus and Dassault Aviation amid a dispute threatening the 100-billion-euro European fighter jet project, French media reported. The Future Combat Air System project, launched by Macron and former German Chancellor Angela Merkel in 2017 with Spain joining later, faces collapse over disagreements between the companies on governance and industrial control. Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz will discuss the crisis during a meeting Wednesday evening ahead of the EU summit, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters. The project aims to develop a digitally connected array of crewed fighters and combat drones to replace current aircraft by 2040.

### Macron calls for EU 'democratic shield' against election interference

BRUSSELS, Belgium — French President Emmanuel Macron called Thursday for the European Union to create a "democratic shield" to protect against electoral interference, particularly on social media and digital platforms. "Europeans must absolutely mobilize to protect our democracies from these interferences. It's a necessity. It's this democratic shield in which we believe," Macron said upon arriving in Brussels for an EU summit. He cited Slovenia as an example, saying outgoing liberal Prime Minister Robert Golob "was victim of characterized interferences" from "third countries" and "false information" during his legislative campaign against nationalist Janez Jansa. France is asking the European Commission for guidelines that would require digital platforms to assess "systemic risks during electoral periods" and mitigate risks related to fake accounts, illegal content, and AI-generated material, according to the Élysée Palace.

### French startup abandons $10 billion supercomputer project for US

PARIS, France — British cloud startup Fluidstack has abandoned its 10 billion euro ($10.7 billion) supercomputer project in France, choosing to focus on U.S. operations instead, Bloomberg reported Friday. The project was part of President Emmanuel Macron's 109 billion euro AI investment plan announced in February 2025. The company had signed agreements to build data centers in Bosquel, northern France, and near Paris, with the first phase scheduled to be operational by 2026. Fluidstack recently secured a $50 billion contract with AI company Anthropic in the United States and moved its headquarters to New York in December.

### Macron Faces Political Chaos as Presidential Term Nears End

PARIS, France — French President Emmanuel Macron cast his ballot in local elections last weekend as political chaos grips France ahead of his departure from office next year, according to Reuters correspondents covering the campaign. Senior political correspondent Michel Rose said the last two years have been "absolutely insane" and "relentless" for Macron, who cannot seek re-election after serving the maximum two terms. The political turmoil began after Macron called a snap election in summer 2024 that backfired, leading to five different prime ministers and a shift of power from the presidency to parliament. Polls show the far-right National Rally, led by Marine Le Pen and Jordan Bardella, could gain momentum from the local elections as France prepares for the 2027 presidential race.
