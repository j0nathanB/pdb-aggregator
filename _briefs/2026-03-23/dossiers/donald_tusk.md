## Top Stories

### Tusk Says Hungary Shares EU Secrets With Russia

WARSAW, Poland — Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Sunday that Hungary has long been suspected of sharing details from European Union meetings with Moscow, responding to a Washington Post report alleging Hungarian officials regularly briefed Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov. "The news that Orban's people inform Moscow about EU Council meetings with details should not surprise anyone. We have had suspicions about this for a long time," Tusk wrote on social media. The Washington Post reported that Hungary's foreign minister made calls to Russia during breaks in EU meetings to provide live updates on discussions, according to the newspaper's findings.

## International

### Polish PM Tusk criticizes president's Hungary visit plans

WARSAW, Poland — Prime Minister Donald Tusk criticized President Karol Nawrocki's planned visit to Budapest, calling it a "fatal error" that would strengthen Russian President Vladimir Putin. Tusk said Nawrocki's participation in a meeting with pro-Russian and Eurosceptic politicians would confirm "a dangerous strategy of weakening the European Union." Presidential aide Marcin Przydacz confirmed the Hungary visit but denied Nawrocki would attend the gathering of pro-Russian politicians, telling Tusk to "delete the tweet, or better yet, the account." The visit is scheduled for Monday during Polish-Hungarian Friendship Day.

### Polish PM Tusk Contradicted on Maternity Ward Closure Claims

WARSAW, Poland — Prime Minister Donald Tusk incorrectly stated that a maternity ward in Lesko remains open during a televised discussion about healthcare access, according to hospital staff and opposition lawmakers. Magdalena Dąbrowska, head of the nurses' union at Lesko hospital, told TVN24 that "the premier said an untruth, there is no maternity ward." Opposition lawmaker Marcelina Zawisza said the ward closed Jan. 1, 2026, contradicting Tusk's claim that Poland has "the densest network of maternity wards in Europe." The exchange occurred during a TVN24 program where a young activist questioned Tusk about women having to travel over 100 kilometers to give birth.

### Tusk Warns Against Polexit Risk in Presidential Campaign

WARSAW, Poland — Prime Minister Donald Tusk said presidential candidate Karol Nawrocki, along with the Law and Justice party and Confederation, want Poland to leave the European Union, according to political analyst Bartosz Rydliński. Rydliński warned that if Tusk continues making such claims about Poland's EU exit, citizens may begin to believe it. The analyst said the path from EU skepticism to actual hatred of European institutions "is really short" and cautioned against overusing polexit rhetoric during campaign season.
