## Top Stories

### Germany's Merz says won't participate in Iran war

BERLIN, Germany — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Wednesday his country will not participate in U.S.-led military operations against Iran, including efforts to secure the Strait of Hormuz. "We would have advised against pursuing this course of action as it has been pursued. Therefore, we have declared that as long as the war continues, we will not participate in ensuring freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, for example, by military means," Merz told lawmakers. Defense Minister Boris Pistorius echoed the stance, stating "This is not our war, we have not started it." However, Merz indicated Germany could consider post-conflict stabilization efforts once fighting ends.

## International

### German Chancellor Merz threatens consequences over Hungary's Ukraine aid block

BRUSSELS, Belgium — German Chancellor Friedrich Merz threatened consequences against Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orban after Hungary blocked up to 90 billion euros in EU financial aid for Ukraine at a Brussels summit Friday. EU Council President António Costa said the funds, intended to cover Ukraine's urgent financial needs through 2027 and support its defense against Russia, cannot be disbursed for the foreseeable future. Merz called Orban's continued blockade an "act of gross disloyalty" and said it would have consequences extending to negotiations over the EU's next long-term budget, where funding to Hungary could be cut or tied to stricter conditions. Merz noted that Orban had previously agreed to the Ukraine support loan at a December summit.

### Merz faces pressure to clarify CDU relationship with Greens

BERLIN, Germany — Chancellor Friedrich Merz faces mounting pressure to clarify his Christian Democratic Union's stance toward the Green Party following recent electoral developments, according to political commentary in Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung. The CDU currently governs alongside the Greens in four German states, with the partnerships showing no apparent electoral damage to the Christian Democrats, columnist Eckart Lohse wrote. Merz initially assured that recent election results in Baden-Württemberg would not affect his federal coalition with the Social Democrats, but analysts said the success of CDU-Green partnerships at the state level raises questions about future federal coalition strategies.
