# 🇧🇷 Brazil
## Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, President
_Week of March 23, 2026_

> Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva signed anti-crime legislation targeting criminal factions and announced a Chinese train factory investment this week, as his administration pursued both domestic security measures and international economic partnerships. ## Top Stories

### Lula leads first round, Flavio Bolsonaro ahead in runoff poll

SAO PAULO, Brazil — Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva leads all potential opponents in first-round scenarios for the 2026 presidential election but trails Senator Flavio Bolsonaro in a simulated runoff, according to an AtlasIntel poll released Wednesday. The poll showed Lula taking approximately 46% of the vote across five first-round scenarios, while Flavio Bolsonaro, son of imprisoned former President Jair Bolsonaro, registered between 36% and 42%. In a direct runoff matchup, Flavio Bolsonaro led with 47.6% compared to Lula's 46.6%, marking the first time the senator has appeared ahead in a second-round simulation. The poll surveyed 5,028 people between March 18-23 with a margin of error of one percentage point.

### Lula signs anti-crime law targeting criminal factions

BRASÍLIA, Brazil — President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva signed legislation Tuesday creating a legal framework to combat organized crime in Brazil, including criminal factions and militias. The law increases penalties for participation in criminal organizations from 20 to 40 years in some cases and restricts benefits like bail and parole for faction leaders, according to the presidential palace. Lula vetoed two provisions, including one the government said could have been used against social movements, said National Public Security Secretary Chico Lucas. The president said the law targets "crime magnates" living in luxury condominiums rather than "small fish from the periphery," according to his remarks at the signing ceremony.

### Lula signs land redistribution decrees for 478 families

BRASILIA, Brazil — President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva signed seven land expropriation decrees Tuesday for agrarian reform that will benefit 478 families across four states, officials said. The decrees target properties in Goiás, Maranhão, Rio Grande do Norte and Santa Catarina covering 15,400 hectares, according to Development Minister Paulo Teixeira. Lula also delivered land titles to quilombola communities, including the disputed Alcântara territory in Maranhão that had been contested with Brazil's Air Force for over 40 years. The announcements came during the opening of Brazil's National Conference on Sustainable Rural Development in Brasilia.
