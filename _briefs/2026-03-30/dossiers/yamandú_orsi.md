### Uruguay President Orsi Accepts Alternative Transit Plan Without Tunnel

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi said Tuesday that his administration will accept Montevideo's alternative proposal for a major transit reform that eliminates plans for an underground tunnel on 18 de Julio Avenue. Orsi said the Montevideo municipal government presented a surface-level Bus Rapid Transit system that would cost less and take less time to build than the original tunnel proposal. The president said the alternative plan maintains exclusive bus lanes and closed stations but acknowledged buses would travel "a little slower" than with the tunnel option. Orsi said construction would begin in 2027 as part of a $590 million metropolitan transport overhaul.

### Uruguay's Orsi drops to 13th in Latin America presidential ranking

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — Uruguayan President Yamandú Orsi fell to 13th place among 18 Latin American leaders in a March approval rating survey, according to Argentine consulting firm CB Global Data. Orsi received 40.7% positive approval, down from leading the regional ranking for several months in 2025, the firm said Tuesday. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum topped the March survey with 72.3% approval, followed by El Salvador's Nayib Bukele at 71.8%, CB Global Data said. The survey of 2,051 Uruguayans was conducted March 10-15 with a margin of error of 2.2 percentage points.

### Uruguay's Orsi presents National Security Plan after year delay

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi presented Uruguay's National Security Plan on Thursday after facing opposition criticism for delays during his first year in office. The plan includes 135 measures targeting homicides, drug trafficking, firearms, cybercrime, gender violence, crime prevention and criminal justice reform, Interior Minister Carlos Negro said. Orsi said the strategy builds on years of police work and experience from previous administrations rather than starting from zero. Opposition party leaders from the National Party, Colorado Party and Independent Party rejected invitations to receive the plan, saying their earlier proposals were not incorporated.

### Uruguay's Orsi announces solar plant crowdfunding initiative

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi announced Monday that state utility UTE will launch a crowdfunding initiative allowing small investors to finance construction of a 30-megawatt solar plant in Baygorria. Industry, Energy and Mining Minister Fernanda Cardona said the program will issue tradeable bonds with a minimum investment of $200 and no maximum limit, offering fixed annual returns with capital repayment expected within five years. Cardona said the initiative aims to advance solar energy development while capturing citizen savings for national projects, with the Baygorria plant in Durazno department being the first of potentially three planned facilities.

### Uruguay extends fuel tax cuts to 60 kilometers from borders

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi announced Monday that the government will extend fuel tax reductions at gas stations located up to 60 kilometers from all national borders, effective May 1. The measure provides a 32% discount on the Imesi tax within the first 20 kilometers from borders, reducing to 16% for the next 40 kilometers, Orsi said during a press conference following a Cabinet meeting. Orsi said the policy targets border departments with Brazil and Argentina, and announced the government will also extend employer contribution exemptions for new jobs to the Argentine border region.

### Uruguay considers UK offer for three Royal Navy patrol vessels

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — President Yamandú Orsi's government is considering with "interest" a United Kingdom offer of three offshore patrol vessels from the Royal Navy, Executive Tower sources confirmed to El Observador. The vessels are HMS Tyne, HMS Mersey and HMS Severn, first-generation River-class ships that have operated in the British navy since 2003 and are scheduled for decommissioning in 2028. Orsi said last week the purchase would be conducted "country to country" as the "most interesting" format, following the cancellation of a contract with Spanish shipyard Cardama. Each used vessel would cost around $20 million, sources said, compared to $60 million for new offshore patrol vessels.

### Spanish Shipyard Accuses Uruguay Government of Contract Manipulation

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — Spanish shipyard Cardama accused the Uruguayan government of manipulating information and acting inconsistently after President Yamandú Orsi rescinded a $92 million contract for two oceanic patrol vessels, according to a letter obtained by local media. ### Uruguay farm group challenges water restrictions opposing Orsi growth plan

MONTEVIDEO, Uruguay — Uruguay's Rural Association challenged government restrictions on agricultural inputs in the Santa Lucía basin, saying the measures contradict President Yamandú Orsi's call for economic growth. Association President Rafael Ferber said the basin generates 25% of agricultural GDP, worth $1.5 billion annually, and restrictions could reduce production by $150 million per year. The criticism targets the Casupá project's controls on nutrients and pesticides in the watershed that supplies water to the capital. Ferber said the restrictions undermine Orsi's stated need for growth to address social challenges, noting agriculture represents 78% of Uruguay's goods exports.
