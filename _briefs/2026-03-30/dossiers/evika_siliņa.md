## Top Stories

### Opposition demands Siliņa explain pressure on corruption bureau

RIGA, Latvia — Latvia's opposition party Apvienotais Saraksts demanded Prime Minister Evika Siliņa explain allegations she pressured officials to provide false information to the country's anti-corruption bureau. Former State Chancellery Director Jānis Citskovskis said in a television interview that Siliņa's office pressured him to alter correspondence with the Corruption Prevention and Combating Bureau to hide former Prime Minister Krišjānis Kariņš' role in authorizing charter flights. Siliņa dismissed Citskovskis' claims as a "defense tactic" related to ongoing criminal proceedings against him, according to Delfi Latvija.

### Latvia PM urges public transport use amid fuel price surge

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa urged Latvians to use public transportation more frequently as the government approved a temporary diesel fuel tax reduction to combat rising prices. The Cabinet agreed Tuesday to reduce excise taxes on diesel fuel, which would lower prices by about 8.6 cents per liter, pending parliamentary approval. Siliņa said the measure targets diesel specifically because it experienced the steepest price increases and is used by 85% of drivers. "Our task is to make everyone think about how much they spend. We must maximize the use of what we have — more public transport," Siliņa said at a press conference following the government session.

### Latvia PM comments on Russian drone explosion near border

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said a Russian drone exploded 13 kilometers from the border in Krāslava district after entering Latvian airspace. The National Armed Forces identified the foreign unmanned aircraft's entry from Russia, with early warning systems detecting an explosion-like sound in the area. Drone debris was found at the incident site, where military, police and border guard units responded, the armed forces said.

### Latvia PM outlines energy independence, border security priorities

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa said Latvia has systematically severed ties with Russian energy resources in recent years, including disconnecting from Russian gas and joining continental European electricity networks alongside Estonia and Lithuania. Siliņa outlined six priorities for achieving energy independence, including developing renewable energy capacity, increasing storage capabilities, and exploring nuclear energy options in coordination with Estonia. She said Latvia faces organized pressure at its border with Belarus, where the regime directs migrant flows to create political pressure, calling it a European security issue. Siliņa criticized Hungary for blocking a 90 billion euro loan to Ukraine despite previously agreeing to it politically, saying such actions are unacceptable.

## Domestic

### Latvia PM Siliņa faces criticism in annual report to parliament

RIGA, Latvia — Prime Minister Evika Siliņa presented her government's annual report to parliament March 12, facing sharp criticism from opposition lawmakers who questioned her administration's achievements. Opposition deputy Edgars Tavars criticized the government's record on environmental and transport issues, while Linda Liepiņa called the report "low quality" and lacking specifics. Even coalition member Antonina Nenaševa acknowledged the government's struggles, saying "old political instincts prevailed" with hesitation and avoidance of responsibility. Siliņa defended her administration's progress on strategic goals, telling lawmakers that necessary but unpopular changes were adopted "so that in the long term we can all live better in Latvia."

### Latvian politician doubts Siliņa will remain prime minister

RIGA, Latvia — A senior Latvian politician said he would be surprised if Prime Minister Evika Siliņa remains in office after upcoming elections in October.
