### Sheinbaum delivers 30,000 land titles to women farmers

MEXICO CITY, Mexico — President Claudia Sheinbaum said her administration has delivered 30,000 agricultural land titles to women farmers nationwide, with a goal of reaching 150,000 during her term. Speaking at a ceremony in Mexico City's Tlalpan borough Sunday, Sheinbaum said the initiative aims to guarantee women's property rights and participation in agricultural communities. "Of course we can be ejidatarias and comuneras. Have agrarian rights," Sheinbaum stated, referring to collective land ownership systems. ### Mexican Senate approves Sheinbaum budget cuts, rejects recall provision

MEXICO CITY, Mexico — Mexico's Senate approved parts of President Claudia Sheinbaum's electoral reform plan Thursday, backing budget cuts and privilege reductions while rejecting a provision to advance presidential recall elections to 2027. The measure passed 87-41, with Sheinbaum's Morena party and allies supporting budget reductions for electoral authorities and local legislatures. The Labor Party presented a reservation to eliminate changes to Article 35 that would have moved up recall vote timing, meaning any recall election for Sheinbaum would occur in 2028 as currently scheduled. Sheinbaum said Thursday that "the main part" of her Plan B was approved but called the Senate's rejection of the recall provision "bad for the country."

### Mexico president says will uphold Cuban doctor agreement

MEXICO CITY, Mexico — President Claudia Sheinbaum said Wednesday that Mexico will maintain its agreement with Cuba to have Cuban doctors working in the country, after several nearby nations ended such arrangements amid U.S. pressure. "We have a very good agreement that's also been a great help to us," Sheinbaum said during her daily morning press conference. Mexico has become a major host for Cuban medical personnel, with thousands of doctors and specialists deployed to work in under-served, rural areas since 2022. The Bahamas, Honduras, Guatemala, Jamaica and Guyana have announced plans to end agreements with Cuba under the medical mission program, which the U.S. says amounts to forced labor.

### Sheinbaum says ship caused Gulf oil spill, orders criminal probe

MEXICO CITY, Mexico — President Claudia Sheinbaum said Monday that an oil spill affecting Gulf of Mexico coasts in Tabasco and Veracruz states was caused by a ship, not state oil company Pemex, and announced she requested prosecutors investigate potential criminal charges. ### Mexico proposes federal feminicide law with 40-70 year sentences

MEXICO CITY, Mexico — President Claudia Sheinbaum announced Tuesday that her government will send legislation to Congress establishing a federal law against feminicide with prison sentences of 40 to 70 years. Attorney General Ernestina Godoy said the General Law for the Prevention, Investigation, Sanction and Reparation of the Crime of Feminicide would standardize the crime across all states and unify investigation protocols. The proposal establishes nine gender-based reasons for classifying feminicide and makes the crime imprescriptible, meaning it cannot be subject to a statute of limitations, Godoy said during the presidential press conference.
