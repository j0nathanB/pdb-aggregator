# Week of March 23, 2026

> European leaders confronted escalating Middle Eastern tensions this week as French President Emmanuel Macron called on Iran to negotiate regional disputes in good faith while joining Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni in condemning Israel's blocking of Palm Sunday mass in Jerusalem. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum delivered 30,000 land titles to women farmers and proposed federal feminicide legislation carrying sentences up to 70 years, while Brazilian President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva signed anti-crime measures targeting criminal factions. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney achieved the NATO 2% defense spending target as his first budget received royal assent, and Uruguayan President Yamandú Orsi faced declining approval ratings while accepting an alternative transit plan without a tunnel project.

### Sheinbaum delivers 30,000 land titles to women farmers
{: #sheinbaum-delivers-30-000-land-titles-to-women-farmers}

MEXICO CITY, Mexico — President Claudia Sheinbaum said her administration has delivered 30,000 agricultural land titles to women farmers nationwide, with a goal of reaching 150,000 during her term. Speaking at a ceremony in Mexico City's Tlalpan borough Sunday, Sheinbaum said the initiative aims to guarantee women's property rights and participation in agricultural communities. "Of course we can be ejidatarias and comuneras. Have agrarian rights," Sheinbaum stated, referring to collective land ownership systems. The president also defended her government as emerging from popular support rather than elite imposition, contrasting it with what she called six neoliberal administrations.

### Italy, France condemn Israel over blocked Palm Sunday Mass *(Emmanuel Macron, Giorgia Meloni)*
{: #italy-france-condemn-israel-over-blocked-palm-sunday-mass}

ROME, Italy — Italy summoned Israel's ambassador Monday after Israeli police prevented Cardinal Pierbattista Pizzaballa from celebrating Palm Sunday Mass at Jerusalem's Church of the Holy Sepulchre, with Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni calling the action an "affront" to believers. French President Emmanuel Macron condemned the decision and expressed "full support" for the Latin Patriarch, calling it part of a "worrying multiplication of violations" at Jerusalem's holy sites. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the restriction was imposed for security reasons with "absolutely no malicious intent," while President Isaac Herzog cited ongoing Iranian missile threats. The Latin Patriarchate said it marked "the first time in centuries" church leaders were prevented from celebrating Palm Sunday mass at the sacred site.

### Italian Tourism Minister Santanche Resigns After Meloni Pressure
{: #italian-tourism-minister-santanche-resigns-after-meloni-pressure}

ROME, Italy — Italian Tourism Minister Daniela Santanche resigned Wednesday after Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni publicly demanded her departure following a bruising referendum defeat. Santanche, who faces trial on false accounting charges and is under investigation for alleged benefit fraud, initially resisted Meloni's call to quit before relenting in a letter stating she was leaving "only in response to a request from the leader of my party." The resignation came as Meloni seeks to reset her government after voters rejected her judicial reforms in a March 22-23 referendum, with the prime minister reportedly saying she was "no longer prepared to cover for discredited allies." Santanche was the third government figure to leave following the referendum defeat.

### Meloni expresses regret after Italian justice reform referendum defeat
{: #meloni-expresses-regret-after-italian-justice-reform-referendum-defeat}

ROME, Italy — Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said she regretted a "missed opportunity to modernize Italy" after voters defeated her government's constitutional reform of the justice system in a referendum held Sunday and Monday. "Sovereignty belongs to the people, and Italians have expressed themselves clearly today," Meloni said, according to the ANSA news agency. She said the government fulfilled its electoral promise by proposing the reform but respected citizens' decision to reject it. Meloni previously said a referendum defeat would not affect her government, while the center-left opposition argued it would damage her political standing.
