*Items worth monitoring that didn't make the executive briefing.*

China's responses to coordinated security infrastructure development among Taiwan, Japan and South Korea could escalate regional competition significantly. The question is whether Beijing moves beyond diplomatic protests to actual military responses as alliance technology transfer patterns deepen.

Saudi Arabia and the UAE's commercial disruptions could signal something more serious than typical Gulf rivalry if they expand beyond defense sectors. Worth watching whether these tensions spill into energy, trade or financial arrangements that underpin their broader alliance architecture.

Japan, South Korea and Australia are moving toward systematic implementation of US burden-shifting demands, which could reshape alliance architecture more broadly. The key is whether Washington sets concrete spending targets, capability requirements and cost-sharing terms that go beyond current NATO guidelines.
