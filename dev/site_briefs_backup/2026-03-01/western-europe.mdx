## Regional Summary

The US-Israeli strikes on Iran split European allies. Norway condemned the strikes as breaches of international law while Italy backed Washington despite threats to Italian forces. France, shut out of planning, demanded UN Security Council meetings. Britain let Washington use its bases for defense but not attack. Sweden called its National Security Council into session. Unlike their unified response to Russian aggression, European countries put national interests first when Washington acted without asking them.

This marks a shift from automatic support to making their own calls. Each country interpreted its obligations differently, showing growing European confidence in going alone when it disagrees with American strategy. Even governments that usually follow Washington's lead split off.

Even as governments handled alliance tensions, domestic politics fell apart across the region. Five countries saw governing party crises this week: Macron's disapproval hit a historic high of 77%, Britain's Labour lost a by-election so badly it fell to third place, Italy's coalition faced calls for resignations over the Crosetto Dubai affair, Spain's governing coalition lost key leader Yolanda Díaz, and Sweden's Tidö coalition faced pre-election pressure and sliding polls. This chaos spans different political systems and governing styles.

The domestic weakness suggests deeper forces hitting European governing parties beyond individual national troubles. Whether driven by post-pandemic exhaustion with government, economic stress, or simple anti-government mood, the pattern points toward election upheaval that could reshape regional politics and alliance reliability. Alliance stress plus domestic weakness leaves European governments squeezed from both sides—weak at home, divided abroad.


Notably absent this week: EU coordination on the Iran crisis response. Brussels offered no collective position as member states took divergent national stances, suggesting either institutional limits for rapid crisis response or deliberate preference for national positioning over EU unity.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/fr.svg" alt="France flag" style={{ width: "24px", height: "18px", display: "inline", verticalAlign: "middle", marginRight: "8px" }} /> France

France is becoming Europe's nuclear protector in the biggest shift since Charles de Gaulle founded the Fifth Republic. Emmanuel Macron, the president, will outline the country's role in European nuclear defence in a speech on March 2nd at the Île Longue submarine base, following talks with Friedrich Merz, the German chancellor, about nuclear cooperation. This follows doubts about American reliability under Donald Trump.

France is clashing more with Washington. Charles Kushner, the US ambassador, refused to come to the Foreign Ministry after embassy comments about the death of far-right militant Quentin Deranque. France temporarily barred him from direct government access. When America and Israel struck Iran this week, killing Supreme Leader Khamenei, they did not tell France or ask for help. Mr Macron called emergency defence councils and called for a UN Security Council meeting.

Mr Macron faces a domestic crisis. His approval ratings hit a record low, with 77% of French citizens judging him negatively. Only 23% consider him a good president — a sustained slump since the June 2024 dissolution.

The opposition is preparing for 2027. Marine Le Pen said she would not campaign for president if sentenced to wear an electronic bracelet. The July 7th appeal verdict will decide whether millions who want to vote for her can do so. She and Jordan Bardella made a rare joint appearance at the Agricultural Salon, showing unity as Mr Bardella emerges as her likely successor.

France is building the military muscle to match its ambitions. The country's forces produced 1,000 military drones in under a year at less than 1,000 euros each, adapting rapidly to lessons from Ukraine.

On economics, France remains defensive. Mr Macron attacked Ursula von der Leyen's decision to apply the EU-Mercosur trade agreement provisionally, calling it an "unacceptable unilateral choice." Both the National Rally and France Unbowed filed censure motions against the government over its energy strategy, but both failed by wide margins.
