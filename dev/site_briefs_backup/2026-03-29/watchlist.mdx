## Items on Watch

China's potential military response to alliance security coordination poses the biggest near-term risk. Beijing has so far avoided direct confrontation as Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and their partners deepen infrastructure links and technology transfers. But Chinese military responses to this alliance-building could escalate regional tensions and test how well these partnerships hold under pressure.

That coordination may face a different test from Washington itself. The United States is moving toward systematic burden-shifting across its Asia-Pacific partnerships, pressing Japan, South Korea, and Australia to shoulder more costs and capabilities. If this shifts from informal pressure to specific spending targets and formal cost-sharing arrangements, it could fracture alliances at the worst possible moment.

Meanwhile, democratic governments from France to Australia are being watched for signs they might coordinate their responses to domestic governance stress. France, Britain, Italy, Canada, and Australia all face similar pressures on democratic institutions. Evidence of shared responses or coordination mechanisms would signal either growing resilience or accelerating crisis—but so far, each country is managing its troubles alone.
