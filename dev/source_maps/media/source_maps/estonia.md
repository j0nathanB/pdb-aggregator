# Source Intelligence Map: Estonia

## Media Landscape Summary

Estonia's media market is small, highly digitized, and dominated by two private groups — Postimees Grupp (Postimees, Postimees na Russkom) and Ekspress Grupp (Delfi, Eesti Ekspress, Eesti Paevaleht, Maaleht) — alongside the public broadcaster ERR. Print circulation has declined sharply, with Aripäev (business daily) going fully digital in late 2022 and Ohtuleht reducing print to five days per week. The Russian-speaking minority (~25% of the population) consumes a distinct information ecosystem; national Russian-language print dailies have disappeared, but Russian-language Delfi, ERR's Russian service (now reorganized as ETV+), and social media fill the gap. Estonia's media is notable for its high internet penetration and the security establishment's active engagement with disinformation monitoring (e.g., the Propastop blog run by Defence League volunteers).

## Notable Exclusions

- **Objektiiv** (objektiiv.ee): Conservative-Christian outlet; has drawn criticism for sharing content from unreliable sources (InfoWars, Breitbart); not reliable enough for an operational pipeline.
- **Postimees na Russkom** (online): Russian-language Postimees web content exists but has been significantly scaled back; monitoring rus.delfi.ee and rus.err.ee provides better coverage.
- **Telegram channels**: Significant for Russian-speaking audience information consumption but outside the scope of curated traditional-media whitelisting.

## Coverage Gap Assessment

The recommended set provides robust coverage across all domains, with particular strength in security/defense thanks to ICDS, Diplomaatia, and Propastop. The main gap is **independent Russian-language investigative journalism** — since the closure of Russian-language print dailies, no independent Russian-language investigative outlet operates from within Estonia, creating a blind spot for detecting how Kremlin narratives penetrate the Russophone community. A secondary gap is **defense-industrial procurement reporting**, which is scattered across Aripäev and ERR rather than concentrated in a specialist outlet.
