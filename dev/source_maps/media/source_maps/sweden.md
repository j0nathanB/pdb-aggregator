# Source Intelligence Map: Sweden

## Media Landscape Summary

Sweden's media landscape is dominated by two ownership groups — Bonnier (Dagens Nyheter, Expressen, Dagens Industri) and Schibsted (Aftonbladet, Svenska Dagbladet, Omni, and soon TV4 Media following the 2025 acquisition). Public service broadcasters SVT, SR, and UR remain the most-used news sources across the political spectrum. The editorial pages of major dailies carry explicit ideological labels ("independently liberal" for DN, "independently moderate/conservative" for SvD, "independent social-democratic" for Aftonbladet), creating a structured left-right media ecosystem that maps neatly onto coalition politics. Sweden has approximately 150 printed papers, nearly all with online editions, and the digital transition is advanced with streaming TV now outpacing linear broadcast.

## Notable Exclusions

- **ETC** (etc.se): Left-wing daily; valuable for tracking Green/Left critique but narrow readership and limited foreign-policy depth.
- **Nya Dagbladet / Samnytt**: Right-populist/alternative media; relevant for domestic radicalization monitoring but outside the scope of strategic-posture analysis.
- **The Local Sweden** (thelocal.se): English-language expatriate outlet; lacks the depth and sourcing for operational OSINT.
- **Arbetet** (arbetet.se): LO trade-union news outlet; useful for labor-market policy but redundant with Aftonbladet's editorial line for foreign/security coverage.

## Coverage Gap Assessment

The recommended set is strong across all five domains. The primary gap is **dedicated defense-trade journalism** — Sweden has a large defense-export sector (Saab, BAE Hagglunds, etc.) but no standalone defense-trade publication; DI and FOI together approximate this coverage. A secondary gap is **Sami and minority-language media**, which occasionally surface Arctic sovereignty and indigenous-rights issues relevant to Nordic defense cooperation but are too niche for systematic monitoring.
