# Source Intelligence Map: Latvia

## Media Landscape Summary

Latvia's media landscape is shaped by linguistic segmentation (Latvian vs. Russian), concentrated private ownership with opaque structures, and a recently reformed public broadcaster. In 2025, Latvian Television and Latvian Radio were merged into a single entity — Latvian Public Media (LSM) — which now serves as the primary publicly funded news platform. Private media is concentrated among a few groups: Ekspress Grupp (Estonia) owns TVNET (second-most-visited portal), while Delfi Latvia leads online readership. Print dailies Diena and Neatkariga Rita Avize (NRA) have histories of oligarchic ownership that have affected editorial trust. The investigative sector is anchored by Re:Baltica, Latvia's only dedicated investigative journalism center. A significant policy shift occurred with the planned ban on public media Russian-language content from January 2026, further fragmenting the Russophone information space.

## Notable Exclusions

- **Pietiek.com**: Anonymously authored political commentary blog; popular but unverifiable sourcing makes it unsuitable for an operational pipeline.
- **Russian-language Telegram channels**: Significant for Russophone audience monitoring post-2026 public-media ban, but outside the scope of curated traditional-media whitelisting.
- **Chas**: Previously significant Russian-language daily; now marginal.
- **Apollo.lv**: News aggregator; duplicates Delfi/TVNET without original reporting.

## Coverage Gap Assessment

The set provides good coverage but has a notable vulnerability: the **Russian-language information space** is becoming increasingly difficult to monitor through legitimate media following the 2026 ban on public-media Russian-language content and the earlier closure of Russian state TV. Analysts should consider supplementing with rus.delfi.lv and social-media monitoring tools. A secondary gap is **defense-industrial reporting** — Latvia's defense spending is surging (4.9% GDP in 2026) but no specialist defense-procurement publication exists; mod.gov.lv press releases and IR/Re:Baltica investigations partially fill this gap.
