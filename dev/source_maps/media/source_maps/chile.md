# Source Intelligence Map: CHILE

**Date produced:** 2026-03-13
**Primary language of political discourse:** Spanish
**Current head of state:** José Antonio Kast (inaugurated 2026-03-11)

---

## MEDIA LANDSCAPE SUMMARY

Chile's media ecosystem is dominated by two legacy conglomerates — the Edwards family's El Mercurio group and the Copesa group (La Tercera) — which together control roughly 90% of print circulation and a large share of digital readership. This duopoly skews center-right to right in editorial orientation, making independent digital outlets (CIPER, El Mostrador, Interferencia, Ex-Ante) essential for triangulation. Broadcast news remains influential through Radio Cooperativa and Radio Bío-Bío (the two highest-credibility radio networks) and television portals like T13. Defense and security coverage is thin domestically; the Spanish-language trade press (Infodefensa, Defensa.com) and the Santiago-based think tank AthenaLab fill much of the gap. Chile's March 2026 transition from Boric (left) to Kast (right) — amid a live US-China rivalry over critical minerals and undersea cable infrastructure — makes diplomatic-alignment and economic-statecraft coverage especially dynamic at this moment.

## NOTABLE EXCLUSIONS

1. **El Líbero** (`ellibero.cl`) — Right-wing digital outlet with opinion-heavy content aligned with Kast's political base. Excluded because its signal is largely duplicative of El Mercurio editorial positions, with lower factual-reporting standards. Monitor if tracking intra-right coalition dynamics becomes necessary.

2. **The Clinic** — Satirical and cultural-political magazine historically associated with the center-left. Excluded because its political content is predominantly commentary/opinion rather than reportage, and its foreign-policy coverage is minimal. El Mostrador and Interferencia cover the same political space with more analytical depth.

3. **CNN Chile** (now under new ownership by Jorge Carey) — Television news channel. Excluded due to editorial-direction uncertainty following its 2025 ownership change, limited text-based output for pipeline ingestion, and redundancy with T13 for broadcast-origin political coverage.

4. **Diario Estrategia** (`diarioestrategia.cl`) — Business daily. Excluded as Diario Financiero provides superior coverage of the same economic-statecraft domain with broader reach and deeper sourcing.

5. **TVN (Televisión Nacional de Chile)** — Public broadcaster. Excluded because it lacks permanent state funding and its online news output is thinner and less structured than T13 or Cooperativa for pipeline purposes, despite its nominal public-service mandate.

## COVERAGE GAP ASSESSMENT

The most significant gap is **dedicated Chilean military/defense journalism** — no domestic outlet maintains a specialized defense beat, forcing reliance on Spanish trade press (Infodefensa) and think-tank output (AthenaLab/ANEPE) for procurement and doctrine signals. A secondary gap exists in **multilateral-institution coverage**: Chile's positions in UN votes, OAS proceedings, and Pacific Alliance negotiations are rarely reported in real time by domestic media, requiring supplementation with official Cancillería releases and wire services. Finally, **Chinese-language sourcing on China-Chile relations** (e.g., Xinhua Santiago, People's Daily coverage of Chilean bilateral meetings) would strengthen detection of Beijing's framing of the relationship but falls outside the Spanish/English pipeline scope.
