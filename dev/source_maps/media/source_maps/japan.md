# Source Intelligence Map: JAPAN

**Date:** 2026-03-13
**Primary language of political discourse:** Japanese (日本語)

---

## MEDIA LANDSCAPE SUMMARY

Japan's media ecosystem is anchored by five national dailies (Yomiuri, Asahi, Mainichi, Nikkei, Sankei) and two wire services (Kyodo News, Jiji Press), supplemented by public broadcaster NHK — all of which maintain English-language output of varying depth. The press landscape exhibits a left-right spectrum on security and constitutional matters: Asahi and Tokyo Shimbun oppose defense expansion and constitutional revision, while Yomiuri and Sankei support the US alliance and a stronger military posture, making cross-spectrum monitoring essential for detecting genuine consensus shifts versus factional framing. English-language access has improved significantly — Nikkei Asia, The Japan Times, NHK World, Kyodo's "Japan Wire," and The Japan News (Yomiuri) all provide substantive daily coverage — though the deepest political reporting (Diet debates, party faction dynamics, bureaucratic maneuvering) remains Japanese-language only. Think tank output from NIDS (Ministry of Defense), JIIA (MOFA-affiliated), and the Sasakawa Peace Foundation provides semi-official strategic signaling in English. Government primary sources (Kantei, MOFA, MOD) publish press conference transcripts and policy documents in English with short delays.

## NOTABLE EXCLUSIONS

1. **Mainichi Shimbun (毎日新聞)**: Center-left daily that would add editorial diversity, but its English edition ("The Mainichi") has limited output and sits behind a paywall. Its editorial niche is substantially covered by the combination of Asahi (center-left domestic reporting) and Tokyo Shimbun (progressive opposition perspective). Include if budget permits subscription.

2. **Fuji News Network / FNN (フジテレビ)**: Conservative broadcast outlet aligned with Sankei Shimbun via the Fujisankei Group. Excluded because JAPAN Forward already captures the Sankei-adjacent perspective in English, and FNN's substantive political reporting does not materially differ from Sankei's editorial line.

3. **Toyo Keizai Online (東洋経済オンライン)**: Strong Japanese-language business journalism with occasional security/industrial policy scoops. Excluded from the minimum set because Nikkei (both Japanese and Asia editions) covers the economic-statecraft domain more comprehensively. Worth adding for deeper economic monitoring.

4. **CSIS (Washington)**: Publishes extensively on Japan but is a US-based think tank reflecting Washington's analytical frame rather than Japan's own strategic signaling. Useful as a complementary external source but not a primary indicator of Japanese posture.

5. **Japan Today (japantoday.com)**: English-language aggregator popular with expatriates but relies primarily on wire service content (Kyodo, Jiji, AP) already captured in the recommended set. Minimal original reporting.

## COVERAGE GAP ASSESSMENT

The recommended set covers the full left-right editorial spectrum of Japan's print/broadcast landscape and all major government primary sources in both English and Japanese. The primary gap is in **real-time Diet proceedings** — committee debates and interpellations where opposition parties probe government security and diplomatic positions are not systematically translated into English and require Japanese-language monitoring of NHK parliamentary broadcasts or the House of Representatives/Councillors websites (shugiin.go.jp / sangiin.go.jp). A secondary gap exists in **social media and informal political discourse** — platforms like X (formerly Twitter) are heavily used by Japanese politicians and commentators for policy signaling, but this requires dedicated social media monitoring tools rather than traditional source subscriptions. Finally, **sub-national and Okinawa-specific defense politics** (base relocation, Status of Forces Agreement friction) are best covered by regional outlets like the Okinawa Times and Ryukyu Shimpo, which publish only in Japanese and are not included in the minimum set.
