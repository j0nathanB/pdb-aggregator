# Source Intelligence Map: INDIA

---

## MEDIA LANDSCAPE SUMMARY

India's media ecosystem is sharply bifurcated between an English-language press that dominates foreign policy and strategic discourse -- read by policymakers, diplomats, and the urban professional class -- and a far larger Hindi/regional-language press (Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, etc.) that shapes mass political opinion but covers international affairs only episodically and through a domestic lens. Ownership concentration is severe: Reliance Industries (via Network18/TV18) controls over 70 television channels reaching ~800 million viewers; the Adani Group acquired NDTV in late 2022; and four Hindi dailies (Dainik Jagran, Hindustan, Amar Ujala, Dainik Bhaskar) capture 76% of Hindi-language readership. This conglomerate-political nexus means that genuinely independent outlets are increasingly confined to digital-native platforms (The Wire, Scroll.in, ThePrint) and a small number of legacy broadsheets (The Hindu, The Indian Express) that have resisted corporate capture. For OSINT purposes, English-language sources provide the richest analytical layer on strategic posture, but Hindi-language outlets and parliamentary proceedings are essential for detecting domestic political constraints that shape or block external action.

## NOTABLE EXCLUSIONS

**NDTV (ndtv.com)** -- Once India's premier independent English-language broadcaster, NDTV was acquired by the Adani Group in December 2022 (64.7% stake). The departure of senior journalists (Sreenivasan Jain, Nidhi Razdan, Ravish Kumar) and editorial realignment make it unreliable as an independent analytical source. Its coverage now largely mirrors government positions on strategic matters.

**Scroll.in (scroll.in)** -- A well-regarded digital outlet with left-center orientation, but its foreign policy and defense coverage lacks the depth and beat-reporter sourcing of ThePrint or The Hindu. Its strength is in domestic rights and governance reporting, which is already covered by The Wire and EPW. Additionally, its reliance on external funding (Omidyar Network) and a failed fact-check have raised credibility concerns.

**Republic TV / Times Now / India Today TV** -- Television news channels that dominate viewership but produce polemical, entertainment-driven coverage of international affairs. Their strategic affairs coverage is performative rather than analytical and would generate noise rather than signal in an automated pipeline.

**OpIndia / Swarajya** -- Right-wing digital outlets that reflect Hindutva nationalist perspectives on foreign policy. While they represent a politically significant viewpoint, their coverage is derivative (commentary on reporting by others) rather than original, and the ideological signal is better captured via Dainik Jagran editorials and ORF publications.

**Firstpost / News18 (Network18 / Reliance)** -- Reliance-owned digital properties with significant reach but compromised editorial independence due to corporate-political alignment. Strategic affairs coverage tracks government messaging closely without adding independent analytical value.

## COVERAGE GAP ASSESSMENT

This source set has two notable gaps. First, **regional-language press in southern and northeastern India** (Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Bengali, Assamese) is absent; these outlets would be needed to detect subnational resistance to defense installations, port access agreements, or connectivity projects (e.g., opposition in Tamil Nadu to Sri Lanka policy, or in the Northeast to Myanmar border management). Second, **classified or semi-restricted government documents** -- such as Parliamentary Standing Committee reports on defense and external affairs, which are technically tabled but not always digitized promptly -- represent a significant analytical layer that requires manual acquisition rather than automated scraping.
