# Source Intelligence Map: TAIWAN

**Date produced:** 2026-03-13
**Primary languages:** Mandarin Chinese (Traditional), Taiwanese Hokkien
**Analyst note:** Taiwan uses Traditional Chinese characters (繁體字), not Simplified. All query vocabulary below uses Traditional script.

---

## MEDIA LANDSCAPE SUMMARY

Taiwan's media environment is one of the freest in Asia (ranked 1st in Asia by RSF), but is deeply polarized along the pan-green (pro-sovereignty, DPP-aligned) and pan-blue (pro-engagement with PRC, KMT-aligned) editorial axis, which maps directly onto strategic posture debates. The most significant structural concern for OSINT purposes is PRC influence channeled through the Want Want Group, whose China Times media conglomerate has been documented receiving editorial direction from Beijing's Taiwan Affairs Office — making it simultaneously a useful barometer of PRC-preferred narratives and an unreliable factual source on cross-strait matters. Print circulation is in sharp decline; the dominant consumption channels are now online portals (Yahoo News, ETtoday, LINE Today) and YouTube, with television (TVBS, SET News, FTV) remaining the second-most-used medium. Trusted, editorially independent outlets with strong analytical depth — CommonWealth Magazine, Business Weekly, The Reporter, and the public broadcaster PTS — tend to have smaller audiences but disproportionate influence among policymakers, making them essential for detecting posture shifts. English-language coverage is thin but serviceable through Focus Taiwan (CNA), Taipei Times, and a handful of bilingual digital outlets and think-tank publications.

## NOTABLE EXCLUSIONS

| Outlet | Reason for Exclusion |
|---|---|
| **CTiTV (中天新聞)** | Had its broadcast license revoked by NCC in 2020 for repeated regulatory violations and documented PRC influence. Now operates only on YouTube. While still a vector for PRC-aligned narratives, its deplatforming and lack of journalistic standards make China Times a more useful proxy for the same editorial line. |
| **ETtoday (東森新媒體)** | Extremely high traffic portal but dominated by clickbait, celebrity news, and sensationalism. Low signal-to-noise ratio for strategic analysis despite large audience. Not useful for posture detection. |
| **Apple Daily Taiwan** | Ceased operations in Taiwan in 2021 following the Hong Kong parent's closure. Former centrist anchor of the media landscape no longer available. |
| **Public Television Service (PTS / 公視)** | Highly trusted but extremely small audience and limited original reporting on defense/diplomacy. Its content on strategic topics is largely drawn from CNA wire. Ranked among most trusted but not a primary generator of posture-relevant content. |
| **Mirror Media (鏡週刊)** | Investigative tabloid with some useful scoops on political scandals and defense procurement but inconsistent coverage of strategic domains and tabloid framing reduces analytical utility compared to The Reporter. |

## COVERAGE GAP ASSESSMENT

The recommended source set provides strong coverage of diplomatic signaling, defense procurement/posture, and domestic partisan constraints, but has a structural gap in **real-time Taiwanese Hokkien-language discourse** — political talk shows on FTV and SET broadcast in Hokkien carry significant grassroots sentiment signals that do not reliably surface in text-based sources. A second gap exists in **semiconductor/tech-statecraft granularity**: while CommonWealth covers policy-level developments, tracking TSMC supply-chain decisions, export control compliance, and fab-location diplomacy at operational tempo requires supplementation with industry-specific sources (e.g., DigiTimes at digitimes.com, SEMI Taiwan reports) that sit outside the political-media landscape mapped here. Finally, **local-level political dynamics** — county/city government positioning on military base expansion, port access agreements, and civil defense exercises — are underrepresented in national outlets and may require monitoring regional newspapers or council proceedings.
