## Recurring / Cross-Pass Failures

These failures appear across multiple passes and represent the most important structural deficiencies:

### 1. Confidence Markers Absent Entirely in Pass 1 (Cross-cutting #5)
Pass 1 contains zero uses of [HIGH CONFIDENCE], [MODERATE CONFIDENCE], or [ASSESSED]. Passes 2 and 3 use these well (Pass 2 in STRUCTURAL CLAIMS blocks; Pass 3 in Section 18). The omission in Pass 1 is complete.

### 2. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS Blocks Absent in Pass 1 (All 8 sections) and Pass 3 (Sections 13–17) (Cross-cutting #9)
This is the single largest gap by ask count — 13 sections with no STRUCTURAL CLAIMS block. Pass 2 handles this correctly for all five of its sections. The STRUCTURAL CLAIMS blocks in Pass 2 are well-executed and could serve as a template.

### 3. No Citations in Pass 1 (Cross-cutting #17, #18, #19, #20)
Zero footnotes in Pass 1. Pass 2 has 31 footnotes and Pass 3 has 66 footnotes. The contrast is stark. The factual claims in Pass 1 are strong and specific but entirely unverified.

### 4. Russia Axis Absent in All Three Passes (Cross-cutting #11)
The rubric's four analytical questions include a "Russia axis" — South Korea's structural relationship to Russia (arms embargo compliance, DPRK-Russia military cooperation, Korean troops in Ukraine context, energy pipeline politics) is mentioned in Pass 1 Section 0 (10,000-12,000 DPRK troops in Ukraine) but never systematically analyzed. Absent from Pass 2 entirely. Absent from Pass 3's Section 14 where it most belongs.

### 5. US Retrenchment Not Formalized as Structural Claim #0 (Cross-cutting #10)
The rubric specifies this as the baseline structural claim of every section. US retrenchment is woven through the dossier analytically but is never numbered as Claim #0 in any pass.

### 6. No Formal ## Sources Section Header (Cross-cutting #18)
Passes 2 and 3 have correctly formatted footnotes but no "## Sources" heading. A structural omission in both passes.
