## Pass-Level Patterns

### Pass 1 (Sections 0-7) — Average: ~80%
Consistently the strongest pass across all countries. These foundational sections (actors, formation trauma, geography, imperial legacy, cleavages, demographics, economics, infrastructure) are generally well-addressed.

### Pass 2 (Sections 8-12) — Average: ~72%
Moderate performance. Environmental, illicit networks, media, institutional commitments, and military sections are generally solid on content but weaker on formatting compliance.

### Pass 3 (Sections 13-20) — Average: ~58%
Consistently the weakest pass. The drop is driven by two factors:
1. **Sections 17-20 are frequently merged or contain wrong content** — these four structured-output sections (Cross-Facet Analysis, Analytical Judgments, Watch Indicators, Pipeline Notes) have the most prescriptive format requirements and are most often violated
2. **Citation and structural apparatus drops off** — even countries with good Pass 1 compliance often lose citations and structural claims blocks by Pass 3

## Universal Systemic Failures

These failures appear in the vast majority of dossiers:

### 1. Missing STRUCTURAL CLAIMS Blocks
**Affected:** ~24 of 28 countries (exceptions: Brazil Pass 1, Estonia Pass 1, Germany Pass 1, a few others)
Every section is required to conclude with numbered, falsifiable structural claims. This is absent in most sections across most dossiers.

### 2. Zero or Insufficient Citations
**Affected:** ~25 of 28 countries
The rubric requires 15-25 footnote citations per pass using `[^N]` syntax with a `## Sources` section. Most dossiers have zero citations, especially in Passes 2 and 3. Notable exceptions: Germany Pass 1 (37+ footnotes), Estonia Pass 1 (86 footnotes), Brazil Pass 1 (52 footnotes).

### 3. Missing Confidence Markers
**Affected:** ~23 of 28 countries
`[HIGH CONFIDENCE]`, `[MODERATE CONFIDENCE]`, and `[ASSESSED]` markers are required where evidence is thin or contested. Most dossiers never use them.

### 4. Sections 17-20 Merged or Wrong Content
**Affected:** ~12 of 28 countries
The prompt explicitly states: "Each section must be produced as a separate, individually headed section... 'Sections 17-20' is never acceptable." Yet Germany, Lithuania, Poland, Spain, UAE, Ukraine merge them. Latvia, Sweden, Finland, and others have wrong content in these sections despite separate headers.

### 5. US Retrenchment Framework Not Applied
**Affected:** ~20 of 28 countries
The four analytical questions (Exposure, Adaptation, Constraint, Russia axis) and Structural Claim #0 (US retrenchment as baseline) are rarely systematically applied.

## Sections 17-20: The Critical Gap

These four sections are the most operationally important for the downstream LLM pipeline, yet they are the most frequently failed:

| Section | Purpose | Common Failure |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| 17: Cross-Facet Intersection | Identify non-obvious structural interactions | Replaced with bilateral analysis or narrative conclusion |
| 18: Key Analytical Judgments | 5-7 falsifiable, structured judgments | Generic summary instead of falsifiable claims with thresholds |
| 19: Watch Indicators | 8-12 concrete observables with observation channels | Unstructured prose instead of Leading/Confirming format |
| 20: Pipeline Integration Notes | Source rules, misreading patterns, bilateral thresholds | Narrative conclusion instead of structured operational guidance |

**Recommendation:** These sections need explicit re-prompting or a dedicated generation pass. The format requirements are highly specific and consistently missed, suggesting the LLM either runs out of context/output budget or deprioritizes structured formatting in favor of analytical prose.

## Content Quality Note

A consistent finding across all evaluations: **analytical content quality is generally high**. The dossiers are specific, causally reasoned, factually dense, and current through early 2026. The failures are overwhelmingly in **structural/formatting compliance** — missing citations, missing structural claims blocks, missing confidence markers, and wrong section formats — rather than in analytical substance. This suggests the prompts are effective at eliciting good analysis but the structural apparatus requirements need stronger enforcement, potentially through post-generation validation or dedicated formatting passes.
