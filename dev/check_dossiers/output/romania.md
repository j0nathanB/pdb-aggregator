## Key Gaps — Priority Ranked

### Gap 1: Citation system entirely absent (all three passes)
Cross-cutting asks 17, 18, 19, and 20 all fail across all three passes. No Markdown footnote citations ([^N]) appear anywhere. No ## Sources section exists in any pass. The minimum 15–25 citations per pass requirement is unmet with a count of zero. This is the most systemically repeated failure — 12 total ask failures (4 asks × 3 passes) from this single gap.

### Gap 2: STRUCTURAL CLAIMS block absent from all sections (all three passes)
Cross-cutting ask 9 fails in every single section across all three passes. The required per-section closing block of numbered, discrete, falsifiable assertions is entirely absent. Each pass ends with a prose "Conclusion" or simply closes, which does not satisfy the requirement.

### Gap 3: Section 17 is entirely the wrong content (Pass 3)
Section 17 was supposed to provide cross-facet structural intersection analysis (at least five intersections, with interacting facets named, mechanism explained, historical example provided, and pipeline operational instruction). Instead, it provides bilateral defense relationship analysis that would belong in Section 14. All 8 asks for Section 17 fail — the single largest section-level failure in the dossier.

### Gap 4: Confidence markers absent from Passes 2 and 3
Cross-cutting ask 5 is fully addressed in Pass 1 (73 instances of [HIGH CONFIDENCE] / [MODERATE CONFIDENCE] / [ASSESSED]), but both Pass 2 and Pass 3 contain zero confidence markers. This is inconsistent and degrades the analytical calibration signal in sections covering illicit networks, information ecosystem, institutional commitments, civil society, constitutional crises, and all synthesis sections.

### Gap 5: Sections 18, 19, 20 do not use required structured formats (Pass 3)
These three synthesis/integration sections all fail to implement the rubric's required output formats:
- **Section 18** needs: claim + structural basis (citing sections) + falsification conditions + confidence level with evidential basis. Gets: prose paragraphs with 4 judgments (minimum 5) and no falsification thresholds.
- **Section 19** needs: Leading/Confirming categorization; five-field format (Indicator / Relates to / Observation channel / Trigger threshold / Structural implication); 8–12 indicators. Gets: 6 prose paragraphs with no categorization or structured fields.
- **Section 20** needs: 5+ conditional source interpretation rules in specified format; event significance threshold examples (3–5 structural continuity + 3–5 genuine deviation); 3–5 misreading patterns; LLM-specific recency bias, Western-framework, and false-equivalence guidance; bilateral void-filling dynamic assessment. Gets: 5 thematic observations covering none of the required structured components.

### Gap 6: US retrenchment not formalized as Structural Claim #0 (all passes)
Cross-cutting ask 10 requires treating US retrenchment as the baseline operating environment and formalizing it as Structural Claim #0. The US troop reduction from 1,700 to 1,000 in October 2025 is mentioned in Pass 3 Section 14, but it is never elevated to the structural baseline status required. This affects all three passes.

### Gap 7: Section 14 — pivot option feasibility analysis missing (Pass 3)
Asks 9 and 10 require explicit analysis of realistic pivot options with feasibility testing (capability, willingness, price, infrastructure, precedent) and distinction between options debated in policy circles vs. those surviving rigorous analysis. The section correctly characterizes Romania as firmly aligned with no hedging but does not analyze what a pivot would entail or test the feasibility of hypothetical alternatives. Ask 14 (observable falsification threshold) is also absent.

### Gap 8: Section 6 — dollar debt and IMF gaps (Pass 1)
Dollar-denominated debt vulnerability (ask 12) and current IMF program conditionality (ask 13) are not addressed. Romania has no active IMF program, but this should be stated explicitly rather than omitted.

### Gap 9: Section 7 — digital and energy supply chain gaps (Pass 1)
Submarine cable vulnerability, electricity grid interdependence with neighbors for stability, and renewable energy supply chain dependencies (critical minerals, Chinese panel manufacturing concentration) are all unaddressed.

### Gap 10: Section 15 — succession and confidence calibration gaps (Pass 3)
Asks 9 (whether Dan built a system that survives his departure), 10 (confidence calibration between institutional observables and behavioral inferences), and 14 (behavioral inferences supported by two episodes, tagged as such) are not met.

## What the Dossier Does Well

The Romania dossier demonstrates exceptional factual depth and specificity throughout all three passes. Quantitative claims are precise and extensive. The institutional actor mapping in Section 0 is comprehensive and functionally differentiated. The formation trauma analysis (Section 1) handles moral complexity around the Holocaust, Antonescu legacy, and contested 1989 revolution with analytical precision. The cleavage analysis (Section 4) is the strongest in the dossier, correctly identifying urban-rural as the dominant active cleavage and tracing its causal mechanisms through media consumption, regional economic disparity, and diaspora realignment. The environmental section (Section 8) is exemplary in tracing causal chains from physical constraints to political consequences. The collective memory section (Section 16) is thorough and correctly maps narrative-to-party alignments. The civil society section (Section 13) provides operationally useful infrastructure identification. The writing throughout is analytical rather than encyclopedic, and Pass 1's extensive use of confidence markers is a model for the other passes to follow.
