## Systematic Failures Across All Three Passes

The following deficiencies appear in every pass without exception:

### 1. Zero Citations (CC-17, CC-18, CC-19, CC-20) — ALL THREE PASSES FAIL
Not a single footnote citation ([^N] syntax) appears anywhere in any pass. The rubric requires 15–25 citations per pass minimum. Zero are present. No Sources section exists. This is the most consequential systematic failure — it makes all factual claims unverifiable and prevents primary/institutional source prioritization assessment.

### 2. No STRUCTURAL CLAIMS Blocks (CC-9) — PASS 2 AND PASS 3 FAIL
Pass 1 has STRUCTURAL CLAIMS blocks in all sections. Passes 2 and 3 have none. Each section is supposed to end with numbered, discrete, falsifiable assertions. The substituted general Conclusion paragraphs do not meet this requirement.

### 3. No Confidence Markers (CC-5) — PASS 2 AND PASS 3 FAIL
Pass 1 uses confidence markers 71 times. Passes 2 and 3 use them zero times. The rubric requires [HIGH CONFIDENCE], [MODERATE CONFIDENCE], and [ASSESSED] throughout, especially where evidence is thin or contested and where behavioral inferences are made.

## Key Gaps by Severity

### Critical (Complete Failures)

**Sections 17–20 gutted by merger (Pass 3)**
Merging sections 17–20 into a general political narrative eliminated all 33 asks for Cross-Facet Intersection Analysis, Key Analytical Judgments, Watch Indicators, and Pipeline Integration Notes. This accounts for the majority of Pass 3's failure. None of the following are present: named cross-facet intersections with mechanistic analysis, structured falsifiable judgments in required format, 8–12 concrete watch indicators organized into leading/confirming categories, source interpretation conditional rules, event significance thresholds, pipeline LLM guidance, or structured bilateral relationship analysis. Sections 17–20 constitute the most operationally useful output for a monitoring pipeline, and their complete absence is the largest single gap in the dossier.

**Citation system absent (all passes)**
Without footnotes, the dossier cannot function as a source-anchored reference document. The downstream pipeline cannot distinguish established fact from inference, identify which sources to query, or calibrate confidence by source quality.

### High Priority

**Section 15 analytical depth (Pass 3)**
Section 15 requires the deepest analytical apparatus of Pass 3 — behavioral inference documentation, endogenous/exogenous trigger distinction, institutional vs. observable claim separation, current regime phase assessment. Only 5 of 14 asks are fully addressed. Key missing items: confidence marking of behavioral inferences with two supporting episodes; identification of endogenous vs. exogenous crisis triggers; systematic democratic erosion stage assessment for each mechanism.

**Section 14 pivot feasibility (Pass 3)**
The most analytically distinctive Section 14 requirement — applying feasibility tests to each pivot option and distinguishing debated from analytically viable alternatives — is entirely absent (S14-9, S14-10). Spain's multi-vector posture is characterized well but not stress-tested.

**Section 12 institutional granularity (Pass 2)**
Section 12 scores 50% (6/12 addressed) — the lowest Pass 2 section score. Gaps: inter-service rivalries not analyzed; defense-industrial interests' constraint on civilian reform not framed; current military redlines not identified; defection risk ranking and fragmentation plausibility not assessed.

**Section 11 constraint disaggregation (Pass 2)**
The binding constraint on realignment not identified (S11-9) — the rubric's most actionable ask for a multi-vector state. Compliance assessment (substantive vs. performative) is incomplete across institutional memberships.

### Moderate Priority

**Spanish diaspora absent (all passes)**
The Spanish diaspora (~2.4 million overseas voters) is not analyzed as a political actor in any pass. Pass 1 (S5-8) flags this gap. Passes 2 and 3 do not repair it. This includes: voting rights exercise, diaspora lobbying, government cultivation/suppression, return migration dynamics, and alignment pressures from diaspora communities in different countries.

**Section 13 operational pipeline guidance (Pass 3)**
S13-6 — the ask to identify specific organizational infrastructure available for 6–12 month crisis scenario, with who controls it, mobilization speed, and communication channels — is entirely absent. This is the most forward-looking, pipeline-useful ask in Section 13.

**Section 16 Western misreading analysis (Pass 3)**
S16-6 requires structured analysis of specific Western misreadings of Spanish redlines (issue, typical Western interpretation, why wrong, corrective frame). This format is not deployed. The content to do so is partially present in the narrative (anti-Americanism, Black Legend, Catalan self-determination) but not organized into the required analytical structure.

**Two stress episodes for media analysis (Pass 2)**
S10-10 and S10-11 require at least two specific historical episodes anchoring information-flow-under-stress analysis. Only the October 2024 Valencia DANA is analyzed in detail. The Catalan crisis (2017) or COVID infodemic would provide the second episode needed to establish pattern recognition for the pipeline.

## What the Dossier Does Well

Despite the gaps, the Spain dossier contains high-quality analytical content in several areas:

- **Section 1** (Formation Trauma) and **Section 4** (Cleavage Structure): Perfect scores in Pass 1. Civil War genealogy, Transición mythology, "Two Spains" political activation — all analyzed with specificity and causal depth.
- **Section 8** (Environmental): Exceptional granularity on the Valencia DANA, Tagus-Segura crisis, reservoir data, desalination buildout, and transboundary Portugal disputes. The environmental section is among the strongest in the corpus.
- **Section 9** (Illicit Networks): Strong structural relevance analysis, detailed cocaine seizure data (142 tonnes), Gürtel/ERE/Koldo corruption cases with specific figures, and realistic assessment of SEPBLAC capacity.
- **Section 11** (International Commitments): The March 2026 base access crisis is analyzed with exceptional operational specificity — legal framework, military repositioning, diplomatic escalation, and economic exposure assessment — providing a template for what this section can achieve.
- **Section 16** (Collective Memory): The Iraq-to-Iran analogy and memory narrative → foreign policy outcomes analysis is the strongest section in Pass 3, correctly identifying how Civil War trauma, 1898 Disaster, and anti-war backlash produce specific, observable foreign policy behaviors.
- **Specificity throughout**: The dossier consistently names figures, dates, tonnages, percentages, treaty names, and legislative references. This specificity distinguishes it from generic country analyses and partially compensates for the citation gap.
