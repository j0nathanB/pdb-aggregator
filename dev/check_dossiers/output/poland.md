## Key Gaps — Ranked by Severity

### Structural Defects (Affect All Three Passes)

**1. Citations entirely absent (CC-17, CC-18, CC-19, CC-20)**
All three passes have zero footnote citations. No Markdown `[^N]` syntax used anywhere. No `## Sources` section in any pass. This is the most consequential structural failure — it affects 4 cross-cutting asks × 3 passes = 12 evaluation instances, all failed. The text references specific sources by name in prose (GLOBSEC, CBOS, Eurostat, GUS, Mandiant) but never in footnote format. Resolution requires retrofitting inline citations to all substantive claims across all passes.

**2. Confidence markers absent (CC-5)**
No `[HIGH CONFIDENCE]`, `[MODERATE CONFIDENCE]`, or `[ASSESSED]` tags appear anywhere across all three passes. This affects not only the cross-cutting ask but also the Section 18 judgment format requirements (S18-5, S18-6) and Section 15 behavioral inference requirements (S15-10, S15-14). Severity is high because the consuming LLM cannot distinguish high-confidence structural claims from provisional assessments.

**3. US retrenchment not set as baseline (CC-10)**
No pass establishes US retrenchment as Structural Claim #0. USAID dissolution is noted factually in Section 13; tariffs and troop deployment discussed in Sections 14 and 15; but none frames US retrenchment as the baseline operating environment per rubric instruction. This is a three-pass failure.

### Structural Completeness Failures (Sections Absent or Near-Absent)

**4. Section 17 (Cross-Facet Intersection Analysis) — 0% compliance**
This section was not produced. The merged "Sections 17–20" block covers fiscal/security/demographic tensions at a summary level, but contains none of the five-intersection format required by the rubric (interacting facets named, mechanism explained, historical example, "When the pipeline encounters..." instruction, mechanistic specificity). Eight asks entirely unaddressed.

**5. Section 20 (Pipeline Integration Notes) — 15% compliance**
Reduced to a two-paragraph "Pipeline and methodology notes" generic source list. Missing: 5+ conditional source interpretation rules, event significance thresholds, LLM-specific recency/Western-framework/false-equivalence calibrations, bilateral relationship pipeline entries. Seven of ten asks unaddressed.

**6. Section 18 (Key Analytical Judgments) — 36% compliance**
Five judgments provided but without the required format (structural basis references, falsification conditions, confidence levels). Judgments are directional and temporally incomplete. Four of seven asks unaddressed.

**7. Section 19 (Watch Indicators) — 19% compliance**
Only six indicators provided (minimum is 8). Not organized into leading/confirming categories. No observation channels, signal/noise distinctions, trigger thresholds, or cross-references to sections. Six of eight asks unaddressed.

**8. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS blocks absent from Pass 2 and Pass 3 (CC-9)**
Pass 1 correctly ends every section with a STRUCTURAL CLAIMS block. Pass 2 drops this entirely. Pass 3 has no STRUCTURAL CLAIMS blocks in Sections 13–16, and the merged 17–20 section provides "key judgments" in a different format. This regression from Pass 1 standard is unexplained.

**9. CC-8 violated: Sections 17–20 merged**
The rubric cross-cutting asks explicitly require each section to be produced as a "separate, individually headed section (no combining/merging)." The Pass 3 document merges Sections 17–20 under one heading. This is a direct violation of a cross-cutting instruction.

### Content Gaps (Specific Asks Not Met)

**10. Section 12 (Military & Security DNA) — 54% compliance**
Military economic interests (PGZ's political autonomy, defense industry's vested stake in spending levels) not analyzed (S12-4, S12-5, S12-6). Military institutional redlines not identified (S12-11). Security sector fragmentation under stress not assessed (S12-12).

**11. Section 15 (Constitutional Crises) behavioral inference methodology**
Personal factors with strategic relevance not assessed for key leaders (S15-7). No [ASSESSED] flags on behavioral inferences (S15-10). No two-episode supporting evidence for behavioral patterns (S15-14). These are procedural requirements, not content gaps — the underlying information about Nawrocki's confrontational behavior is present but not formatted per rubric.

**12. Section 16 (Collective Memory) Western misreading absent**
No corrective framing for Western analytical mistakes on Polish grievance narratives (S16-6, S16-8). The rubric asks for specific issue + typical Western interpretation + why wrong + corrective frame — a pipeline-specific output that is entirely absent.

**13. Section 2 (Geographic Determinism) EEZ/fisheries absent**
Polish Baltic EEZ claims and fishing industry politics not addressed (S2-8, S2-9). Given Poland's Baltic offshore wind expansion and Russia's Baltic Fleet proximity, these are non-trivial omissions.

**14. Section 7 renewable energy supply chain dependencies**
No analysis of Poland's dependence on Chinese solar panel manufacturing, critical minerals for battery storage, or technology licensing for wind turbines (S7-12). This is increasingly material given Poland's 34 GW renewables buildout.

## Strengths

- **Factual depth and specificity** are exceptional across all three passes. The dossier consistently provides precise figures, dates, vote totals, dollar amounts, and polling data. Pass 1 and the substantive sections of Pass 2 and 3 are among the more detailed structural dossiers evaluable under this rubric.
- **Section 8 (Environmental & Climatic Structure)** achieves 100% compliance — a model section. Causal chains (coal mine brine → Oder algae bloom → fish kill → Germany-Poland diplomatic crisis) exemplify the analytical standard required throughout.
- **Section 11 (International Institutional Commitments)** achieves 96% compliance. Defection costs disaggregated across four dimensions with quantified binding constraints.
- **Section 1 (Formation Trauma)** achieves 100% compliance. Layered trauma analysis, institutional transmission channels, and narrative contestation all addressed.
- **Causal reasoning** is a consistent strength. The dossier favors explanation over description: "X happened because Y, which means Z" is the dominant analytical mode, meeting CC-21 across all passes.
- **Self-containedness** is well maintained. All institutions, acronyms, and political figures are introduced with sufficient context for a consuming LLM without prior Poland expertise.
