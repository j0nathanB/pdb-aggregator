## Key Gaps (All Passes)

### 1. CITATIONS ABSENT ACROSS ALL THREE PASSES (CC17–CC20)
**Severity: Critical | Affects: 100% of passes**

Not a single Markdown footnote ([^N]) appears in any of the three passes. There is no Sources section in any pass. Zero citations out of a required minimum of 15–25 per pass (45–75 total across the dossier). This is the single most significant structural deficiency. The rubric's citation requirement is unambiguous and applies to "every substantive factual claim, statistic, date, treaty reference, or institutional detail." The India dossier contains hundreds of such claims — all uncited.

**Affected asks:** CC17, CC18, CC19, CC20 (4 asks × 3 passes = 12 NOT ADDRESSED items)

---

### 2. STRUCTURAL CLAIMS BLOCKS ABSENT FROM ALL SECTIONS (CC9)
**Severity: Critical | Affects: Sections 0–17 (18 sections)**

The rubric requires each section to conclude with a STRUCTURAL CLAIMS block of numbered, discrete, falsifiable assertions. No such block appears in any section of any pass. Section 18 provides excellent analytical judgments, but these are a separate section (not section-terminal blocks for each of Secs 0–17).

**Affected asks:** CC9 (1 ask × 3 passes = 3 NOT ADDRESSED items, but cumulative across 18 sections)

---

### 3. CONFIDENCE MARKERS ABSENT OR MALFORMATTED (CC5, S18-6)
**Severity: High | Affects: All three passes**

The [HIGH CONFIDENCE], [MODERATE CONFIDENCE], [ASSESSED] markers required by the rubric are entirely absent from Passes 1 and 2. In Pass 3, Section 18 uses confidence language in prose but not the required bracketed format with attached evidential basis. Multiple claims throughout the dossier—Partition death tolls, shadow economy percentages, nuclear warhead counts, Naxal combatant figures—would appropriately carry MODERATE CONFIDENCE or ASSESSED markers.

---

### 4. SECTION 19 NON-COMPLIANT FORMAT (S19-7, S19-8)
**Severity: High | Affects: Pass 3**

The 12 Watch Indicators are substantively excellent (concrete observables, leading/confirming distinction, reasonable baselines). However, the rubric requires a specific five-field format: **Indicator / Relates to / Observation channel / Trigger threshold / Structural implication**. None of the 12 indicators use this format. The "Relates to" section-and-claim tagging is entirely absent for all 12.

---

### 5. SECTION 17 PIPELINE OPERATIONAL INSTRUCTIONS MISSING (S17-6)
**Severity: High | Affects: Pass 3**

Six cross-facet intersections are identified — all substantively strong with clear mechanisms and concrete examples. But the rubric requires each to conclude with an explicit pipeline operational instruction in the format "When the pipeline encounters [X], it should [Y] because [Z]." None of the six intersections include this required element.

---

### 6. SECTION 20 SOURCE GUIDANCE FORMAT NON-COMPLIANT (S20-1)
**Severity: Medium | Affects: Pass 3**

Five source types are assessed in Section 20 (government sources, Supreme Court rulings, investigative journalism, international indices, RSS publications). The analysis is accurate and useful. But the rubric requires the format: **(outlet, topic domain, interpretation, specific adjustment, basis)** — a conditional rule structure. The current treatment is analytical prose, not a set of conditional rules the pipeline can apply mechanically.

---

### 7. MILITARY ECONOMIC INTERESTS NOT ADDRESSED (S12-4, S12-5, S12-6)
**Severity: Medium | Affects: Pass 2**

Three sequential asks about military economic interests are unaddressed: the scale of military-owned enterprises (canteen stores, ordnance factories, ECHS hospitals, land holdings), how those interests affect defense reform feasibility, and whether economic assets grant military autonomy from civilian government. This is a substantive analytical gap — India's military has significant economic interests through the Canteen Stores Department, military hospitals, ex-servicemen cooperatives, and land use that shape bureaucratic resistance to reforms like theaterization.

---

### 8. ELITE OFFSHORE ASSET EXPOSURE ABSENT (S9-7)
**Severity: Medium | Affects: Pass 2**

Section 9 (Illicit Networks) does not address the specific foreign jurisdictions where Indian political and business elites hold assets — Mauritius, Singapore, British Virgin Islands, UAE — or assess the leverage that targeted sanctions/asset freezes would create. Given the dossier's otherwise strong coverage of state-criminal integration and ED weaponization, this is a notable omission.

---

### 9. SECTION 16 WESTERN MISREADINGS NOT FORMATTED (S16-6)
**Severity: Low-Medium | Affects: Pass 3**

The required four-field structure (specific issue / typical Western interpretation / why wrong / corrective frame) for Western misreadings of Indian grievance narratives is not used. The substantive analysis is present (nuclear weapons as civilizational restoration, not deterrence) but not formatted as discrete, citable correction rules for the pipeline.

---

### 10. STRUCTURAL CLAIM #0 / US RETRENCHMENT BASELINE ABSENT (CC10)
**Severity: Low-Medium | Affects: All three passes**

The rubric requires treating US retrenchment as the baseline operating environment through Structural Claim #0 in each pass. Pass 3 (Section 14) addresses US retrenchment substantively — the Trump 2.0 disruption, 50% tariffs, Quad uncertainty. But this is never framed as a structural baseline Claim #0 in any section of any pass. The multi-alignment architecture is correctly analyzed as under stress, but the framing requirement is not met.

## Strengths

The India dossier is analytically sophisticated and content-rich despite the structural formatting failures. Notable strengths:

- **Factual density:** Every section is grounded in specific statistics, dates, legislation, and named actors. The level of empirical specificity is high across all three passes.
- **Causal reasoning:** The dossier consistently explains *why* things happen, not just *that* they happened. The BJP electoral bond → quid pro quo chain, the Galwan → LAC infrastructure race, the Operation Sindoor escalation ladder, and the FCRA → NGO hollowing are all causally explained.
- **Section 14 (Alliance Genealogy):** A standout — all 14 asks addressed. The multi-alignment analysis with domestic constituency mapping is thorough and analytically distinctive.
- **Section 8 (Environmental):** All 10 asks addressed. The three-frontier transboundary water crisis (Indus/IWT abeyance, Brahmaputra/Motuo, Ganges/1996 Treaty expiry) is one of the dossier's most original analytical contributions.
- **Section 10 (Information Ecosystem):** Near-perfect (11.5/12). The Reliance-Disney JV consolidation, DAVP advertising leverage, and WhatsApp Pramukh network at booth level are well-documented and analytically integrated.
- **Section 1 (Formation Trauma) and Section 4 (Cleavages):** Both at 100% with strong comparative and causal analysis.
- **Section 17 (Cross-Facet Intersections):** Six substantive intersections, all mechanistically specific, with the Indus-nuclear and economic-structure-alliance intersections particularly strong.
- **Currency:** The dossier is current through early 2026 (Operation Sindoor May 2025, India-EU FTA January 2026, Macron visit February 2026, February 2026 US tariff deal), making it unusually timely for a structural dossier.
