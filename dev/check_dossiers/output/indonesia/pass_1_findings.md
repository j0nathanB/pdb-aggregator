1. **S2.9** — Fisheries as political variable/interstate friction: Not addressed. Indonesia's fisheries (Arafura Sea, North Natuna) generate real interstate friction with China and have domestic patronage dimensions that warranted treatment.
2. **S5.8** — Diaspora as distinct political actor: Omitted. The overseas worker diaspora is analyzed as economic pressure valve, but diaspora political agency (voting rights, lobbying, remittance leverage, government cultivation) is not addressed.
3. **S6.13** — IMF lending program conditionality: Absent. Indonesia's regular IMF Article IV consultations and the fiscal constraints these impose on policy autonomy are not discussed.
4. **S7.6** — Digital infrastructure dependencies on specific foreign actors: Huawei/ZTE vs. Western telecom equipment supply chain not assessed; a significant omission given Indonesia's contested position between Chinese and Western tech stacks.
5. **S7.7** — Water infrastructure: Not addressed. Though Indonesia lacks transboundary water dependency, domestic water management (Jakarta groundwater extraction driving subsidence) had structural implications that could have been developed here.
6. **S7.12** — Renewable energy supply chain dependencies: The critical minerals / renewable manufacturing / technology licensing dimension is absent despite nickel being central to the dossier.
