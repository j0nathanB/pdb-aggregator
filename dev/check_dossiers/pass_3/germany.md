## Section 13: Civil society under pressure from all directions

### Trade unions remain institutionally significant but structurally weakened

The DGB umbrella federation encompasses approximately **5.67 million members** across eight affiliated unions — down from 11.8 million at the 1991 peak. Union density stands below one-fifth of the workforce, and fewer than half of employees are covered by direct collective bargaining agreements. [Worker Participation](https://worker-participation.eu/national-industrial-relations/countries/germany) Despite this decline, unions retain considerable strike capacity and political weight.

**IG Metall** (~2.1 million members), Germany's largest industrial union, led the most consequential labor action of the period: two major Volkswagen strikes in late 2024 — the largest in VW history — over 35,000 planned job cuts. After 70 hours of negotiations (also a VW record), an agreement was reached on restructuring with capacity reductions [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IG_Metall) but no forced layoffs. **Ver.di** (~2 million members) negotiated a public sector deal in April 2025 covering 2.5 million federal and municipal workers: a pay freeze through March 2025 followed by **3% from April 2025 and 2.8% from May 2026**, a 27-month contract [World Socialist Web Site](https://www.wsws.org/en/articles/2025/04/09/26ab-a09.html) that effectively restricts strike action until 2027. The GDL (train drivers' union, ~40,000 members) [Worker Participation](https://worker-participation.eu/national-industrial-relations/countries/germany) conducted six national rail strikes in 2024 alone.

Germany registered **286 official industrial disputes in 2024** with **912,000 strike participants** [Substack](https://savageminds.substack.com/p/recent-strikes-in-germany) and approximately 946,000 strike days. DGB unions participated heavily in organizing the January–February 2024 anti-AfD protests and maintain strong (though formally neutral) SPD links — Ver.di chairman Frank Werneke and deputy Christine Behle are both SPD members. [World Socialist Web Site](https://www.wsws.org/en/articles/2025/04/09/26ab-a09.html)

### The anti-AfD movement was the largest postwar mobilization — but did not halt the AfD's rise

Following Correctiv's January 10, 2024 exposé of a far-right meeting in Potsdam — where AfD members and Identitarian Movement leader Martin Sellner discussed mass deportation plans [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024–2025_German_anti-extremism_protests)[Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_German_anti-extremism_protests) — **the largest protest wave in postwar German history erupted**. Over **1,800 demonstrations** drew an estimated **4 million participants** between January and April 2024. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_German_anti-extremism_protests) The weekend of January 19–21 alone saw 1.4 million marchers. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Potsdam_far-right_meeting) A Berlin rally on February 3 brought 150,000–300,000 people, organized by approximately 1,700 civil society organizations including trade unions, churches, Fridays for Future, and Campact. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_German_anti-extremism_protests) A second protest wave erupted in early 2025 after the CDU/CSU pushed through an immigration motion with AfD votes.

These unprecedented mobilizations demonstrated civil society's organizational capacity but ultimately did not prevent the AfD from winning **20.8%** in the February 2025 federal election [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_for_Germany) — a record. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_for_Germany)

### Climate activism faces legal criminalization

Fridays for Future Germany retains organizational infrastructure and mainstream legitimacy but has significantly reduced mobilization capacity compared to its 2019 peak. The movement pivoted toward co-organizing the 2024 anti-AfD protests and maintaining a reformist, legally registered protest approach.

**Letzte Generation** (Last Generation) pursued more confrontational tactics — road blockades, airport disruptions, building occupations — and faced escalating legal consequences. In December 2024, the group rebranded as **"Neue Generation"** (New Generation), shifting focus toward democratic reform. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letzte_Generation) Key legal developments include a **22-month prison sentence** (without parole) for a 65-year-old activist convicted of 40 road blockades (August 2024); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letzte_Generation) a Hamburg court ordering 10 activists to pay approximately **€1.1 million** in compensation and court costs for an airport blockade (November 2025); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letzte_Generation) and most significantly, a criminal trial beginning in February 2026 at Potsdam Regional Court against five organizers charged under **§129 of the Criminal Code — forming a criminal organization** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letzte_Generation) — a statute typically reserved for organized crime and terrorism. [Politischeverfolgung](https://politischeverfolgung.de/en/free-speech-restriction)

### Churches lose a million members annually but retain massive institutional infrastructure

For the fourth consecutive year, Germany's two major churches lost a combined **~1.13 million members** in 2025. [Newsworm](https://www.newsworm.de/news/german-church-membership-drops-by-1-13-million-in-2025) The religiously unaffiliated now outnumber combined Catholic and Protestant membership for the first time: **39.1 million (46.8%)** versus approximately 38 million church members (45%). [Evangelical Focus](https://evangelicalfocus.com/europe/30925/nones-outnumber-catholics-and-protestants-in-germany-for-the-first-time) The Catholic Church fell to **~19.2 million members** (23% of population), with only **25 priest ordinations** in 2025 — a historic low. [Newsworm](https://www.newsworm.de/news/german-church-membership-drops-by-1-13-million-in-2025) Regular Mass attendance stands at 6.6%. [Zenit](https://zenit.org/2025/03/27/result-of-the-german-synodal-way-half-a-million-catholics-leave-the-church-official-figures-show/) The Protestant EKD declined to approximately **17.4 million members**. [Newsworm](https://www.newsworm.de/news/german-church-membership-drops-by-1-13-million-in-2025)

Despite membership collapse, church welfare organizations remain Germany's largest non-state social infrastructure. **Caritas** (Catholic) employs **739,410 people** across 25,453 facilities, serving 13 million people annually. [Caritas-germany](https://www.caritas-germany.org/aboutus/what-we-do) **Diakonie** (Protestant) employs approximately 450,000–600,000 people across 33,000 care centers. [Diakonie Baden](https://www.diakonie-baden.de/sprachversionen/english) Together, Caritas and Diakonie account for roughly two-thirds of all employees in Germany's nonprofit welfare sector [Europa-uni](https://www.rewi.europa-uni.de/de/professuren/buergerl-recht-europ-dt-arbeitsrecht-zivilverfahren/_dateien/Care4Care_-National-Report-Germany_-Working-Conditions.pdf) — a structural weight that persists regardless of declining religious identification.

### Campus protests, protest restrictions, and civic space narrowing

Pro-Palestinian campus protests erupted across Germany in May 2024, with the most significant at Freie Universität Berlin (79 arrests) [Scholars at Risk](https://www.scholarsatrisk.org/report/2024-05-07-free-university-of-berlin/) and Humboldt University. The ensuing academic freedom scandal — when Education Minister Bettina Stark-Watzinger's ministry explored stripping research funding from academics who signed a letter supporting students' protest rights — forced State Secretary Sabine Döring's resignation and provoked widespread academic outrage. [Science|Business](https://sciencebusiness.net/news/universities/german-science-official-steps-down-row-about-pro-palestine-protest)

Germany's civic space has drawn increasing international criticism. Freedom House downgraded Germany's assembly freedom score from 4 to 3. [Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/country/germany/freedom-world/2024) CIVICUS now classifies Germany as having **"impaired civic space."** [Ilmr](https://ilmr.de/2025/demokratie-unter-druck-entwicklungen-im-umgang-mit-protest-und-oeffentlicher-meinungsaeusserung-in-deutschland/) The European Court of Human Rights ruled against Germany in May 2025 (*Ruß v. Germany*) for violating freedom of assembly. [Greenlegal](https://greenlegal.eu/versammlungsfreiheit-und-selbstschutz-die-unterschaetzte-begruendungslast-staatlicher-institutionen/) Amnesty International documented increasing blanket assembly bans, preventive detention, and excessive police force, [Politischeverfolgung](https://politischeverfolgung.de/en/free-speech-restriction) particularly against pro-Palestinian demonstrations. [Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/country/germany/freedom-world/2024) In February 2025, Berlin police banned the use of Arabic at demonstrations; in April 2025, a protester was detained for speaking Irish at a rally. [Hyphen](https://hyphenonline.com/2025/05/28/germany-laws-palestine-protest-citizenship-deport-legal/) An index documented over **750 incidents** of repression against pro-Palestinian activists since 2019. [Hyphen](https://hyphenonline.com/2025/05/28/germany-laws-palestine-protest-citizenship-deport-legal/)

Following the February 2025 election, the CDU filed **551 parliamentary questions** about 17 civil society organizations — including Correctiv, Campact, Greenpeace, and "Omas gegen Rechts" — regarding government funding. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024–2025_German_anti-extremism_protests) This was widely interpreted as political intimidation of democratic civil society.

### Farmer protests shook the Scholz coalition but did not repeat at scale

The December 2023–January 2024 farmer protests were triggered by planned cuts to agricultural diesel tax subsidies (~€440 million/year, averaging ~€2,900 per farm) following the Constitutional Court's November 2023 ruling that invalidated €60 billion in repurposed COVID funds. [Clean Energy Wire](https://www.cleanenergywire.org/factsheets/qa-how-are-farmers-protest-germany-linked-climate-policy) The German Farmers' Association (Bauernverband) organized a "Week of Action" (January 8–15, 2024) with nationwide highway blockades across all 16 states. [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/german-farmers-block-roads-with-tractors-in-protest-of-plan-to-scrap-diesel-tax-breaks) The culmination on January 15 brought approximately **3,000 tractors and 10,000+ people** to Berlin's Brandenburg Gate. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/1/15/thousands-of-tractors-block-berlin-traffic-over-plans-to-end-diesel-subsidy) The government conceded by retaining vehicle tax exemptions and phasing diesel subsidy cuts over three years. [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/german-farmers-block-roads-with-tractors-in-protest-of-plan-to-scrap-diesel-tax-breaks) No repeat of this scale occurred in 2025–2026, though structural agricultural discontent persists.

The AfD has achieved effective symbiosis with adjacent movements. ## Section 14: Alliance relationships in an era of transatlantic rupture

### US-Germany relations defined by tariffs, defense demands, and ideological friction

The Trump administration's return has catalyzed a fundamental reorientation of German strategic thinking. JD Vance's February 2025 Munich Security Conference speech [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/04/germany-trump-nato-ukraine-defense-zeitenwende?lang=en) — praising the AfD and criticizing European democracy — was described as a "watershed moment." [Mercator Institute for China Studies](https://merics.org/en/beyond-zeitenwende-us-policies-and-us-china-conflict-present-germany-historic-challenges) Merz responded by articulating a doctrine of **"step-by-step European independence from the United States."** [Carnegie Endowment for International Peace](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/04/germany-trump-nato-ukraine-defense-zeitenwende?lang=en)[Delorscentre](https://www.delorscentre.eu/en/publications/detail/publication/grand-coalition-in-europe)

On trade, the US imposed a **15% baseline tariff** on most EU goods (including automobiles) after negotiations, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/28/what-the-us-eu-trade-deal-means-for-the-auto-sector.html) with 50% tariffs on steel and aluminum. German automotive exports to the US fell approximately **14% year-on-year** in the first three quarters of 2025. [GMK Center](https://gmk.center/en/news/german-auto-industry-significantly-affected-by-us-tariffs-study/) VW warned of up to €5 billion in tariff costs. [Dealership Guy](https://news.dealershipguy.com/p/tariff-impact-grows-for-german-carmakers-as-u-s-market-tightens-2025-12-30) Berlin estimates the tariff regime could reduce German GDP by approximately **1%** and cost over 100,000 manufacturing jobs. [USImportData](https://www.usimportdata.com/blogs/us-germany-trade-relations-2025-us-tariffs-bilateral-data)

On defense, Germany reformed its constitutional debt brake in March 2025 to exempt defense spending above 1% of GDP from borrowing limits. [Foederalist +3](https://www.foederalist.eu/2025/09/zeitenwende-germany-future-security.html) The **2026 defense budget reached €108.2 billion** [Atlasinstitute](https://atlasinstitute.org/germanys-path-to-kriegstuchtigkeit-the-2026-defence-budget/) (~2.8% of GDP), [Overt Defense](https://www.overtdefense.com/2025/08/11/germanys-e108-2 billion-2026-defense-budget-setting-stage-for-historic-military-build-up/) making Germany the **fourth-largest military spender globally**. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundeswehr)[Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/02/europe-ramps-up-defense-spending-to-21-of-global-total-led-by-berlin-report/) Merz committed to **3.5% of GDP by 2029**, aligned with the new NATO target [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2025/06/26/germany-plans-to-double-its-defense-spending-within-five-years/) agreed at The Hague summit. [Business Sweden](https://www.business-sweden.com/insights/blogs/germany-a-new-era-for-investment/germanys-record-defence-modernisation-drive-as-of-22-october-2025/) Trump initially demanded 5%.

Merz visited the White House on June 5, 2025 [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/06/musk-vs-trump-dominated-yet-germanys-merz-walked-away-with-a-win.html)[American German Institute](https://americangerman.institute/2025/06/friedrich-merz-and-the-constraints-of-german-coalition-politics/) — a meeting deemed successful partly because it was overshadowed by the Trump-Musk public dispute. Subsequent meetings occurred in Egypt (October 2025) and Washington (March 2026, regarding the US-Israeli bombing campaign in Iran). [The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/germanys-merz-to-discuss-mideast-conflict-with-trump-during-dc-visit-tomorrow/) At Munich 2026, Merz declared: *"The culture war of the MAGA movement is not ours"* [Time](https://time.com/7378512/united-states-europe-divide-warning-merz-trump-rubio/) and warned of **"a deep rift"** between Europe and the United States. [Time](https://time.com/7378512/united-states-europe-divide-warning-merz-trump-rubio/)

### Franco-German relationship dramatically revitalized — except on fighter jets

After years of dysfunction under Scholz-Macron, the relationship was transformed under Merz, [Groupe d'Etudes Géopolitiques](https://geopolitique.eu/en/2025/05/05/the-conditions-of-a-franco-german-deal-on-european-defense/) described as a "Rhenish, Francophile Christian Democrat." [Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/2025/05/merz-and-macron-can-restart-europes-franco-german-engine) Merz's first foreign trip as chancellor on May 7, 2025 was to Paris, [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/05/07/germany-and-france-to-set-up-joint-defence-and-security-council) followed immediately by Warsaw. [Anadolu Ajansı](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/europe/poland-and-germany-resetting-relations-after-a-decade-of-misunderstandings/3560516)[American German Institute](https://americangerman.institute/2025/06/friedrich-merz-and-the-constraints-of-german-coalition-politics/) A joint op-ed in Le Figaro/Die Welt called for a "Franco-German new start for Europe," [Foederalist](https://www.foederalist.eu/2025/10/franco-german-engine.html) announcing a **Joint Defence and Security Council**.

The most consequential development was the **March 2, 2026 Joint Declaration on Nuclear Deterrence** signed at the Élysée Palace. France and Germany established a bilateral nuclear steering group for doctrinal dialogue and coordination — Germany would participate conventionally in exercises linked to French nuclear deterrence [elysee](https://www.elysee.fr/en/emmanuel-macron/2026/03/02/joint-declaration-of-president-macron-and-chancellor-merz) while remaining NPT-compliant. [Élysée](https://www.elysee.fr/en/emmanuel-macron/2026/03/02/joint-declaration-of-president-macron-and-chancellor-merz) This represents a historic hedging strategy against potential US nuclear umbrella withdrawal.

However, the **€100 billion FCAS** (Future Combat Air System) sixth-generation fighter program is in **existential crisis**. Dassault (France) and Airbus (Germany) remain deadlocked over workshare [European Council on Foreign Relations](https://ecfr.eu/article/the-trouble-with-fcas-why-europes-fighter-jet-project-is-not-taking-off/)[ВПК.name](https://vpk.name/en/1002981_cygnus-pike-and-fcas-eu-countries-unhappy-with-cooperation-on-key-defense-programs.html) (Dassault demands up to 80%) and intellectual property. German Defense Minister Pistorius issued an ultimatum in October 2025; French counterpart Vautrin publicly stated Germany "did not have the capacity to build a fighter jet." [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Combat_Air_System) Belgium declared FCAS "dead" in early 2026. Germany is reportedly exploring alternatives: joining the UK-Italy-Japan GCAP/Tempest program or partnership with Sweden's Saab. The **MGCS tank program** is in better shape, with a project company established in Cologne (April 2025) [European Security & Defence](https://euro-sd.com/2025/08/articles/exclusive/45998/mgcs-status-update/) and eight-pillar technology development proceeding, though fielding has slipped to 2040–2045. [European Security & Defence](https://euro-sd.com/2025/08/articles/exclusive/45998/mgcs-status-update/)[Army Technology](https://www.army-technology.com/news/mgcs-project-new-agreement/)

### Poland, UK, and the emerging E3 security architecture

The Germany-Poland relationship experienced a genuine reset. Merz visited Warsaw on his first full day in office; [American German Institute](https://americangerman.institute/2025/06/friedrich-merz-and-the-constraints-of-german-coalition-politics/) Tusk declared it "a new beginning, perhaps the most important in the last dozen years." At December 2025 intergovernmental consultations, Germany returned **73 medieval documents** looted during WWII — described as "the most important return of stolen cultural heritage in modern Polish history." Merz pledged rapid construction of a Berlin monument to Polish victims of German occupation. On reparations, Tusk effectively shelved the €1.3 trillion demand: "I am a historian… but we will not ask for it." A bilateral defense agreement is being drafted for 2026 completion. [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/01/29/germany-proposes-two-speed-eu-to-hasten-defense-buildup/)

The UK-Germany relationship was formalized through two landmark agreements: the **Trinity House Agreement** (October 2024) on defense cooperation [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-germany-trinity-house-agreement-on-defence) including a joint deep precision strike capability with **2,000+ km range**, [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/joint-statement-on-uk-germany-trinity-house-agreement-progress-15-may-2025) and the **Kensington Treaty** (July 2025) — the first bilateral friendship treaty since WWII, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington_Treaty) covering diplomacy, defense, economy, science, energy, and people-to-people exchange. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington_Treaty) The UK dropped from Germany's 3rd largest trading partner (2016) to 9th (2024) post-Brexit; [Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/2025/07/after-brexit-e3-new-treaty-puts-uk-germany-and-france-back-heart-european-security) the treaty aims to reverse this decline.

The **E3 format** (UK-France-Germany) has re-emerged as the driving force of European security, with back-to-back Macron and Merz visits to London in July 2025 and trilateral coordination on Ukraine. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington_Treaty)

### China remains the central de-risking challenge

Bilateral trade reached approximately **€246 billion in 2024**, [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/china-germany-relations-2025-merz-leadership-trade-investment/) with China reclaiming status as Germany's largest trading partner in the first three quarters of 2025. Germany accounts for over **35% of total EU-China trade**. [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/02/merz-in-china-germany-between-de-risking-and-strategic-partnership/) However, the trade deficit reached an all-time high of approximately **€87–90 billion** in 2025, with German exports to China slumping ~11% while Chinese imports surged ~11%.

The Merz coalition agreement identifies China as a **"systemic rival"** — dropping the Scholz-era "partner" language. [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/china-germany-relations-2025-merz-leadership-trade-investment/) Yet German FDI in China reached a four-year high of over **€7 billion** (January–November 2025), up 55.5% from 2024, as VW, BMW, and Bosch doubled down on "in China, for China" localization. Over 5,000 German companies operate in China. [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/china-germany-relations-2025-merz-leadership-trade-investment/) Merz visited Beijing in February 2026, maintaining the pragmatic balancing act — hawkish rhetoric combined with continued deep economic engagement.

On 5G, Germany restricted Huawei/ZTE components in core networks in 2024, [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/02/merz-in-china-germany-between-de-risking-and-strategic-partnership/) requiring removal by end of 2026, with RAN/transport network removal by 2029 [TelecomTV](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/5g/germany-finally-unveils-huawei-zte-ban-plan-50809/) — significantly later than allies like Sweden and the UK.

### Germany-Israel: Staatsräson under maximum strain

Germany is Israel's **second-largest arms supplier** (after the US), providing approximately 30% of Israel's major arms imports. Between October 2023 and May 2025, Germany issued arms export licenses worth over **€485 million** to Israel. In the other direction, Germany purchased over **$10 billion** in Israeli defense equipment in 2024–2025, including the **Arrow 3 missile defense system** [Calcali Tech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/vrvgh4wdb) (delivered December 2025 at Holzdorf Air Base — the first Israeli deployment outside its territory). [The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/as-germany-rearms-holocaust-begotten-ties-with-israel-take-on-increasing-importance/)

Merz **suspended arms exports** to Israel on August 8, 2025, [American German Institute](https://americangerman.institute/2025/08/a-zeitenwende-in-germanys-israel-policy/) citing the "worsening toll on civilians" after Israel's Gaza City takeover plan — then **lifted the suspension** in November 2025, citing a Gaza ceasefire. Bertelsmann Stiftung surveys show favorable German views of Israel declined from **46% (2021) to 36% (2025)**, [American German Institute](https://americangerman.institute/2025/08/a-zeitenwende-in-germanys-israel-policy/) with **80% of Germans** believing Israel's actions in Gaza are unjustified and **40% under 40** wanting reduced relations with Israel. [Gppi](https://gppi.net/2025/06/12/germany-must-revisit-its-relationship-with-israel)

### Broader partnerships and EU institutional reform

Germany leads EU institutional reform advocacy, pushing for qualified majority voting extension (particularly in foreign policy, where 45 vetoes occurred between 2011–2025, 19 by Hungary alone), [Centre for European Reform](https://www.cer.eu/insights/does-eu-enlargement-require-voting-reform) and linking QMV expansion to eastward enlargement as a "package solution." [Stiftung Wissenschaft und Politik](https://www.swp-berlin.org/10.18449/2022C61/) In January 2026, Finance Minister Klingbeil convened an **"E6" format** (France, Poland, Spain, Italy, Netherlands), proposing a **"two-speed Europe"** for defense and competitiveness. [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/01/29/germany-proposes-two-speed-eu-to-hasten-defense-buildup/)

## Sections 17–20: Cross-cutting structural vulnerabilities

### The pension-defense squeeze defines Germany's fiscal frontier

Germany confronts a structural zero-sum competition between social spending and rearmament absent fundamental fiscal reform. The Labor and Social Affairs Ministry received approximately **€190 billion** in the 2025 budget — over one-third of total expenditures — including €122.5 billion for pensions and €52 billion for unemployment benefits. Social expenditure has hovered around 26% of GDP since the 1990s. The old-age dependency ratio stands at **35.2%**, above the EU average. [Population Europe](https://www.population-europe.eu/events/event-reviews-recordings/defence-meets-demography-future-armed-forces-ageing-societies)

Defense spending is on a historic trajectory: from €53 billion in 2020 to **€108.2 billion in 2026** (fourth-largest globally), [Atlasinstitute](https://atlasinstitute.org/germanys-path-to-kriegstuchtigkeit-the-2026-defence-budget/) with a target of €152 billion (~3.5% of GDP) by 2029 [Business Sweden +2](https://www.business-sweden.com/insights/blogs/germany-a-new-era-for-investment/germanys-record-defence-modernisation-drive-as-of-22-october-2025/) and Merz's Davos 2026 pledge to reach 5%. [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/01/29/germany-proposes-two-speed-eu-to-hasten-defense-buildup/) The March 2025 constitutional amendment [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Merz) — exempting defense spending above 1% of GDP from borrowing limits [Business Sweden](https://www.business-sweden.com/insights/blogs/germany-a-new-era-for-investment/germanys-record-defence-modernisation-drive-as-of-22-october-2025/) and creating a **€500 billion infrastructure fund** [Charles Schwab +2](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/what-happened-to-germanys-spending-boom) — resolved the immediate constraint but pushed projected government deficits from 2.7% (2024) to 4.0% (2026), with debt-to-GDP rising from 62.2% toward 67% by 2027. [Economy and Finance](https://economy-finance.ec.europa.eu/economic-surveillance-eu-member-states/country-pages/germany/economic-forecast-germany_en)

The Bundeswehr's active strength [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/03/23/europe/germany-military-investment-intl/index.html) of **186,221** (January 2026) falls far short of the 203,000 target by 2031 [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/03/23/europe/germany-military-investment-intl/index.html) and the NATO-required 260,000 active troops plus 200,000 reservists by 2035. Only 1,750 new positions were authorized for 2026. An aging, shrinking population competing simultaneously for military recruits, healthcare workers, and industrial labor creates a demographic bottleneck with no near-term solution. [Population Europe](https://www.population-europe.eu/events/event-reviews-recordings/defence-meets-demography-future-armed-forces-ageing-societies)

### Energy dependency creates transatlantic leverage and alliance vulnerability

The nuclear phase-out (completed April 2023) and loss of Russian pipeline gas have created new dependencies. **86% of German LNG imports came from the United States in 2024**, with long-term supply contracts extending 20 years beginning 2026–2027. Germany operates four floating LNG terminals covering 5–12% of gas consumption. Total economic losses from reduced Russian gas are estimated at **€735 billion**. Gas consumption fell approximately 14% versus the 2018–2021 average, and energy-intensive industrial output in 2025 remains **17% below pre-2022 levels**.

Renewables reached 59% of electricity in 2024 but dropped to 47% in Q1 2025 due to weak winds. The 80% target by 2030 remains ambitious. Green hydrogen partnerships with Namibia, Saudi Arabia, Brazil, Morocco, Canada, and others — targeting 200,000+ tonnes annually by 2030 — represent diversification efforts. The AfD's position — supporting coal, gas, restarting Nord Stream, and leaving the Paris Climate Accord — would fundamentally reshape Germany's alliance positioning if implemented.

### Economic stagnation fuels the immigration-far right feedback loop

Germany's economy has experienced its **worst five-year period since reunification**: GDP contracted 0.3% (2023) and 0.2% (2024), stagnated in 2025, and remains at approximately 2019 levels in real terms. Manufacturing PMI plummeted to 40.6 in September 2024 — the 27th consecutive month of contraction. Corporate bankruptcies hit a ten-year high. Germany was the **only G7 economy to contract in both 2023 and 2024**.

Simultaneously, first-time asylum applications peaked at **329,120 in 2023** (highest since 2016) before declining to 229,751 (2024) and 106,298 (January–November 2025, a 51% decrease). Three high-profile migrant-perpetrated attacks since December 2024 intensified public anxiety. FES Security Radar 2025 data shows concern about uncontrolled immigration rose from **61% (2021) to 73% (2024)**. Pre-election polling identified economy and immigration as the two most pressing voter concerns.

The correlation between economic weakness, immigration anxiety, and AfD support is strongest in eastern states where all three factors concentrate. AfD's 20.8% federal result, 33–38.5% in eastern state elections, and current 25–28% national polling represent a structural rather than cyclical phenomenon. The party's combined economic protectionism and immigration restrictionism addresses anxieties that mainstream parties' pro-growth and integration-oriented approaches have failed to resolve.

### A dramatic shift in threat perception

German opinion polling reveals a population undergoing rapid strategic reorientation:

- **79%** believe Russia is the greatest threat to global peace (Allensbach, January 2025)
- **46%** now see the United States as the biggest threat to world peace — up from **24%** in 2024 (Allensbach) — the most dramatic single-year shift in decades
- **73%** support NATO membership (YouGov 2025), but trust in the US as an alliance partner has collapsed
- **70%+** view the EU favorably (Pew 2025), among the highest rates in the EU
- **74%** are concerned about war in Europe in the next few years (More In Common 2025)
- **69%** believe Germany could not defend itself against Russia (FES 2025)
- Only **10%** believe Germany is well-prepared for war or disasters (Allensbach)
- **64%** support reducing ties with Russia even at the cost of lower living standards; **57%** say the same for China (FES 2025)

The simultaneous perception of Russia and the United States as threats — combined with strong EU and NATO support but deep skepticism about national defense capacity — captures Germany's strategic predicament: a country that wants collective security but doubts every available provider, including itself.

### Digital infrastructure remains a critical vulnerability

The BSI's 2025 report found **80% of cyberattacks targeted SMEs**, with 461 data leak incidents involving German institutions and 791,722 IP addresses with exposed security weaknesses. Russian APTs (Fancy Bear, Midnight Blizzard) and Chinese APTs (Nylon Typhoon) actively target German critical infrastructure. Russian sabotage operations across Europe increased **four-fold** in 2024 versus the prior year, accelerating in 2025. Signal phishing campaigns targeting politicians, military, diplomats, and journalists prompted a joint BfV/BSI advisory in February 2026.

Germany's NIS2 Directive transposition (December 2025, over a year late) expanded regulated entities from ~4,500 to approximately **29,500**. Huawei/ZTE core network removal must be completed by end of 2026, with broader RAN/transport removal by 2029. The BSI flagged solar inverters and smart grid technologies as growing attack surfaces, creating a tension between Energiewende infrastructure expansion and cybersecurity.

------

## Conclusion: structural fractures and institutional resilience in tension

Germany in early 2026 presents a paradox of simultaneous institutional strength and accelerating legitimacy erosion. The December 2024 constitutional court reform, the March 2025 debt brake amendment, the Kensington and Franco-German nuclear declarations, and the largest rearmament since the Cold War demonstrate a state apparatus capable of decisive structural adaptation. Yet the **fastest-declining government approval in modern German history**, an opposition party potentially overtaking the governing party in polls for the first time since 1932, five lost years of economic growth, and a civic space increasingly criticized by international human rights monitors all suggest the adaptations may not be fast enough.

Three dynamics will determine Germany's trajectory through 2029. First, whether the AfD's 25–28% national support stabilizes or continues rising — the September 2026 eastern state elections in Saxony-Anhalt and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern are the critical test. Second, whether economic recovery materializes — the €500 billion infrastructure fund and defense spending surge provide fiscal stimulus, but structural competitiveness problems (energy costs, regulation, demographics) remain unaddressed. Third, whether the E3 European security architecture can mature fast enough to compensate for an unreliable transatlantic partner — the FCAS collapse and persistent Franco-German industrial friction suggest this remains far from certain.

The most novel insight from this analysis is the emergence of Germany as a country where **security threat perception and alliance trust are moving in opposite directions simultaneously**: Germans perceive escalating external threats while losing confidence in every available security guarantor, including their own military. This creates political space for both rearmament *and* populist rejection of rearmament, for both Europeanism *and* national retreat — a structural ambivalence that no current political formation has resolved.
