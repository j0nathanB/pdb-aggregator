# Section 13: Dissent infrastructure and civil society

Poland possesses one of Central Europe's most vibrant civil society ecosystems, shaped by the Solidarity legacy, EU integration, and eight years of mobilization against PiS rule (2015–2023). The sector now faces a paradox: the liberal-democratic movement's partial electoral victory in 2023 removed its unifying threat, while the May 2025 election of PiS-backed President Karol Nawrocki has created new institutional blockages that prevent legislative delivery on the social promises (abortion, LGBTQ+ rights, education reform) that motivated mass mobilization. Civil society remains deeply polarized between a progressive-liberal wing and a conservative-Catholic countermovement of comparable organizational sophistication.

### The funding landscape shifted dramatically in 2025

Two financial shocks reshaped the NGO ecosystem. The Trump administration's dissolution of USAID in February 2025 stripped funding from prominent progressive organizations including the Helsinki Foundation for Human Rights, Campaign Against Homophobia, Polish Migration Forum, and Watchdog Polska. Gazeta Wyborcza, Poland's leading liberal newspaper, reportedly received **$20 million from USAID** between 2016 and 2023. The government's replacement response was slow, drawing sharp criticism from sector leaders.

Partially offsetting this loss, new EEA/Norway Grants worth **€924.9 million** were allocated to Poland in April 2025 across eight programs, including a dedicated **€83.5 million Civil Society Fund** launched in December 2025 and managed by a consortium including the Stefan Batory Foundation. The Tusk government's 2025 budget added 70 million złoty (~€17 million) for civil society. EU Rapid Response Grants provided an additional PLN 800,000 for fundamental rights protection. Poland maintains no "foreign agent" law comparable to Russia or Hungary, and the constitutional framework (Articles 12 and 58) provides robust protections for freedom of association.

Simultaneously, dozens of conservative and Christian NGOs face prolonged audits related to PiS-era Justice Fund distributions, connected to the prosecution of former Deputy Justice Minister Marcin Romanowski (who received political asylum in Hungary in December 2024). This has effectively paralyzed parts of the right-wing civil society infrastructure.

### Solidarity has completed its transformation into a PiS auxiliary

The NSZZ "Solidarność" trade union, under Chairman Piotr Duda, retains under **600,000 members** — a fraction of its 1981 peak of 10 million. It is now firmly embedded in PiS's political infrastructure rather than functioning as an independent labor force. Solidarity endorsed presidential candidate Nawrocki at the historic Gdańsk shipyard in February 2025, with Duda calling him "the only guarantor" of workers' postulates. The union's relationship with the Tusk government is adversarial. Its policy positions combine economic protectionism with cultural conservatism, including opposition to "LGBT ideology" and support for the Sunday trading ban enacted in 2017. While Solidarity retains the capacity to mobilize hundreds of thousands through membership and family networks, its direct policy influence has become marginal.

### Think tanks span the political spectrum with high analytical output

Poland's think tank ecosystem is extensive and consequential for policy formation:

**Government-affiliated institutions** include OSW (Centre for Eastern Studies), the premier analytical body on post-Soviet affairs with ~50+ research fellows, directed by Wojciech Konończuk; and PISM (Polish Institute of International Affairs), ranked among the EU's most influential foreign policy think tanks, which supports diplomatic decision-making and participates in the EU Non-Proliferation Consortium.

**Independent liberal institutions** include the Stefan Batory Foundation, co-founded by George Soros and Polish opposition leaders in 1988, now a major player in the civil society funding ecosystem managing EEA grant distribution; and the Helsinki Foundation for Human Rights, which maintains credibility through nonpartisan rule-of-law advocacy, having criticized both PiS judicial overhaul and some Tusk government reform methods.

**Conservative institutions** are anchored by Ordo Iuris, a Catholic legal think tank that has emerged as the most effective conservative counter-mobilization force. It led the Coalition to Save the Polish School (KROPS), comprising **91 member organizations**, which successfully lobbied President Nawrocki to veto the government's education reform bill in December 2025. Additional notable institutions include the Kosciuszko Institute (Kraków, cybersecurity focus), CASE (economic research), the Warsaw Institute (geopolitics), and the Institute of Public Affairs (social policy).

### Women's Strike faces organizational decline amid legislative stalemate

The All-Poland Women's Strike (Strajk Kobiet), which mobilized hundreds of thousands in 2020–2021 against the Constitutional Tribunal's near-total abortion ban, has seen its organizational capacity diminish. Key leader Marta Lempart faces approximately **80 pending legal cases** from the PiS era, many politically motivated. Academic observer Agnieszka Graff warns that "activists are burnt out, and society at large may care about this topic but they won't mobilise alone."

Abortion reform remains effectively blocked despite being a central Tusk campaign promise. In July 2024, a decriminalization bill was **rejected 218–215** after PSL coalition partners voted with the opposition. Three competing draft laws circulate within the coalition: abortion on demand up to 12 weeks (KO/Left), decriminalization of assistance (Left), and return to the pre-2020 "compromise" (Third Way). President Nawrocki's certain veto of any liberalization and the coalition's inability to muster the **3/5 supermajority** to override it have rendered legislative progress impossible before 2027. Public opinion remains supportive: approximately **60% of Poles** favor legal abortion access, and abortion was cited as a key motivator in the 2023 election's record 74% female turnout.

### Farmer protests represent a potent and potentially exploitable force

Rural Solidarity led major protests beginning February 2024, blocking **260 major roads** and marching ~10,000 farmers to Warsaw. Core grievances centered on EU Green Deal agricultural restrictions and Ukrainian grain imports that had driven down domestic prices after 4.5 million tonnes of Ukrainian grain flooded Polish warehouses in 2022–2023. The protests featured an anti-Ukrainian dimension — banners reading "Putin, get Ukraine, Brussels and our government in order" appeared alongside legitimate economic complaints, prompting Foreign Ministry warnings of possible Russian infiltration. Violence erupted near Parliament on March 6, 2024, with 23 detained. Fresh protests at the Medyka border crossing in November 2024 targeted the EU-Mercosur deal and agricultural tax rates. Academic analysis characterizes these movements as "substitute banner" protests reflecting deeper class frustration, with exploitation potential by far-right groups building broader anti-EU sentiment.

### LGBTQ+ rights: symbolic progress, legislative deadlock

By April 2025, **all "LGBT-free zone" declarations** — which at peak covered roughly a third of Poland — had been withdrawn or invalidated by court order, driven by EU funding withholding and Supreme Administrative Court rulings. A civil partnerships bill was added to the government agenda in July 2024 but has stalled. The ECHR issued yet another judgment against Poland in March 2025 (Andersen v. Poland) finding Article 8 violations for non-recognition of same-sex cohabitation. Public support has shifted markedly: **67% now support** same-sex marriage or registered partnerships, and **54% support full marriage equality**. However, Nawrocki's veto power ensures no legislative action before 2027 at the earliest. Poland ranks at **17.50%** on the ILGA-Europe Rainbow Map, among the worst in the EU.

### The Catholic Church operates as a de facto opposition force

Church-state relations under Tusk represent the most significant tension since the democratic transition. The government reduced religion classes to one hour per week at inconvenient times, prompting the Polish Bishops' Conference to condemn the move as illegal. A new Health Education curriculum replaced Family Life Education in September 2025, triggering a massive conservative protest in Warsaw on December 1, 2024, attended by presidential candidate Nawrocki.

Father Tadeusz Rydzyk's media empire — Radio Maryja, TV Trwam, Nasz Dziennik newspaper, and associated educational institutions — serves as a de facto media arm of the conservative political ecosystem, reaching several million listeners domestically and the Polish diaspora globally. Rydzyk was summoned as a witness in the Justice Fund case in December 2024. The media regulator (under a PiS appointee) fined TVN 142,800 złoty for a critical documentary about Rydzyk, illustrating ongoing institutional protection.

### Diaspora engagement persists but influence has narrowed

The Polish American Congress (PAC) represents approximately **9.15 million Americans** of Polish ancestry through over 3,000 member organizations, but its direct political influence has declined from its Cold War peak, when it successfully lobbied for NATO expansion. The UK Polish community has shrunk from ~1,020,000 to approximately **700,000** post-Brexit. Both diasporas demonstrate strong engagement with Polish domestic politics — half a million diaspora voters registered for the May 2025 presidential election — but exert limited organized influence on host-country policy. KOD (Committee for the Defense of Democracy), with ~8,000 members, has transitioned from mass protest movement to an institutional NGO focused on election monitoring, its relevance diminished by PiS's removal from government.

# Section 14: Alliance history and current partnerships

Poland has emerged as the pivotal state on NATO's eastern flank, combining record defense spending with a sophisticated alliance strategy that simultaneously deepens the US bilateral relationship and builds European autonomous defense capacity. Under the Tusk government's stewardship (with significant complication from President Nawrocki's parallel foreign policy), Warsaw pursues what is best characterized as **alliance maximization** — building redundant security ties within the Western bloc — rather than multi-vector hedging between competing power centers. At **4.7% of GDP** on defense in 2025 (approximately **PLN 186.6 billion / ~$47–51 billion**), Poland is NATO's top proportional spender and is building what will be one of Europe's most powerful conventional militaries.

### The US alliance is paramount but complicated by cohabitation

The Poland-US defense relationship has reached unprecedented depth under Trump 2.0. Approximately **10,000 US troops** are stationed in Poland, with the first permanent base opened in Poznań in March 2023 (V Corps HQ). The "Fort Trump" concept has been revived, with preparation underway in **Bolesławiec** and a logistics hub around Wrocław airport. At a September 2025 White House meeting, Trump told Nawrocki: "We'll put more there if they want."

Poland's US weapons portfolio is massive and accelerating. Current procurement includes **96 AH-64E Apache helicopters** (~$10 billion, making Poland the world's second-largest Apache operator), **250 M1A2 SEPv3 Abrams tanks** (~$4.75 billion, deliveries from January 2025), **32 F-35A Lightning II** ($4.6 billion plus $1.85 billion sustainment, first arrival in Poland expected 2026), **48 F-16 Viper modernizations** ($3.8 billion, contract August 2025), and up to **400 AIM-120D-3 AMRAAM missiles** (~$500 million). A $3.08 billion Foreign Military Financing loan was approved in October 2024 to help fund the Apache purchase.

The diplomatic landscape is complicated by institutional cohabitation. Trump endorsed Nawrocki before the May 2025 presidential election; DHS Secretary Kristi Noem visited Poland to support conservative candidates. Nawrocki has visited Trump at the White House twice — without inviting Foreign Ministry representatives, prompting FM Sikorski to accuse him of "breaching the Constitution." Tusk, meanwhile, manages tensions over Polish deportations (~130 Poles deported in FY2025, nearly double the prior year), digital tax disputes, and a public Musk-Sikorski exchange over Ukraine. The result is effectively dual-track foreign policy: Nawrocki handles the Washington channel with personal rapport, while Tusk coordinates European positions.

### Ukraine support is structurally strong but socially eroding

Poland has delivered **46–48 military aid packages** since February 2022, with total value exceeding **PLN 18 billion (~$4.5 billion)** by March 2025. Equipment transferred includes **318 main battle tanks** (making Poland the global leader in tank transfers to Ukraine), **536 combat vehicles**, **136 artillery systems**, **10 MiG-29 aircraft**, and **10 combat helicopters**. Poland serves as the transit corridor for over **90% of all military aid shipments** to Ukraine.

However, public support has deteriorated sharply. Acceptance of Ukrainian refugees dropped from **94% (March 2022) to 48% (October 2025, CBOS)** — the lowest since 2014. Approximately **46% now say military aid should halt** (June 2025 IBRIS), and support for Ukraine's EU membership has fallen to just **35%**. Roughly 998,000 Ukrainian refugees remain in Poland, generating an estimated **2.7% of GDP** through employment (78% of working-age adults are employed).

The Volhynia historical issue achieved a breakthrough in January 2025 when Tusk and Zelensky agreed on exhumations. The first dig began April 24, 2025 at Puzhnyky village, uncovering remains of 42 Poles — the first exhumation since Ukraine imposed a moratorium in 2017. A second site (Ugły) was approved in October 2025. Both the Tusk government and Nawrocki agree on the core demand but differ on tone: Deputy PM Kosiniak-Kamysz warned that "without commemorating the Polish victims in Volhynia, there is no question of Ukraine joining the EU," while Nawrocki conditions both EU and NATO membership on full resolution.

### The France-Poland Nancy Treaty marks a strategic watershed

On May 9, 2025, Macron and Tusk signed a "Friendship and Strategy Pact" in Nancy — the first such bilateral security treaty between France and an Eastern European state. Article 4.2 commits both parties to provide "mutual assistance, **including by military means**" in the event of armed aggression, going beyond generic NATO/EU obligations. The treaty opens nuclear deterrence discussions: Macron stated he was "ready to open discussions" on nuclear deterrence with European partners, though the use decision remains solely with the French president. Tusk described it as "groundbreaking," noting it "opens up the possibility of cooperation" on nuclear matters. The treaty represents a dramatic rapprochement from PiS-era hostility and signals Poland's willingness to build European autonomous defense capacity alongside the US alliance.

### South Korea has become Poland's transformational defense partner

The Poland–South Korea defense relationship represents the **largest bilateral defense procurement program in recent European history**, with cumulative value exceeding **$25 billion**. Deliveries are proceeding at remarkable speed:

The K2 Black Panther tank program includes **360 tanks** across two contracts (180 in August 2022, 180 in August 2025), with a framework target of up to 1,000. By September 2025, **160 K2GF had been delivered**. The second contract ($6.5 billion) introduces the K2PL variant with domestic production at Bumar-Łabędy in Gliwice. The K9 Thunder howitzer program has completed delivery of all **212 units** under the first contract (December 2025), with a second contract for 152 additional units through 2027 and a framework total of up to 672. The CHUNMOO/Homar-K rocket launcher program has delivered **126 launcher modules** of 290 ordered, with a $4 billion ammunition production joint venture (Hanwha-WB) established for local CGR-080 guided rocket manufacturing. Additionally, **12 FA-50GF fighter trainers** have been delivered, with 36 FA-50PL (Block 20) deliveries beginning 2026. Technology transfer is fundamental to all deals, establishing domestic production capacity across platforms.

### Germany relations are productive but fraught with historical baggage

Chancellor Merz visited Warsaw on his first full day in office (May 7, 2025), hailing a "new beginning in Polish-German relations." First intergovernmental consultations since 2018 were held in July 2024. Germany is Poland's largest trading partner (bilateral trade exceeds **€150 billion annually**). Poland formally joined the German-led European Sky Shield Initiative (ESSI) in 2024, reversing PiS-era opposition.

The reparations issue remains a domestic political tool more than a genuine bilateral negotiation. The Tusk government effectively dropped the formal €1.3 trillion claim, instead pressing Germany to "think creatively" about compensation. Germany offered ~€200 million for surviving victims (rejected as insufficient), established a Berlin institution commemorating Polish victims, and returned 73 medieval documents. Nawrocki, meanwhile, demanded reparations at Westerplatte on September 1, 2025, and during a Berlin visit suggested Germany fund Poland's defense industry as reparation. Foreign Policy described the relationship as one that "needs couples therapy."

### Regional frameworks: V4 is moribund while B9, Three Seas, and Nordic ties flourish

The **Visegrad Four is effectively dead** on core security issues. Poland withdrew its ambassador to Hungary in July 2025 after Budapest granted asylum to fugitive Romanowski. Even PiS-aligned Nawrocki cancelled his bilateral meeting with Orbán in November 2025 after Orbán visited Moscow. FM Sikorski explicitly stated Poland's priority should be the Weimar Triangle and the US, not V4. The "V2+V2" split (Poland+Czechia vs. Hungary+Slovakia on Russia) is entrenched, though the format still functions on lower-priority consensus items like anti-illegal migration and nuclear energy.

The **Bucharest Nine** has expanded its significance. At the June 2025 Vilnius Summit, B9 merged with the Nordic Five for the first time, with leaders pledging to work toward **5% GDP defense spending**. B9 members host all eight NATO enhanced Forward Presence battlegroups. A February 2026 analysis argues Poland's post-2022 success is "intelligible only in the context of its leadership of and participation in the B9."

The **Three Seas Initiative** held its 10th anniversary summit in Warsaw (April 2025), adding Spain and Turkey as strategic partners. The Investment Fund has accumulated ~€1.3 billion and committed over €800 million across nine countries with ~15% annual returns. The initiative oversees **143 priority infrastructure projects** valued at €111 billion, with Via Carpatia, Rail Baltica, and energy interconnectors as flagships.

**Nordic-Polish security cooperation** has deepened markedly since Finland and Sweden joined NATO. All Baltic Sea states except Russia are now NATO members. Poland signed a new strategic partnership with Sweden in November 2024, selected **Saab Kockums** for three submarines in November 2025, and signed a defense cooperation MoU with Norway in September 2025. A Nordic-Baltic training center for unmanned systems operators is planned for Poland. Poland also signed a defense cooperation memorandum with Turkey in December 2025, framed explicitly in Intermarium terms.

The **UK relationship** is advancing toward a bilateral security/defense treaty announced by PM Starmer during his January 2025 Warsaw visit. The MBDA Narew air defense contract (CAMM missiles) is valued at approximately **£4 billion**, with a joint program office in Bristol. Both nations participate in the E5 format (France, Germany, Italy, Poland, UK) emerging as Europe's core defense grouping.

### Alliance strategy assessment: maximization, not hedging

Poland is a **firm transatlantic ally** pursuing alliance maximization rather than multi-vector hedging. It simultaneously deepens ties with the US (primary patron), NATO (institutional anchor), the EU (SAFE mechanism — Poland allocated **€43.7 billion** of €150 billion total), the Weimar Triangle, B9, Nordic/Baltic partners, Three Seas, and the Turkey-Romania trilateral. This represents the construction of redundant security ties within the Western bloc, not balancing between competing powers. Defense Secretary Hegseth chose Poland for his first foreign trip, calling it "a model ally on the continent." FM Sikorski captured the posture precisely: "We have been and will continue to be a loyal ally of America. But we cannot be suckers."

# Section 15: Constitutional crises and regime transition patterns

Poland's democratic trajectory has entered a period of **institutional paralysis** defined by the cohabitation between PM Tusk's governing coalition and PiS-backed President Nawrocki. The Tusk government can legislate but cannot implement reforms requiring presidential cooperation — creating a structural deadlock that will persist until at least the 2027 parliamentary elections. The effort to reverse PiS-era judicial capture has stalled against multiple veto points, while the far-right Confederation party's surge to ~20% combined support represents the most significant structural shift in Polish politics since 1989.

### The 1989 settlement's institutional architecture is under strain

Poland's democratic transition began with the 1989 Round Table Agreements between the Communist PZPR and Solidarity, producing semi-free elections that catalyzed communism's fall. The 1997 Constitution established a parliamentary republic with a directly elected president holding veto power (overridable by 3/5 Sejm supermajority), an independent judiciary, and a Constitutional Tribunal. No constitutional amendments have been enacted since. The system's vulnerability was exposed during PiS's 2015–2023 single-party majority, which demonstrated that constitutional norms could be systematically undermined without formal constitutional change.

### Judicial capture remains substantially unreversed

PiS's judicial capture involved four interconnected mechanisms: packing the Constitutional Tribunal with illegitimate judges (December 2015), politicizing the National Council of the Judiciary by transferring judicial member selection to the Sejm (2017), creating PiS-loyal chambers in the Supreme Court (2018), and appointing approximately **2,500 "neo-judges"** through the politicized KRS process. The government also merged the Justice Minister and Prosecutor General roles under Zbigniew Ziobro and enacted a "muzzle law" (2020) to discipline independent judges.

Justice Minister Adam Bodnar presented an Action Plan to the EU in February 2024, and parliament passed two bills reforming the Constitutional Tribunal and one reforming the KRS. All three were blocked: President Duda referred them to the PiS-controlled CT, which ruled them unconstitutional (final ruling November 20, 2025). The government has responded with a constitutionally controversial "non-recognition" strategy — refusing to publish CT rulings in the Official Journal since March 2024, arguing the CT is illegitimate. The CT president filed a criminal complaint in January 2025.

The Venice Commission issued four major opinions in 2024–2025 providing detailed guidance. It rejected a wholesale "reset" of the CT, favored phased reform, and recommended individualized case-by-case verification of neo-judges rather than blanket invalidation. Despite these frameworks, implementation remains blocked by the presidential veto.

EU funds have nonetheless been released: approximately **€137 billion was unlocked** in early 2024 after the Commission concluded Poland satisfied "super milestones" on judicial independence. The Article 7 procedure was formally **closed on May 29, 2024** — criticized by Human Rights Watch as premature given incomplete reforms.

### Cohabitation without compromise defines the Nawrocki era

Karol Nawrocki won the presidential runoff on June 1, 2025 with **50.89%** versus Trzaskowski's 49.11% — the tightest margin in post-1989 history, approximately 370,000 votes. A 42-year-old historian and former IPN director with no prior political office, Nawrocki ran as nominally independent but was fully PiS-backed and MAGA-aligned, having visited Trump at the White House before the election.

Nawrocki has been significantly more confrontational than his predecessor Duda, deploying the presidential veto at a rate exceeding Duda's entire decade in office. Key vetoed legislation includes: electricity price freeze/onshore wind liberalization (August 2025, termed "legislative blackmail"), Ukrainian refugee benefits extension (August 2025), DSA national enforcement (termed "Ministry of Truth"), MiCA crypto-asset implementation, and the education reform bill (December 2025). He has also blocked military promotions, refused ambassadorial appointments (Poland reportedly still lacks an ambassador to Washington as of early 2026), refused 46 judicial nominations, and proposed beginning discussion on an entirely new constitution by 2030. A United Surveys poll found **62.1% of Poles** feared conflict between Tusk and Nawrocki. The German Marshall Fund characterizes the situation as producing "institutional paralysis."

### The far right has restructured Polish politics

The Confederation party split in January 2025 when Grzegorz Braun was expelled and formed the KKP (Confederation of Polish Crown). Combined far-right polling now reaches approximately **20%** — the strongest showing in post-1989 Polish history. Sławomir Mentzen (b. 1987), an economist and TikTok-savvy communicator with 800,000+ followers, leads the main Konfederacja (KWiN) at ~11–12% in polls. He achieved **14.8%** in the first round of the presidential election — the party's best-ever result, dominating the youth vote. Braun's KKP polls at ~8–9%, with 64% of its voters having previously supported PiS.

The combined far right espouses economically libertarian (flat tax, deregulation, anti-Green Deal), socially conservative (anti-abortion, anti-LGBT, anti-immigration), and EU-skeptic positions. Confederation is positioning for potential coalition participation, reportedly identifying target ministries (migration, finance, digitalization). However, PiS-Confederation relations have soured — Kaczyński condemned Mentzen in September 2025, comparing Confederation's worldview to "Pinochetism."

### PiS succession remains unresolved as 2027 approaches

Jarosław Kaczyński, 76, retains total control of PiS despite polling showing most Poles and many PiS voters believe he should step down. He was scheduled to announce a PM candidate on **March 7, 2026** (today) at a symbolic convention in Kraków. Shortlisted candidates include Zbigniew Bogucki (Presidential Chancellery chief), Przemysław Czarnek (former Education Minister, reported front-runner), Anna Krupka (party vice-chair), and Tobiasz Bocheński (MEP). Notably, Mateusz Morawiecki is explicitly excluded — his faction ("scouts") is marginalized, and he reportedly scheduled a counter-event for March 6.

Current polling (Rzeczpospolita/IBRIS, March 2–4, 2026) shows **KO at 34.2%, PiS at 22.0%, Konfederacja at 14.5%**, suggesting the governing coalition would retain the lead but current coalition partners (PSL at ~3.6%, Polska 2050 at ~2.4%) face elimination below the 5% threshold. The Third Way alliance dissolved in June 2025, and Polska 2050 fractured further in early 2026. A PiS-Konfederacja-KKP right-wing coalition polling at ~48–49% combined is arithmetically plausible but ideologically fraught. Parliamentary elections must be held by November 11, 2027.

### Democratic quality indicators show mixed signals

Freedom House's Freedom in the World 2025 score Poland at **82/100** (up from 80), status "Free." The RSF Press Freedom Index ranks Poland **22nd globally** (up from 24th). V-Dem classifies Poland as an "electoral democracy" but not a "liberal democracy." Freedom House's Nations in Transit score actually declined slightly to **4.43** (from 4.54) due to electoral process flaws and corruption concerns. The closure of Article 7 proceedings and release of EU funds signal institutional confidence, but the CT crisis, neo-judge problem, and cohabitation-induced paralysis represent continuing democratic deficits.

# Section 16: Collective memory, humiliation, and grievance

Historical memory is not merely background context in Polish politics — it is an active, instrumentalized political resource deployed by all major parties with different framing and strategic purpose. The cohabitation between PM Tusk and President Nawrocki, a historian who built his career running the IPN and the WWII Museum in Gdańsk, ensures memory politics will remain central to political contestation through 2027. The Russia-Ukraine war has intensified anti-Russian dimensions while simultaneously straining Polish-Ukrainian relations over Volhynia.

### Partition memory anchors the "victim of great powers" identity

Poland's 123-year absence from the map (1795–1918) under Russian, Prussian, and Austrian partition created a foundational national trauma that continues to shape political discourse. Both major political camps invoke partition memory, but draw opposing conclusions: PiS uses it to justify sovereignty maximalism and EU skepticism, framing Brussels as a potential new partitioning power; KO uses it to justify Euro-Atlantic integration as protection against historical repetition. President Nawrocki, a professional historian, explicitly leverages this narrative, while Tusk reframes it toward European solidarity — stating at Westerplatte in September 2025 that Poland "must never again become a victim of anyone's aggression."

### WWII memory remains the most contested political terrain

The **80th anniversary of the Warsaw Uprising** (August 1, 2024) produced a significant symbolic moment when German President Steinmeier knelt before the commemorative cross — an act acknowledged by then-President Duda as meaningful reconciliation. Annual Katyń commemorations continue, but Russia has escalated provocations: removing Polish flags from the memorial complex (2022), ordering symbol removals (2025), and releasing FSB "archival documents" reviving Soviet-era disinformation blaming Germany. Polish officials now explicitly draw parallels between Soviet crimes against Poles and Russia's crimes in Ukraine.

Holocaust commemoration disputes persist as a deep structural tension. Poland insists it has more Righteous Among the Nations than any other country and that no collaborationist government existed; Yad Vashem counters that "Poles' assistance to Jews during the Holocaust was relatively rare, and attacks against and even the murder of Jews were widespread phenomena." The WWII Museum in Gdańsk remains a paradigm of memory warfare: conceived under Tusk with a universalist approach (advised by Norman Davies and Timothy Snyder), its directorship was seized by PiS, which installed Nawrocki himself as director. Under the restored Tusk government, new director Rafał Wnuk reversed some PiS-era additions in June 2024, triggering backlash even from within the coalition. A 2025 exhibition on Polish soldiers who served in the Wehrmacht drew condemnation from Duda as a "moral provocation."

### The Volhynia issue has achieved diplomatic progress but remains volatile

The 1943–1945 massacres by the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA) killed an estimated 100,000 ethnic Poles. Poland officially designates it "genocide" (2016 Sejm resolution); Ukraine rejects this label. After years of deadlock, the Tusk-Zelensky January 2025 agreement produced the first exhumation since Ukraine's 2017 moratorium — at Puzhnyky, uncovering remains of 42 Poles. A second site was approved in October 2025, and reciprocal permission was granted for Ukrainian exhumation of UPA soldiers in Poland. The Sejm formalized July 11 as "The Volhynia Massacre Day" in July 2025. However, a controversial memorial in Domostawa depicting a baby impaled on a Ukrainian trident was vandalized with UPA symbols in August 2025, with both countries suspecting Russian provocation. Both Tusk and Nawrocki support exhumations but differ on EU accession linkage — Nawrocki has stated he "does not envision Ukraine in either the EU or NATO" until the issue is fully resolved.

### Smolensk conspiracy has lost mainstream salience

The Tusk government abolished PiS's Smolensk reinvestigation commission on December 15, 2023, just two days after taking power. A Defence Ministry investigation found the commission wasted tens of millions of złoty, destroyed a 47-million-złoty Tu-154 aircraft during testing, and that commission head Macierewicz attempted to bribe a British aviation expert £5,000 to alter a report. In August 2025, the Sejm voted **240–178** to strip Macierewicz's immunity, and he was formally charged in September 2025 with disclosing classified information. The conspiracy narrative now functions primarily as a PiS internal loyalty marker rather than a mainstream political issue.

### German reparations have shifted from demand to "creative compensation"

The PiS government's 2022 parliamentary report estimated WWII damages at €1.3 trillion. The Tusk government has acknowledged that formal reparations are legally closed from Germany's perspective, pivoting to requests for symbolic compensation. Germany established a Berlin institution commemorating Polish victims, returned 73 medieval documents, and pledged support for surviving victims — but the €200 million offered was rejected as insufficient. At a December 2025 meeting with Chancellor Merz, Tusk stated that if Germany doesn't compensate ~50,000 surviving victims, "I will consider Poland meeting this need from its own resources." Nawrocki continues demanding reparations at ceremonial occasions. Public opinion remains supportive: **58% favor** seeking reparations despite the government's pragmatic shift.

### Anti-Russian sentiment has reached structural permanence

**GLOBSEC Trends 2025** data shows **86% of Polish respondents** consider Russia a threat — the highest in the region — with only 4% viewing the Kremlin as a strategic partner. CBOS polling shows 72% hold negative attitudes toward Russians (down slightly from a 2023 peak of 82%). The IPN continues active dismantling of Soviet-era monuments: a propaganda monument was removed in Płoty in August 2025, with the IPN declaring "there is no place for Soviet objects in a free Poland." Russia placed Nawrocki on its wanted list in February 2024 for his role in dismantling the Red Army Gratitude Monument. The September 2025 incident in which Poland shot down Russian drones entering its airspace during an attack on Ukraine — the first known instance of a NATO member firing weapons during the invasion — reinforced the existential threat perception.

### The IPN Act's chilling effect persists despite criminal provision removal

The 2018 Amendment to the IPN Act's criminal provisions were removed in June 2018 after international outcry, but civil liability provisions remain in force, and the IPN's expanded mission to "protect the reputation of the Republic of Poland and the Polish Nation" remains legally operative. Academic analysis concludes the law "had an alarming impact on mentalities and the culture of public debate" even after amendment. The removal of criminal provisions paradoxically eliminated the academic research exemption that had been included in the original text, arguably leaving researchers more exposed. Poland-Israel relations remain damaged.

# Sections 17–20: Cross-cutting analysis, key judgments, and watch indicators

## The security-fiscal paradox is Poland's central structural challenge

Poland faces an acute tension between its ambition to build NATO's most powerful European conventional military and the fiscal discipline required by EU membership. Defense spending is set to reach **4.8% of GDP in 2026** (PLN 200 billion, ~€45–55 billion), with an aspiration of 5%. Poland successfully advocated for the NATO summit's July 2025 agreement to raise the alliance spending target to 5% of GDP. Under the EU's SAFE mechanism, Poland was allocated **€43.7 billion** — the single largest national allocation of the €150 billion total.

However, the budget deficit is projected at **6.9% of GDP in 2025** and **6.5% in 2026** — far exceeding the EU's 3% limit. The EU launched an Excessive Deficit Procedure in July 2024, requiring correction by 2028. Public debt has risen to **58.1% of GDP** (Q2 2025, second-fastest increase in the EU) and is projected to exceed the 60% EU limit in 2026, peaking at ~66% including EU RRF loans before returning below threshold by 2030. ING analysis states Poland is "not on the path to correct its excessive deficit." Nawrocki's pledge to veto any tax hikes constrains fiscal consolidation options. Fitch maintains an **A- rating with negative outlook**.

## Demographic decline threatens long-term sustainability

Poland's **fertility rate has fallen to 1.1 children per woman** — a record low and one of the world's lowest. Only **252,000 births** were recorded in 2024 against 409,000 deaths, marking 12 consecutive years of natural population decline. The population fell by 157,000 in 2025 to approximately 37.3 million. The post-retirement-age population now represents **24.2%** of the total, with a dependency ratio of 72 per 100 working-age people. GUS projects the population could fall to **28.4 million by 2060** — a 24% decline — under sustained low fertility assumptions. Immigration (including over 1 million registered foreign workers) provides partial offset, but ZUS has cautioned it is "unrealistic" to assume migration alone can counter the decline. The 800+ złoty child benefit program has failed to halt the trend. Eighteen maternity wards closed in January 2026 alone.

## Energy transition is accelerating but uneven

Coal's share of electricity generation has dropped from **70% in 2022 to 52.8% in 2025**, driven by rapid renewables deployment (**34 GW** of operational wind and solar capacity) and declining coal economics. The first offshore wind farm (Baltic Power, 1.14 GW) is expected to generate first electricity in 2026, with Baltica 2 (1.5 GW) following in early 2027. Total offshore wind targets are 5.9 GW by 2030.

The nuclear program has cleared a critical hurdle: EU Commission approved state aid in December 2025, with PLN 60 billion (~€13.5 billion) in state equity committed and 100% debt guarantees. The final contract with Westinghouse/Bechtel is expected mid-2026, construction beginning 2028, and the first reactor online by **2036–2038** at a total cost of approximately PLN 200 billion (~€45 billion). A second nuclear plant is planned at the former coal site of Bełchatów/Pątnów. Grid operator PSE plans €15 billion in investment (2025–2034) to accommodate the transition, as net electricity demand is projected to surge to 210–230 TWh by 2040.

## Russian hybrid warfare has escalated to unprecedented intensity

Poland faces the most intense Russian hybrid campaign of any EU member state. The December 29, 2025 cyberattack on Polish energy infrastructure — targeting **30+ renewable energy sites** and a CHP plant serving ~500,000 people — represented a significant escalation. Attackers had been present in systems since March 2025, mapping infrastructure before uploading corrupted firmware to substation controllers. Deputy PM Gawkowski called it "an act of Russian sabotage," attributed to the GRU-linked Sandworm/Electrum group. The attack was successfully repelled with no blackout occurring. Poland now faces **2,000–4,000 cyberattacks daily**, with government sector attacks rising from ~2,500/week (October 2025) to ~3,100/week (January 2026).

On the physical frontier, the Belarus border crisis continues with ~30,000 illegal crossing attempts in 2024 and the discovery of **four professionally engineered tunnels** in 2025 — one extending 50 meters on the Belarusian side. Poland has suspended asylum applications at the Belarus border and launched the **Eastern Shield** — a $2.5 billion, 700-km defensive network spanning borders with Russia and Belarus. Over **50 people** suspected of planning sabotage were detained in late 2025. GPS jamming from Russia's Kaliningrad exclave rendered GPS positioning unavailable **~17% of the time** off Gdańsk during summer 2025, with evidence of the "Tobol" electronic warfare network. Poland declared a **no-fly zone** in eastern regions below 3km from March 10 to June 9, 2026.

## Poland's role in Ukraine peace scenarios is pivotal but constrained

Poland's position on peace negotiations rests on three pillars: "nothing about Ukraine without Ukraine" (agreed by both Tusk and Nawrocki on January 9, 2026), territorial decisions require Ukrainian popular consent via referendum, and peace must include "real, reliable security guarantees." Tusk has repeatedly ruled out Polish troop deployment in Ukraine under any scenario. Poland participated in the 35-nation Paris Coalition of the Willing summit on January 6, 2026, with Tusk describing Poland as "one of four leading states" in the peace process.

The Tusk-Nawrocki division of labor assigns the president direct negotiations with Washington and the PM coordination of the European-level Coalition of the Willing. This arrangement, formalized on January 9, 2026, includes agreement to exclude Ukraine/security issues from domestic political disputes. Trump's leaked 28-point plan (November 2025, later revised to 20 points) included a provision for European fighter jets stationed in Poland. Nawrocki told CNBC in January 2026: "Regardless of whether a peace deal is signed or not...Russia will remain a threat to Europe."

## Trump administration impact: Poland as exemplary ally, but not immune

Poland has positioned itself as the **model European ally** for Trump 2.0, leveraging its record defense spending to build diplomatic capital. This has yielded tangible rewards: Trump pledged not to reduce the US military footprint, Poland was invited to the **2026 G20 summit in Miami** (the first invitation, recognizing Poland's economy approaching $1 trillion), and bilateral defense cooperation has deepened across all platforms.

However, Poland has not been immune to Trump-era disruption. The April 2025 "Liberation Day" tariffs imposed 20% on the EU — twice the rate on Russia, as Sikorski noted — with the Polish Economic Institute estimating a 0.4% GDP reduction. The July 2025 EU-US "Turnberry agreement" was an unbalanced trade deal accepted to preserve the defense relationship. Poland was notably **not among the eight NATO allies** targeted with additional tariffs in January 2026 over the Greenland dispute, suggesting Washington views Warsaw distinctly from Western European allies. The February 2026 Supreme Court ruling striking down IEEPA tariffs added further uncertainty, with Trump pivoting to 10% Section 122 tariffs on all countries.

## Key judgments

**1. Institutional paralysis is the defining condition through 2027.** The Tusk-Nawrocki cohabitation produces governance deadlock on judicial reform, social policy, and diplomatic coordination. Neither side can deliver on core promises, creating frustration across the political spectrum.

**2. The far-right surge is structural, not cyclical.** Combined Konfederacja/KKP support at ~20% represents a permanent shift in Polish politics, driven by youth demographics, social media effectiveness, and disillusionment with both PiS and KO. Any post-2027 right-wing government would likely include far-right coalition partners.

**3. Poland's alliance strategy is maximization, not hedging.** Warsaw builds redundant security ties within the Western bloc — not balancing between competing powers. The Nancy Treaty, SAFE allocation, and Nordic partnerships complement rather than substitute for the US relationship.

**4. The demographic-fiscal-security trilemma has no current solution.** Record defense spending driving 6.5% deficits cannot be sustained alongside an aging population requiring expanding social expenditure, absent either tax increases (vetoed) or economic growth significantly above trend.

**5. Russian hybrid warfare will intensify.** The December 2025 energy infrastructure cyberattack, border tunnels, sabotage attempts, and GPS jamming from Kaliningrad demonstrate escalating capability and intent. Poland's frontline exposure makes it NATO's primary hybrid warfare laboratory.
