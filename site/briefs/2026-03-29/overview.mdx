## Week of March 23, 2026

The systems that held the Western order together for decades are failing under pressure, forcing countries to handle crises alone that demand joint action. This is not gradual erosion but sudden breakdown when the systems are most needed.

Ukraine's war has spilled into NATO territory. Ukrainian operations have struck an Estonian power plant, caused explosions on Latvian soil, and sent drones into Lithuanian and Finnish airspace. Each incident has triggered bilateral responses, not joint action. NATO's collective defence machinery, designed for these situations, has not engaged. Instead, frontline allies must balance Ukraine support with domestic security on their own, creating the fragmentation that alliances exist to prevent.

The Iran crisis has sent fuel prices surging globally, but every region is responding separately. Eastern Europe has launched emergency fiscal packages. The Near East and South Asia have introduced their own crisis measures. Asia-Pacific has increased state spending. All address the same shock through separate channels. Economic nationalism has replaced joint action, creating competition and market distortions that worsen the original crisis.

Democratic governance itself may be the cause. Six of seven Western European countries face threats from far-right breakthroughs, constitutional crises, and political breakdown. Eastern Europe adds three more countries with collapsing coalitions. This timing across different democratic systems and electoral cycles suggests deeper forces at work, not coincidence. Governments fighting for survival at home cannot build the consensus that international coordination demands.
