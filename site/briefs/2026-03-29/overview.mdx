## Week of March 23, 2026
The institutions that held the Western order together for decades are failing under pressure, forcing countries to handle crises alone that demand collective response. This is not gradual erosion but sudden breakdown when these institutions are most needed.

The war Ukraine is fighting with NATO's support has produced security incidents on allied soil — accidental strikes on an Estonian power plant, explosions in Latvia, drone incursions into Lithuanian and Finnish airspace. Each incident has triggered bilateral responses, not coordinated ones. NATO's collective defence machinery, designed for exactly these ambiguities, has not engaged. Frontline allies are left to balance their support for Ukraine against domestic security concerns on their own, opening the fractures that alliances exist to prevent.

The Iran crisis has sent fuel prices sharply higher, but every region is responding alone. Eastern Europe has launched emergency fiscal packages. The Near East and South Asia have introduced price caps and subsidies. Asia-Pacific has increased state spending. All address the same shock through separate channels. Economic nationalism has replaced coordination, breeding competition and market distortions that worsen the original crisis.

The fragility of democratic governance compounds the problem. Six of seven Western European countries face far-right breakthroughs, constitutional crises or political breakdown. Eastern Europe adds three more collapsing coalitions. That this is happening across different systems and electoral cycles points to structural forces, not coincidence. Governments struggling to hold power at home cannot build the consensus that international action demands.
