## Items on Watch

Washington is pushing its East Asian allies to shoulder more of their defense burden. Japan, South Korea and Australia face pressure to boost military spending and take on capabilities they have long left to America. This could push these countries toward greater defense independence, particularly if Washington sets specific spending targets or cost-sharing arrangements that go beyond what NATO members face. How China responds will shape what comes next. Chinese military reactions to coordinated alliance infrastructure projects or technology transfers could change how Japan and South Korea weigh the benefits of working together against the risks of provoking Beijing. This dynamic reflects a broader global pattern. Middle powers from India to Turkey to Brazil and Indonesia are trying to balance between the superpowers while guarding their autonomy. If these countries begin coordinating their hedging strategies or building shared institutions, they could create alternatives to the traditional alliance system that neither Washington nor Beijing controls.
