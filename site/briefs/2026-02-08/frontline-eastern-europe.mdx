## Regional Summary

The countries closest to Russia are pushing back against Washington on multiple fronts. Ukraine faces pressure to accept territorial concessions by a deadline it did not set. But the friction extends well beyond Kyiv — Poland's government is openly clashing with the American ambassador, Finland's president has called Washington's foreign policy outdated, and Estonia's president is advocating negotiations with Russia against his own government's position. 

Each confrontation has different triggers, but all reflect the same tension: frontline states know their exposure better than their protectors. None are leaving NATO or abandoning Western orientation. They are testing how much autonomy they can claim within the alliance when their security needs diverge from broader strategy.

Wartime governance is straining institutions built for peacetime consensus. Poland's split between president and prime minister has escalated into open conflict, and Estonia is experiencing its first major foreign policy breach between the two offices since the war began. The Czech Republic and Romania face their own constitutional and coalition pressures. 

Despite shared threat perceptions and growing friction with Washington, frontline states are pursuing parallel rather than coordinated defence strategies. Poland is deepening weapons production with Ukraine while Latvia deploys domestic drones, but no regional defence initiatives have emerged. Proximity to danger, it turns out, breeds national responses rather than regional solidarity.

## Country Summaries

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/ee.svg" width="32" alt="Estonia" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Estonia

Estonia's foreign policy consensus cracked this week when President Alar Karis publicly broke with his government over Russia strategy.

In interviews with NBC and Euronews, Mr Karis said Ukraine should decide for itself about territorial concessions for peace and advocated for EU special envoy participation in negotiations with Russia. Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna swiftly rejected his position, creating the first major public disagreement between Estonia's constitutional institutions since 2022. The controversy raises questions about Mr Karis's potential second term and signals growing domestic pressure for accommodation despite the government's hardline stance.

Even as his president called for dialogue, Mr Tsahkna reinforced his hardline stance. He visited Ukraine to open a resilience centre in Kyiv and continued pushing for EU-wide entry bans on Russian military veterans. Estonia added over 1,000 Russian fighters to the Schengen blacklist while seeking broader European support for the veteran ban initiative. The split reveals an institutional divide over the sustainability of Estonia's threat-maximalist posture toward Russia.

Elsewhere, the government pressed ahead with defence improvements. Defence Minister Hanno Pevkur announced one-third increases to conscript allowances — from 115 to over 150 euros monthly — plus air defence upgrades and a new defence education programme for eighth-graders. The ministry allocated €11 million for conscript allowances in the 2026 budget. Meanwhile, the Bank of Estonia has sold most of its gold reserves while global central banks are buying, keeping only 256.6 kg for historical reasons. Europe's largest battery storage park opened at Kiisa through Estonian-French cooperation, representing €170 million in investment and improving grid stability after desynchronisation from Russia.

The institutional fracture comes as Estonia maintains 5% GDP defence spending and continues its EU policy entrepreneurship on anti-Russian measures, suggesting the unified hawkish consensus that has characterised Estonian governance may be under stress.

Lithuania is attempting to reverse one of its most controversial foreign policy decisions, calling its Taiwan office a costly mistake and signalling a desire to restore relations with China.
