## Regional Summary

All four countries are executing sophisticated multi-alignment strategies in response to US-Iran tensions, pursuing private cooperation with preferred partners while maintaining public autonomy. Turkey coordinates with the US on Syria and Gaza while offering Iran mediation; Saudi Arabia privately encourages US strikes while publicly denying airspace; the UAE declares explicit neutrality; India deepens EU partnership while maintaining multi-vector engagement. This represents parallel evolution from simple hedging to active manipulation of competing relationships. Regional powers have sufficient strategic weight to avoid forced alignment during great power competition, instead extracting maximum advantage from competing suitors. This undermines assumptions about regional polarisation producing clear bloc formation. (moderate confidence; linkage: moderate)

Saudi Arabia's contradictory Iran messaging—private encouragement of US action combined with public denial of airspace access—creates strategic space that the UAE and Turkey are exploiting. The UAE's explicit neutrality declaration becomes more viable because Saudi contradictions reduce pressure for unified Gulf positioning, while Turkey's mediation offer gains credibility precisely because key regional players are demonstrating their own ambivalence about military escalation. Saudi strategic contradictions are inadvertently enabling other regional powers' positioning strategies, suggesting that even leading regional powers cannot unilaterally shape alliance dynamics when partners have independent strategic weight. (moderate confidence; linkage: moderate)

Notably absent this week: Regional security coordination among US partners during Iran crisis escalation. The absence of security coordination despite shared US partnerships suggests regional powers prioritise strategic autonomy over alliance solidarity, even during security crises involving shared adversaries. This indicates structural changes in alliance behaviour under multipolarity.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/tr.svg" width="32" alt="Turkey" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Turkey

Turkey occupies the single most consequential geopolitical position of any middle power, straddling Europe and Asia while controlling the only naval chokepoint between the Mediterranean and Black Seas. Under President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan's hyper-presidential system, Turkey has exploited the vacuum left by US retrenchment to pursue strategic autonomy, but is now selectively deepening alliance partnerships—particularly with Washington on Middle East conflicts through continued Gaza Peace Council coordination and Syria policy alignment. The Kurdish peace process displays both extraordinary symbolic progress (Nationalist Movement Party (MHP) leader Devlet Bahçeli's personal gratitude to Abdullah Öcalan for a carpet gift) and dangerous transactional instrumentalism, with the personal exchanges increasingly confirming the linkage to constitutional amendments rather than genuine reconciliation. Meanwhile, the systematic judicial persecution of Istanbul Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu is generating unintended international costs (Association to Advance Collegiate Schools of Business investigation of Istanbul University), demonstrating how institutional weaponisation can produce consequences beyond intended political targets. With 86 million people, NATO's second-largest military, and a \$1.4 trillion economy, Turkey remains too large to ignore, too strategically located to abandon, and too institutionally compromised to fully trust, but is increasingly revealing the transactional nature of its internal political arrangements while maintaining its bridging role in regional conflicts.

**Key developments:**

- **Domestic:** Istanbul University's cancellation of Mr İmamoğlu's business degree has prompted the Association to Advance Collegiate Schools of Business (global business school accreditation body) to launch an investigation into the university's business faculty. The investigation could result in accreditation loss, representing the first international institutional consequence of the judicial persecution campaign.
- **Domestic:** Mr Bahçeli revealed that the carpet brought by the Democratic Peoples' Party delegation on December 12, 2025 was a personal gift from Mr Öcalan, publicly thanking Mr Öcalan for his "sincerity regarding Turkish-Kurdish unity and brotherhood." This confirms the highly personal dimension of the peace process negotiations.
- **Diplomatic:** Mr Erdoğan emphasised Turkey's readiness to facilitate Iran-US dialogue and stressed full implementation of Syria ceasefire agreements. President Donald Trump and Mr Erdoğan discussed Gaza Peace Board activities and bilateral cooperation expansion.
- **Diplomatic:** Mr Erdoğan spoke with Iranian President Ebrahim Pezeshkian offering Turkey as facilitator for Iran-US dialogue. Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan met Iranian counterpart Abbas Araghchi, opposing military intervention while calling for diplomatic engagement.
- **Security:** Turkish intelligence and police arrested six suspects in a coordinated operation across five provinces, dismantling a network accused of collecting military intelligence and reconnaissance of Incirlik Air Base. The network was directed by Iranian intelligence officers.

Saudi Arabia operates as a hyper-centralised petrostate under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, who has consolidated unprecedented personal authority while attempting ambitious socioeconomic transformation. The kingdom is executing sophisticated triangulation strategy, privately encouraging US action against Iran while publicly maintaining neutrality, demonstrating evolution from simple multi-vector hedging to active manipulation of competing relationships. Realistic adjustment of Vision 2030 ambitions continues with NEOM pivot toward AI infrastructure, while Public Investment Fund asset growth to \$930 billion shows continued sovereign wealth expansion. Growing Saudi-UAE strategic divergence represents fundamental shift in Gulf alliance architecture. Critical vulnerabilities include geographic concentration of oil infrastructure, dependence on desalinated water, and social contract renegotiation dependent on continuous job creation for massive youth demographic.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman told US officials that failing to strike Iran would strengthen Tehran's position, contradicting public Saudi statements urging restraint. Simultaneously, the crown prince told Iranian President Mr Pezeshkian that Saudi Arabia will not allow its airspace for military actions against Iran.
- **Diplomatic:** Multiple analyses highlight Saudi Arabia's shift toward Turkey and Qatar while the UAE aligns more closely with Israel and the US. Divergence includes approaches to Iran normalisation, Israel relations, and regional conflicts in Yemen and Sudan, with December 2025 Saudi airstrikes on UAE-backed forces representing first direct military confrontation.
- **Security:** State Department approved potential \$9 billion sale of 730 Patriot Advanced Capability-3 missiles and related equipment to enhance Saudi integrated air and missile defence capabilities. Congressional notification allows 30-day review period.
- **Economic:** Saudi Arabia significantly downsizing NEOM megaproject including The Line linear city after cost overruns and delays. Kingdom may redirect project toward AI and data centre infrastructure instead of original futuristic city vision.
- **Economic:** Public Investment Fund (PIF) reached \$930 billion in assets and lifted year-long ban on PricewaterhouseCoopers advisory services, while seeking increased domestic investment from wealthy Saudi families to support Vision 2030 projects.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/ae.svg" width="32" alt="United Arab Emirates" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />United Arab Emirates

The UAE navigates intensifying regional polarisation through strategic autonomy, explicitly declaring neutrality on Iran military action while managing continued Saudi rivalry following December 2025 proxy warfare collapse. Major institutional restructuring consolidates \$263 billion sovereign wealth under Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan through L'IMAD creation, accelerating succession planning while maintaining economic statecraft capabilities. Formal conclusion of Yemen mission represents strategic retrenchment acknowledging limits of proxy power projection against stronger regional opponents. AI partnership with the US deepens through G42's K2 Think V2 launch and semiconductor approvals, demonstrating technological sovereignty development within Western alliance framework. Multi-alignment strategy adapts to constrained options as regional blocs harden.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** UAE Foreign Ministry issued official statement that it will not allow its airspace, territory, or territorial waters to be used for any hostile military action against Iran. Statement emphasised dialogue, de-escalation, and diplomatic means for resolving disputes as UAE approach to current crises.
