## Regional Summary

France and Germany are executing coordinated European NATO reconnaissance missions to Greenland in response to President Donald Trump's territorial ambitions, explicitly proceeding without US participation. This represents deliberate alliance framework coordination that excludes the alliance's leading power, demonstrating European capacity for autonomous security responses within NATO structures. Establishes precedent for European security coordination that bypasses US leadership while maintaining NATO institutional framework. Shows European allies can organise collective responses to threats that may emanate from alliance partners themselves.

The UK's first China prime ministerial visit in eight years and Norway's selection of South Korean weapons over US systems both represent strategic hedging moves that occur despite—and perhaps because of—intensifying tensions with the Trump administration. Both countries maintain alliance membership while reducing dependence on US partnerships. Indicates European allies are implementing diversification strategies that reduce US dependency across both diplomatic and procurement channels. This hedging behaviour suggests structural adaptation to unreliable US partnership rather than temporary policy adjustments. (moderate confidence; linkage: moderate)

Notably absent this week: Economic coordination in response to US trade pressure. Expected regional economic coordination in response to US pressure is not materialising. Countries are pursuing bilateral or multilateral solutions rather than European economic solidarity frameworks.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/de.svg" width="32" alt="Germany" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Germany

Germany pursues systematic diversification through trade agreements (the European Union-Mercosur signing) while maintaining European strategic autonomy through coordinated response to Grönland crisis without US participation. Defence transformation continues with record spending but confronts implementation gaps exposing procurement-to-capability disconnect. The export-industrial model achieves trade policy victories that provide geopolitical positioning rather than near-term competitiveness solutions. Domestic legitimacy constraints intensify with the Alternative for Germany (AfD) polling at record highs despite active governance, while European institutional coordination demonstrates collective capacity for managing alliance relationships amid US retrenchment.

**Key developments:**

- **Economic:** European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen signed the EU-Mercosur agreement January 17 creating world's largest free trade area by population (700+ million). However, the European Parliament voted January 21 to refer the agreement to the European Court of Justice for legal review, delaying implementation and prompting Chancellor Friedrich Merz to express regret at institutional obstacles to trade diversification.
- **Economic:** The Bundesbank's February Monthly Report confirms EU-Mercosur and India agreements will have modest macroeconomic effects (under 0.1% additional trade volume) but serve crucial political signalling function that multilateral cooperation remains possible amid protectionist trends. Spain and Portugal expected to benefit disproportionately from Mercosur deal.
- **Diplomatic:** Foreign Minister Alexander Wadephul conducted bilateral meetings with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Congressional leaders January 11-13 to strengthen transatlantic partnership amid Grönland tensions. Meetings emphasised multilateral cooperation and UN role strengthening despite US pressure on European defence burden-sharing.
- **Diplomatic:** Germany deployed 13-soldier reconnaissance team to Grönland January 15-17 on Danish invitation, assessing Arctic security arrangements amid US territorial claims. Mission explicitly coordinated with European NATO partners but conspicuously excludes direct US participation despite formal alliance consultation.
- **Security:** Defence Minister Boris Pistorius acknowledged persistent failures in the D-LBO digitalisation programme despite expected 2026 series integration launch. System breakdown occurred with only 20+ participants, data transmission failures persist, and industry timeline delays force procurement reordering prioritising less critical systems.

The United Kingdom actively implements its bilateral web strategy through the first China leadership visit in 8 years and renewed EU defence engagement, confirming diversification away from US alliance dependence amid continued Mr Trump friction. Domestic regime fragmentation accelerates with high-profile Conservative defections to Reform UK (now 8 MPs), explicit Conservative abandonment of centrism, and escalating Labour factional warfare including blocking potential leadership rivals. **Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** Prime Minister Keir Starmer met President Xi Jinping in Beijing, announcing visa-free travel for UK citizens, reduced whisky tariffs, and AstraZeneca's £10.9bn China investment. Mr Trump called UK-China business 'very dangerous' and threatened to pivot to Canada with similar warnings.

- **Domestic:** Former Home Secretary Suella Braverman and Robert Jenrick defected to Reform UK. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch responded by explicitly stating centrist ideas are no longer wanted in the party.

France continues navigating structural governance instability while maintaining international positioning as leader of European resistance to American pressure. Domestically, the government depends entirely on Article 49.3 to pass basic legislation, with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu using the mechanism for the third time on the 2026 budget while carefully managing 2027 electoral calendar pressures. The July 7, 2026 Marine Le Pen appeal verdict remains the critical binary event determining 2027 election dynamics, with tactical manoeuvring accelerating across the political spectrum. Internationally, France sustains its confrontational stance toward US coercion through sharp diplomatic disagreements with NATO leadership, strategic territorial solidarity (Greenland consulate), and innovative communication strategies, while continuing multilateral responsibilities through G7 presidency. The central tension persists between France's European leadership ambitions and domestic political fragmentation that undermines institutional credibility.

**Key developments:**

- **Domestic:** PM Lecornu engaged government responsibility for third time using Article 49.3 to force through the 2026 state budget without parliamentary vote. Both France Unbowed (LFI) and the National Rally (RN) filed new censure motions, bringing total to eight motions during budget process, all of which failed without Socialist and Republican support.
- **Domestic:** Ms Le Pen's appeals trial continued with her abandoning conspiracy defence in favour of acknowledging possible unintentional errors. The July 7, 2026 verdict will determine whether her 5-year ban from public office stands, directly affecting her eligibility for the 2027 presidential election.
- **Domestic:** Mr Lecornu announced plans for government 'adjustment' after budget adoption, addressing ministers running in municipal elections and aiming to disconnect government from approaching 2027 presidential campaign. Several ministers expected to leave for electoral campaigns.
- **Domestic:** Jean-Luc Mélenchon used the term 'grand remplacement' to describe generational demographic change, deliberately co-opting far-right terminology to promote the LFI's 'new France' concept. The tactical provocation drew criticism across the spectrum while Mr Mélenchon claimed to be 'trapping' the extreme right.
- **Diplomatic:** Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot directly contradicted NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte's statement that Europe cannot defend itself without US support, declaring 'Europeans can and must take charge of their security' and citing the European pillar of NATO.
