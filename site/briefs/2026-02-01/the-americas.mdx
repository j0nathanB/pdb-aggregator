## Regional Summary

Canada, Mexico, and Brazil are simultaneously managing intensified US engagement through distinctly different approaches — Canada sustaining institutional challenges while offering selective bilateral cooperation, Mexico employing tactical accommodation combined with sovereignty assertions, and Brazil maintaining strategic multi-alignment positioning. Each country's approach reflects its specific structural constraints and domestic political requirements rather than coordinated strategy. This reveals the absence of coordinated regional responses to US pressure, with middle powers in the hemisphere pursuing independent bilateral strategies rather than multilateral frameworks. The divergent approaches suggest different assessments of leverage and risk tolerance vis-à-vis the Trump administration. (moderate-high confidence; linkage: weak)

Notably absent this week: Coordinated regional responses to US policy through multilateral institutions. The absence of institutional regional coordination suggests either weakened regional institutional capacity or strategic preference for bilateral management of US relationships over multilateral frameworks.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/ca.svg" width="32" alt="Canada" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Canada

Canada is sustaining its most significant strategic reorientation since the 1940s despite escalating US pressure, demonstrating this represents strategic rather than tactical positioning. The Carney government explicitly refuses to back down from challenging Western institutional effectiveness while maintaining selective bilateral cooperation. Domestically, the government shows responsiveness to cost-of-living pressures through major affordability initiatives while the Conservative opposition consolidates under Pierre Poilievre's strengthened leadership. However, traditional cleavage systems continue to activate — particularly English-French tensions — confirming persistent institutional fragmentation even during external threat periods. Democratic stability continues through established constitutional processes.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** Prime Minister Mark Carney explicitly denied walking back his Davos remarks to President Donald Trump, stating "I meant what I said in Davos" during a 30-minute phone conversation. Mr Trump threatened 100% tariffs if Canada pursues a China deal, despite Mr Carney clarifying no free trade agreement is planned. US Treasury Secretary claimed Mr Carney "aggressively" walked back comments, which Mr Carney flatly denied.
- **Diplomatic:** Mr Carney publicly called Kevin Warsh "a fantastic choice" for Fed chair, highlighting their past working relationship when Mr Carney led the Bank of Canada. This represents continued selective engagement with the US despite broader bilateral tensions.
- **Domestic:** Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre won leadership review with 87.4% approval — higher than Stephen Harper's 84% in 2005 after his first election loss. Result strengthens his position despite recent defections to Liberals and eliminates questions about his viability following the 2025 electoral defeat.
- **Domestic:** Mr Carney announced \$3.1 billion first year, expanding over five years, featuring expanded Goods and Services Tax (GST) credit renamed "Canadian Groceries and Essentials Benefit" for 12+ million Canadians. Includes one-time 50% top-up this year and \$500 million Strategic Response Fund for business supply chain disruptions.
- **Domestic:** Mr Carney described 1759 Battle of Plains of Abraham as beginning of "partnership between two founding peoples," causing unanimous criticism across Quebec's political spectrum. Bloc Québécois leader Yves-François Blanchet demanded apology, Parti Québécois leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon said it contradicted historical fact, even interim Quebec Liberal leader called it "clumsy."

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/mx.svg" width="32" alt="Mexico" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Mexico

Mexico operates as a periphery middle power locked into asymmetric structural integration with the United States through overwhelming economic dependency (83% export concentration) while pursuing calibrated sovereignty boundary-setting. President Claudia Sheinbaum continues managing the bilateral relationship through tactical flexibility — operationally accommodating US pressure (Cuba oil suspension) while maintaining sovereignty rhetoric, with President Donald Trump's public praise suggesting this approach is succeeding in managing tensions. The National Regeneration Movement (Morena) government maintains genuine popular support but faces intensifying coalition management challenges as allied parties resist key institutional reforms, requiring public unity declarations and leadership transitions to manage internal strain. The military has consolidated unprecedented commercial and civilian governance roles that transcend electoral cycles.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** Pemex cancelled planned crude oil shipments to Cuba after Bloomberg reported removal of January delivery from export schedules. Ms Sheinbaum defended the decision as "sovereign" while facing US threats of tariffs on countries supplying oil to Cuba. The last shipment of 86,000 barrels of high-quality Istmo and Olmeca crude arrived at Havana in mid-January. Mexico had become Cuba's primary oil supplier after Venezuela reduced shipments.
- **Diplomatic:** Ms Sheinbaum and Mr Trump held multiple phone calls discussing security cooperation, trade, and bilateral relations. Mr Trump publicly praised Ms Sheinbaum following their conversations. Ms Sheinbaum stated they "coincided that we're going very well" on security matters and discussed commercial themes. The tone represents a marked improvement from earlier bilateral tensions.
- **Diplomatic:** Foreign Minister Juan Ramón de la Fuente reaffirmed Mexico's commitment to providing humanitarian aid globally, stating it should not be subject to political or economic interests. He emphasised there is "no subordination" in Mexico-US relations while defending aid to Cuba. The Foreign Minister stressed that Mexico maintains cooperation but never subordination with the US.
- **Domestic:** The governing coalition parties signed a unity declaration to maintain their alliance for 2027 elections amid acknowledged tensions over electoral reform. Leaders explicitly addressed opposition expectations of seeing them divided. The Labour Party (PT) indicated it would not support electoral reform representing "regression," while the Ecologist Green Party of Mexico (PVEM) and the PT maintain conditions for their support. Electoral reform negotiations continue with Gobernación Secretary mediation.
- **Domestic:** Adán Augusto López stepped down as Morena's Senate coordinator during the installation of the second ordinary session period, with Ignacio Mier unanimously chosen as replacement. Mr Mier stated his main task will be advancing electoral reform while maintaining dialogue with the PT and PVEM allies. Mr López transitions to territorial party strengthening for 2027.
