## Regional Summary

Multiple frontline countries are simultaneously implementing unprecedented defense modernisation through European Union Security Action for Europe (SAFE) programme funding mechanisms. Estonia secured EU approval for defense investment plans, Romania announced €16.6 billion in SAFE funding representing potentially the largest defense transformation in its modern history, while Poland's presidential veto blocked €44 billion in SAFE funding despite maintaining defense modernisation commitments. This represents coordinated European institutional capacity-building for frontline defence. The EU SAFE programme is emerging as the primary mechanism for European defence integration on the eastern flank, creating shared funding sources and coordination mechanisms that transcend national defence planning. Poland's domestic political obstruction of SAFE funding reveals how constitutional crises can disrupt coordinated European defence initiatives. (moderate confidence; linkage: strong)

Frontline NATO members are deepening military integration through multinational exercises and coordination mechanisms. Poland's NATO Steadfast Dart exercise included Romanian force integration, while Baltic states formalised joint military mobility agreements. This represents operational-level alliance integration beyond traditional bilateral cooperation. NATO's eastern flank is evolving from individual national defence preparations to integrated multinational operational capacity. This creates interaction effects where each country's military capabilities enhance neighbouring countries' security through shared defence architecture. (moderate confidence; linkage: moderate)

Notably absent this week: Energy security coordination mechanisms given shared Russian energy independence challenges. Country desks may have collection gaps on energy coordination, or regional energy cooperation may be occurring at technical rather than political levels that don't register in political assessments.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/pl.svg" width="32" alt="Poland" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Poland

Poland demonstrates sophisticated dual-track alliance management, with cohabitation enabling President Andrzej Nawrocki and European Union coordination simultaneously, though former Justice Minister Zbigniew Ziobro's asylum confirms definitive break with Hungary. Economic outperformance (3.5% growth, Group of Twenty qualification) coexists with fiscal sustainability crisis (6.3% deficit, highest in EU) and SAFE veto creating €44 billion defence financing gap. Constitutional crisis escalated to operational level with competing Constitutional Tribunal judge appointment procedures and National Council of the Judiciary reform deadlock, potentially reshaping institutional balance by end-2026 depending on alternative oath-taking resolution.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** Mr Nawrocki attended President Donald Trump's Board of Peace launch in Davos but did not sign, demonstrating sophisticated diplomatic hedging. Prime Minister Donald Tusk's constitutional objection provided legal cover while maintaining US relations. The 'fragile truce' between Mr Tusk and Mr Nawrocki in foreign affairs enabled dual-track positioning: Mr Nawrocki managing the Trump administration relationship while Mr Tusk secured European partnerships.
- **Diplomatic:** Hungary granted political asylum to Mr Ziobro facing 26 criminal charges, representing the first such EU-internal asylum case. Hungary simultaneously modified surrender laws to block European Arrest Warrants against recognised refugees. Mr Ziobro thanked Prime Minister Viktor Orbán while claiming to fight 'creeping dictatorship' in Poland.
- **Economic:** European Commission forecasts Poland GDP growth at 3.5% in 2026, highest in EU after Malta, but fiscal deficit projected at 6.3% of GDP - highest in the EU. The International Monetary Fund warns debt rising toward 76% of GDP by 2030. Poland achieves world's 20th largest economy status, qualifying for G20 participation. The National Bank of Poland (NBP) expected to continue monetary easing with inflation contained at 2.9%.
- **Economic:** President Nawrocki vetoed Poland's €43.7 billion SAFE implementation bill, blocking access to EU's largest defence loan allocation. The Tusk government launched 'Plan B' to access funds through alternative mechanisms. Mr Nawrocki proposed 'SAFE 0%' using NBP gold reserve revaluation profits, dismissed by the finance minister as unfeasible given NBP losses.
- **Domestic:** The Sejm elected six new Constitutional Tribunal judges in March 2026 despite Law and Justice (PiS) procedural objections. President Nawrocki invited only two judges (Ms Szostek and Ms Bentkowska) to take oath, creating constitutional crisis over whether presidential swearing-in is ceremonial or substantive. Justice Minister Adam Żurek prepared 'Plan B' involving alternative oath-taking procedures.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/cz.svg" width="32" alt="Czech Republic" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Czech Republic

The Czech Republic's democratic resilience faces its most severe test since 1989 as the Foreign Minister Petr Macinka blackmail scandal triggered a full constitutional crisis between the presidency and government. While structural Western integration (F-35, KHNP nuclear, EU economic dependence) remains irreversibly embedded, the breakdown of executive coordination creates unprecedented dysfunction in foreign policy formulation. However, the immediate mobilisation of 90,000 protesters and opposition demands for no-confidence votes demonstrate that Czech institutional safeguards extend beyond formal governmental mechanisms to include robust civil society resistance. The crisis validates the cohabitation tensions predicted in the dossier while confirming that democratic resistance mechanisms activate effectively when constitutional norms are directly threatened.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** President Petr Pavel publicly accused Mr Macinka of attempted blackmail over Filip Turek's ministerial appointment. Mr Pavel released text messages where Mr Macinka threatened consequences if Mr Turek wasn't appointed Environment Minister, stating Mr Pavel would only have 'peace' if he agreed. Mr Pavel referred the matter to security services for criminal investigation.
- **Institutional:** Approximately 90,000 people demonstrated in Prague on February 1 supporting President Pavel against the Prime Minister Andrej Babiš coalition's alleged blackmail attempts. The mass mobilisation occurred in response to the constitutional crisis triggered by Mr Macinka's threatening messages to the presidency.
- **Domestic:** The cohabitation between Mr Pavel and Mr Babiš reached crisis levels when Mr Macinka's blackmail attempt triggered criminal investigation referral and 90,000-person protests. Opposition parties demanded Mr Macinka's dismissal and scheduled no-confidence votes, while Mr Babiš defended his Foreign Minister despite acknowledging the messages were 'unfortunate.'
- **Unexpected:** Foreign Minister directly threatens Czech President with blackmail over ministerial appointments.
- **Notable absence:** Normal diplomatic coordination between President and Foreign Minister — The absence of basic executive coordination mechanisms shows cohabitation tensions have moved beyond policy disagreement into constitutional crisis

Finland has undergone the most dramatic strategic realignment in its modern history since 2022, pivoting from decades of military non-alignment to NATO's most heavily militarised frontline member with the Alliance's longest Russian border. The country now fields one of Europe's largest reserve armies (280,000 wartime strength expanding toward 1 million) and spends 2.5% of GDP on defence with a target of 3% by 2029. Diplomatically, Finland demonstrated mature engagement with China while firmly holding Western positions on Ukraine, and coordinated Alliance responses to territorial revisionism. The audit revealing significant gaps between claimed (10 billion) and actual (3.5-5 billion euros) government savings underscores persistent fiscal pressures under the EU Excessive Deficit Procedure. Domestically, the right-wing Prime Minister Petteri Orpo government maintains stability while the leading opposition Social Democratic Party (SDP) faces a major internal crisis over harassment allegations that could affect 2027 electoral dynamics. Finland's institutional framework remains among the world's strongest, demonstrating sophisticated diplomatic engagement and continued NATO integration.

Estonia is a digitally advanced Baltic microstate whose entire strategic orientation constitutes a continuous response to the existential problem of surviving beside Russia without sufficient mass to defend itself alone. This week demonstrated Estonia's evolution from stress-tested alliance solidarity to active policy entrepreneurship within EU frameworks, as Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna led bloc-wide initiatives against Russian war veterans and publicly rejected accommodation discourse from within the alliance. Estonia continues spending 5% of GDP on defence while achieving the world's highest density of unicorn companies and ranking 2nd globally in press freedom, now using its deep institutional embedding to shape European responses to Russian aggression rather than merely adapting to external pressures.
