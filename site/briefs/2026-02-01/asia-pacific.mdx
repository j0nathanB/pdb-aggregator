## Regional Summary

Regional economies are experiencing dramatically different outcomes despite shared exposure to global conditions. Japan and South Korea achieve record performance through semiconductor dominance and fiscal expansion, while Indonesia faces its worst crisis since 1998 with \$80 billion stock routs and institutional capture concerns. Australia uniquely confronts inflation persistence requiring monetary tightening reversal. These divergent trajectories reflect different structural vulnerabilities and policy frameworks rather than coordinated responses. This divergence reveals that Asia-Pacific lacks economic integration mechanisms that would produce more uniform responses to global pressures. Countries' different institutional frameworks and economic structures are producing increasingly asymmetric outcomes that could affect regional stability and cooperation patterns.

Japan's unprecedented explicit linkage between alliance survival and Taiwan response is creating coordination pressures while South Korea simultaneously pursues 'full-scale restoration' with Beijing. Taiwan continues deepening US economic integration through the 6th Economic Partnership and Prosperity Dialogue framework. This creates a regional tension where Japan's explicit contingency articulation occurs alongside South Korea's Beijing rapprochement, potentially complicating unified alliance responses. Japan's shift from deterrence ambiguity to explicit contingency articulation represents a fundamental change in regional alliance dynamics that other US partners must now respond to. South Korea's simultaneous Beijing engagement creates potential alliance coordination challenges if contingencies emerge. (moderate confidence; linkage: moderate)

Notably absent this week: Coordinated regional response to President Donald Trump's transactional alliance approach. Despite facing similar transactional pressure from Washington, regional countries show no evidence of coordination in their response strategies, suggesting limited institutionalised consultation mechanisms for managing shared alliance challenges.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/jp.svg" width="32" alt="Japan" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Japan

Japan is consolidating domestic political strength while escalating international assertiveness under Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's leadership. The Liberal Democratic Party's (LDP) polling trajectory toward potential single-party majority demonstrates exceptional political confidence, enabling increasingly provocative positioning on Taiwan contingencies and aggressive fiscal expansion. Ms Takaichi's explicit linkage between alliance survival and Taiwan response represents unprecedented public articulation of operational red lines, moving beyond traditional deterrence ambiguity. Economic policy combines ¥5 trillion consumption tax elimination pledges with sustained wage coordination, pursuing fiscal expansion to consolidate escape from deflation. The combination of domestic dominance and international assertiveness suggests Japan is transitioning from reactive alliance management to proactive strategic positioning.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** Prime Minister Takaichi stated the Japan-US security alliance would 'collapse' if Tokyo did nothing when American forces were attacked during a Taiwan crisis evacuation. She emphasised the 110km distance between Taiwan and Japan, stating 'we might take joint action' to rescue Japanese and Americans, and that this distance 'is roughly the same' as Tokyo to Atami. This represents her most specific articulation of Taiwan contingency scenarios.
- **Diplomatic:** Prime Minister Keir Starmer met Ms Takaichi in Tokyo, agreeing to strengthen supply chains for critical minerals and expand industrial cooperation in wind, quantum and nuclear fusion power. They confirmed coordination among like-minded countries toward bolstering supply chains amid growing concerns about China's export restrictions on critical minerals. Mr Starmer invited Ms Takaichi to visit the UK this year.
- **Economic:** Ms Takaichi pledged to eliminate consumption tax on food and beverages for two years, potentially implementing within fiscal 2026 if cross-party consensus is reached by summer. The policy would cost approximately ¥5 trillion annually. However, she provided minimal funding details beyond 'reviewing subsidies' and went silent on the topic during subsequent campaign speeches, creating market uncertainty and voter scepticism.
- **Economic:** Japan Business Federation (Keidanren) Chairman Masakazu Tokura and Japanese Trade Union Confederation (Rengo) President Tomoko Yoshino agreed on maintaining wage increase momentum, with Keidanren supporting pay scale increases as 'the standard' for 2026 negotiations. Both sides emphasised the importance of spreading 5%+ wage increases to small and medium-sized companies. This follows 2025 achievements of over 5% increases for the second straight year.
- **Economic:** Ms Takaichi highlighted benefits of yen weakness in campaign speeches, citing export competitiveness and government foreign exchange reserve performance. She later clarified that she had 'no preference for the yen's direction' after market concerns. The comments contrasted with Finance Ministry efforts to counter excessive volatility.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/tw.svg" width="32" alt="Taiwan" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />Taiwan

Taiwan continues consolidating its strategic position through sustained US economic cooperation and indigenous defence capability development, with the 6th Economic Partnership and Prosperity Dialogue advancing supply chain coordination and the Hai Kun submarine achieving critical submerged trial milestones. While lacking the dramatic breakthroughs of the previous assessment period's trade agreement, this week demonstrated institutional stability in Taiwan's external relationships despite persistent domestic political gridlock. The Kuomintang (KMT)-Taiwan People's Party (TPP) legislative coalition continues obstructionist tactics while the TPP adapts to post-Ko leadership under Huang Kuo-chang, maintaining established patterns of institutional crisis without fundamental change.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** Under Secretary for Economic Affairs Jacob Helberg led the 6th Economic Partnership and Prosperity Dialogue on January 27, resulting in the American Institute in Taiwan (AIT) and Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Office (TECRO) signing a joint statement on the Pax Silica Declaration and cooperation on economic security. The dialogue focused on supply chain security across the AI tech stack, trusted digital infrastructure, unmanned aircraft systems certification, critical minerals cooperation, and addressing tax-related investment barriers.
- **Security:** Taiwan's first indigenous submarine Hai Kun (SS-711) successfully completed its first submerged sea trials on January 29, marking a critical milestone after pre-submerge tests on January 26. Initial trials focused on snorkel and periscope-depth operations at 10-20m depth to verify watertight integrity, underwater trim, and stability. Despite delivery delays (originally scheduled for November 2025), CSBC Corporation continues to face daily fines but targets June 2026 delivery.
- **Security:** On February 25, the Ministry of National Defence detected 28 People's Liberation Army aircraft sorties including J-10, J-16, KJ-500, and drones, with 22 sorties crossing the median line into Taiwan's northern, central, and southwestern airspace. These aircraft coordinated with People's Liberation Army Navy vessels under the pretext of 'joint combat readiness patrol.' The Armed Forces employed joint intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance means and dispatched aircraft, vessels, and shore-based missile systems to respond.
- **Domestic:** Huang Kuo-chang officially assumed TPP chairmanship on January 1, 2025, replacing Ko Wen-je who resigned in December 2024 after corruption indictment. The party continues positioning as anti-establishment alternative focusing on economic issues affecting the 'sandwich generation' while Mr Ko remains detained. The party maintains unity around Mr Ko's founding principles while adapting to new leadership dynamics.
- **Domestic:** Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) caucus warned that the KMT-TPP majority would likely advance at least four controversial bills including amendments to the Act Governing Properties Improperly Obtained by Political Parties, Offshore Islands Development Act, Satellite Broadcasting Act, and Organic Act of the Legislative Yuan. The party characterised these as serving specific interests rather than social fairness and pledged to block passage.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/kr.svg" width="32" alt="South Korea" style={{display: 'inline', verticalAlign: 'middle', position: 'relative', top: '-2px', marginRight: '8px', border: '1px solid #d1d5db', borderRadius: '2px'}} />South Korea

South Korea's multi-vector hedging strategy demonstrates operational effectiveness through President Lee Jae-myung's simultaneous Beijing rapprochement ('full-scale restoration' rhetoric from January 2026 China visit) and management of President Donald Trump's transactional alliance enforcement (tariff escalation to 25% over legislative compliance). The framework validates both the strategic ambiguity approach and its structural limits when domestic processes intersect with alliance obligations. Economic fundamentals remain robust with Samsung's record Q4 profits (operating profit tripled) confirming AI-driven semiconductor dominance, while Mr Lee's sustained real estate normalisation pressure signals continued capital allocation optimisation. Defence export relationships continue expanding European patron-client bonds through Norway's \$2B Hanwha deal, diversifying strategic portfolio beyond Northeast Asia. Domestic governance position strengthens with 53.1% sustained approval while institutional accountability from martial law crisis continues through Kim Keon Hee's 20-month sentence, demonstrating ongoing judicial processing with moderated penalties. Presidential ceremonial authority reinforces progressive lineage through highest civilian honours for democratic mentor Lee Hae-chan.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** Mr Lee's January 4-7, 2026 state visit to Beijing - the first Korean presidential visit in six years - included meetings with President Xi Jinping and resulted in Mr Lee declaring his intention to make '2026 the first year of full-scale restoration of Korea-China relations.' The visit was accompanied by a 200+ business delegation including Samsung, SK, Hyundai, and LG chairmen. Fourteen cooperation agreements were signed across technology, transportation, trade, and economy.
- **Diplomatic:** Mr Trump announced on social media plans to raise tariffs on South Korean exports from 15% back to 25%, specifically citing Korea's legislature not affirming their summer agreement. The People Power Party urged Mr Lee to directly contact Mr Trump. Opposition leader Jang Dong-hyuk criticised the government's response, saying 'We have a prime minister boasting about having a hotline number, and a president who only vents anger while blaming the National Assembly that his party controls.'
- **Economic:** Samsung Electronics announced record Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 93.8 trillion with operating profit of KRW 20.1 trillion - more than tripling year-over-year. The Memory Business achieved record quarterly revenue and operating profit driven by AI chip demand, expanded high-bandwidth memory sales, and overall market price surge. SK Hynix's high-bandwidth memory products with industry-leading 11.7Gbps performance are beginning delivery in Q1 2026.
- **Economic:** Mr Lee announced the government will not extend current exemptions from higher capital gains taxes for multiple-home owners, with relief set to expire May 9. He also proposed the 'Stock Price Suppression Prevention Act' to address inheritance tax manipulation. The People Power Party criticised these policies as targeting property owners rather than offering effective solutions, with opposition spokesperson calling Mr Lee's rhetoric 'highly inappropriate for a president.'
- **Domestic:** Former first lady Kim Keon Hee received a 20-month prison sentence for accepting luxury gifts including Chanel bags and diamond necklace from Unification Church officials in exchange for political favours. The sentence was lighter than prosecutors sought but marks the first time a presidential spouse has been indicted while detained. She faces additional trials on other charges.
