## Regional Summary

Western Europe's governments are falling apart. France has political violence, Britain faces constitutional breakdown, and coalitions are cracking in Germany, Italy, Spain and Sweden. Yet they are pulling away from Washington — criticising American policy and building their own defences.


Notably absent this week: any EU response to the political chaos across member states. Either EU institutions are paralysed or we're missing their activities entirely.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/fr.svg" alt="France flag" style={{ width: "24px", height: "18px", display: "inline", verticalAlign: "middle", marginRight: "8px" }} /> France

France's political crisis turned violent this week with the fatal beating of Quentin Deranque, a 23-year-old far-right activist, in Lyon. Police charged multiple suspects linked to Jean-Luc Mélenchon's France Unbowed party, including parliamentary assistants, sparking bitter confrontations between Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu and opposition leaders. The National Assembly held a minute of silence, but the killing has shattered civil discourse ahead of the 2027 election. Marine Le Pen will learn her fate on July 7, when the Paris appeals court decides whether five years of ineligibility for embezzlement will bar her from running for president.

The violence affected France's foreign relations. Jean-Noël Barrot, the foreign minister, summoned the American ambassador after the Trump administration commented on the Deranque investigation, condemning what he called the use of a domestic tragedy for politics. Emmanuel Macron separately wrote to Donald Trump demanding he lift sanctions on former EU Commissioner Thierry Breton and ICC judge Nicolas Guillou, arguing that the measures violate European autonomy. During a visit to India, Mr Macron called social media platforms' free speech defence "pure bullshit" and demanded transparent algorithms, setting up a clash with the Trump administration's tech policies.

Not all of France's international moves were defensive. Mr Macron's three-day trip to India raised the relationship to "Special Global Strategic Partnership" status, with a roadmap for defence cooperation that could include 114 additional Rafale fighters worth \$40 billion. Both leaders cast the partnership as vital for global stability. At home, the defence ministry created a robotics and artificial intelligence laboratory called LARIAD and deployed GenIAl.intradef, a sovereign alternative to ChatGPT, to fight what officials call "Shadow AI." But France's infrastructure showed its weaknesses when hackers accessed a national banking database through stolen civil servant credentials, compromising data on 1.2 million accounts.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/de.svg" alt="Germany flag" style={{ width: "24px", height: "18px", display: "inline", verticalAlign: "middle", marginRight: "8px" }} /> Germany

Germany gave its intelligence service sweeping new powers this week in the most significant overhaul since the Cold War, while Chancellor Friedrich Merz consolidated his authority and the far-right opposition faced its gravest credibility crisis.

Thorsten Frei, the chancellery chief, announced plans to give the BND authority to conduct cyber-attacks, sabotage operations abroad, and armed operations. The reforms shift German intelligence from passive information gathering to active operational capabilities, aiming to make Germany "a credible intelligence partner" that can respond to threats rather than simply observe them. The change breaks decisively with decades of post-war restraint on intelligence operations.

Mr Merz strengthened his position within the Christian Democratic Union at the party congress in Stuttgart, winning re-election as chairman with 91.2% of delegates' votes — an improvement from 89.8% last year despite governing difficulties. The stronger showing consolidates his authority to pursue strategic independence from US leadership, a doctrine he has explicitly articulated as rejecting American cultural politics and protectionism.

Meanwhile, the Alternative for Germany confronts a systematic nepotism scandal that threatens its anti-establishment credibility precisely when the party stands poised for governing power in eastern states. Multiple AfD politicians across Saxony-Anhalt, Lower Saxony, and North Rhine-Westphalia have been accused of employing family members in parliamentary offices and misusing taxpayer funds. The party has initiated disciplinary proceedings, but the scandal undercuts its central message. Lower Saxony's constitutional protection service compounded the pressure by upgrading the AfD state branch from a "suspected case" to a "confirmed extremist organisation," joining eastern German states in this classification and enabling expanded surveillance.

Boris Pistorius, the defence minister, faced political controversy over plans to spend €540 million on kamikaze drones from companies including Stark Defence, which counts Peter Thiel as a minority shareholder. Parliamentary critics raised concerns about Mr Thiel's anti-democratic views, though the ministry dismissed the investment as unproblematic. Federal President Frank-Walter Steinmeier conducted routine diplomatic visits to Lebanon and Jordan, meeting officials and visiting a German naval vessel participating in UN peacekeeping.
