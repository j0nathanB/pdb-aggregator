### Russia strikes Ukraine energy grid before peace talks

KYIV, Ukraine — Russia struck Ukraine's energy infrastructure with hundreds of drones and missiles Tuesday, breaking what President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said was a commitment to halt such attacks before planned peace talks. The bombardment included 450 long-range drones and 70 missiles targeting at least five regions, wounding at least 10 people and leaving 1,170 apartment buildings in Kyiv without heating, officials said. "Taking advantage of the coldest days of winter to terrorize people is more important to Russia than diplomacy," Zelenskyy said, adding that Ukraine believes the strike "clearly violates what the American side discussed." The attack came a day before scheduled talks in Abu Dhabi between Russian and Ukrainian delegations, with NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte calling the strikes "a really bad signal" during his visit to Kyiv. A Kremlin official had said last week that Russia agreed to halt strikes on Kyiv until Feb. 1 following a personal request from U.S. President Donald Trump to Russian President Vladimir Putin.

### Zelenskyy says energy accident hit Kyiv, central Ukraine hardest

KYIV, Ukraine — President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Friday that Kyiv and central Ukraine bore the most severe impact from a technological accident in the country's energy system that left nearly 3,500 buildings in the capital without heating. The Ukrainian leader said in his evening video address that the incident occurred Friday morning when power lines between Romania and Moldova, as well as lines on Ukrainian territory, stopped operating due to weather-related icing and automatic shutdowns. Zelenskyy said the Chernihiv, Kharkiv, Vinnytsia and Sumy regions also faced significant consequences from the grid failure. He said electricity imports have been increased and utility services promised to restore heating in Kyiv by Saturday morning, though he called for faster progress.
