## Items on Watch

The most serious item being tracked is China's potential shift from diplomatic to military responses to alliance coordination in the Asia-Pacific. Beijing has so far stuck to diplomatic pressure as Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan deepen their security coordination, but military responses to alliance infrastructure development or technology transfers would push regional tensions beyond current diplomatic sparring.

The same alliance partners face pressure from Washington's systematic implementation of burden-shifting. Japan, South Korea, and Australia are being watched for signs that the United States is moving beyond current guidelines to impose specific spending targets, capability requirements, or formal cost-sharing arrangements. Such moves could split alliance integration, creating tiers of partnership based on financial contribution rather than strategic value.

A broader institutional shift is being tracked among Western democracies facing systematic governance stress. France, Britain, Italy, Canada, and Australia are being monitored for coordination mechanisms or shared institutional responses to democratic pressures. Coordinated responses would signal that democratic allies are treating current pressures as systematic challenges requiring institutional adaptation, not just individual crisis management.
