## Regional Summary

**Solidarity on Credit**

Nordic-Baltic governments spent this week projecting solidarity and autonomy — Baltic summits in Tallinn, a French-led coalition for Hormuz shipping, Alexander Stubb, Finland's president, in Ottawa telling a Canadian audience that America risks ending up "quite alone," Sweden's king in Lviv. The performance was coherent. What lay behind it was less so. Coalitions are fracturing, fiscal arithmetic is worsening, and the Iran war — the same conflict driving the urgency — is disrupting arms deliveries, pushing debt off course, and driving up the energy costs that voters will pay. The gap between the posture these governments are adopting and the political foundations available to sustain it is the week's real story.

Finland shows the geometry most clearly. Mr Stubb's "more shouting than whispering" remark to Canadian audiences was the sharpest public break Helsinki has made with Washington in years, and his reading of Hungary's election — "a huge relief for international liberals" — reflected a real interest: Péter Magyar's majority removes the main EU veto on a €90 billion Ukraine loan. But domestic conditions are harder to manage. Lulu Ranne, the Finns Party transport minister, told a regional paper that a rail project "won't proceed this government term" — a direct contradiction of the coalition programme. Petteri Orpo, the prime minister, had to correct her on camera. She retracted the comment, but the operating company acknowledged construction was unlikely before 2028. NATO has placed infrastructure requirements on Finland that compete directly with that project's funding — the alliance's own logistics demands are now pulling the coalition's investment priorities apart. Sixty-six percent of Finns rate the government poorly — its worst result this term. The spring budget, needing €400 million in additional savings after the Iran war pushed its debt off course, opens in these conditions. A minister who told parliament that the welfare system must be fair to those who "wake up in the morning to go to a low-paid job while their neighbour stays asleep" has handed the opposition a phrase to pin to that disapproval rating ahead of 2027.

The credibility problem is sharpest in Lithuania, where the gap between what governments say and what they have prepared proved hard to close. After Inga Ruginienė, the prime minister, told parliament in January that Lithuania has "Plan B" and "Plan C" for a scenario in which NATO support collapses, the national security committee held a closed session to hear them. The opposition emerged unsatisfied: what the government presented, said the committee's vice-chair, was standard NATO planning frameworks, not contingency plans. The Social Democrats, meanwhile, spent two hours weighing whether to expel their coalition partner — and chose to wait a month for their party congress — while a bribery indictment against Saulius Skvernelis, alleging €51,000 in cash bribes channelled through a money-laundering scheme, closed off one of the few paths to an alternative majority. Someone then found an audio-recording pen in the room. Whether a journalist from Lithuanian Radio and Television left it or not, the accusation deepened a conflict with a public broadcaster that the same parties are trying to weaken through legislation — a combination of dysfunction and institutional pressure that the opposition is now taking to the president. Estonia's government faces a different version of the same squeeze. Its three coalition parties hold 16% of public support combined; Margus Tsahkna, the foreign minister, was contradicted by his own past votes on an EU directive his party now calls "ideological"; and the Internal Security Service (KAPO)'s annual report — naming China as a serious domestic threat alongside Russia for the first time — landed alongside confirmation that arms deliveries from the United States are being delayed by the same Iran war driving the urgency.

Latvia and Norway are reaching further than their immediate neighbourhood usually requires. Evika Siliņa, Latvia's prime minister, joined the French- and British-led Hormuz call despite Latvia having no navy for such a mission; Norway pledged to explore a contribution to the same operation, from which the United States — at war with Iran — is excluded. Both moves signal commitment to European security arrangements that do not depend on Washington. But Latvia's Fiscal Discipline Council warned this week that without tax rises or spending cuts, fiscal space could turn negative by 2028, with defence commitments above 5% of GDP generating deficits of 3–5% while current forecasts do not fully account for pension obligations and Rail Baltica costs. Sweden's governing coalition, trailing in polls ahead of a September election, is watching households carrying debt equal to roughly 151% of income face energy-driven mortgage increases that commercial banks have already imposed ahead of any central bank decision. Solidarity expressed at coalition summits and through royal visits to Lviv does not pay those bills.

The Iran war is the connective tissue. It delays the High Mobility Artillery Rocket System (HIMARS) ammunition that Lithuania and Estonia have contracted, raises the energy costs that Sweden's central bank and Latvia's Fiscal Discipline Council are warning about, and forces governments to find savings at the very moment they are committing to spend more on defence. None of these governments is near collapse. But the architecture of European autonomy they are building — summits, maritime coalitions, declarations of strategic depth — rests on domestic political foundations that are narrowing as elections in Latvia in October, Sweden in September, and Estonia and Lithuania in 2027 draw closer. Governments that cannot answer their own parliaments' questions about contingency plans, keep ministers in line, or match defence spending to what they can afford are poorly placed to honour what they are promising. The posture is ahead of the politics, and the distance between them is growing.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/fi.svg" alt="Finland flag" style={{ width: "24px", height: "18px", display: "inline", verticalAlign: "middle", marginRight: "8px" }} /> Finland

Finland's diplomatic posture has shifted from routine alliance-management to active liberal-order leadership: President Stubb's Ottawa summit with Canada's Carney, explicit public distancing from Trump ('more shouting than whispering'), open acknowledgement of planning for potential US NATO withdrawal, and effusive celebration of Orbán's electoral defeat collectively represent the most assertive Finnish diplomatic positioning in years, with Stubb and Carney now publicly framed as intellectual co-leaders of a centrist counter-movement. Simultaneously, Finland's domestic legitimacy crisis has deepened to historically severe levels: term-worst 66% negative approval (fifth-worst since 1995 tracking), the first visible Finns Party coalition programme rupture over Länsirata rail, and a government that survived its 14th interpellation while generating a durable 'contemptuous of jobseekers' reputational liability. The spring budget framework (kehysriihi) locks in continued austerity with €400 million in additional required savings and PM Orpo's €8 billion+ pre-election fiscal adjustment commitment, extending the coalition's economic political liability through the entire 2027 campaign. Security posture has stabilised following last week's hybrid threat escalation, with no new incidents this reporting period. The Hungary election's removal of Orbán's EU blocking role is assessed by Finnish leaders as a structural improvement for Ukraine sanctions architecture — Finland's most direct EU institutional interest.

**Key developments:**

- **Diplomatic:** During his April 14–15 working visit to Ottawa, Stubb gave a CBC interview in which he described the Trump relationship as 'more shouting than whispering,' said Trump has 'very much his own mind,' and warned the US risks ending up 'quite alone.' WSJ (per story map) reported Stubb as one of a handful of European leaders maintaining close Trump ties while simultaneously planning for potential US NATO withdrawal. The joint statement with PM Carney was titled 'We are stronger together' and framed around 'value-based realism' and shared defence of rules-based order. Politico characterised Stubb and Carney as 'intellectual leaders of a new counter-movement' co-leading centrist liberal resistance to Trumpian disruption of the rules-based order. Stubb noted that he and Trump message each other frequently and the relationship remains operative, but the public framing was the most explicit distancing Stubb has deployed in any foreign media forum.
- **Diplomatic:** PM Orpo called the Hungarian election result 'a turning point for all of Europe,' directly criticised Orbán for repeatedly blocking EU decisions on Russia sanctions and Ukraine support, and stated he had already contacted incoming PM Magyar to congratulate him and welcome Hungary back to European cooperation. Stubb said he had been 'smiling for four days' and called it 'a huge relief for international liberals.' Orpo noted that Tisza's supermajority creates the conditions to restore Hungarian democratic institutions, and expressed confidence this will unblock EU decision-making on the €90bn Ukraine loan already agreed in principle. Orbán's backers included Trump and Putin; Finnish leaders noted that Hungarians 'made their own decision, uninfluenced from East or West.'
- **Diplomatic:** President Stubb conducted Finland's first presidential visit to Jordan since Halonen in 2010, meeting King Abdullah II, Crown Prince Al Hussein, the PM, Senate and House speakers, and Jordanian Armed Forces leadership. Topics included Iran, Hormuz, the Middle East, Ukraine, and global challenges. The Egypt visit (April 21–22, Finland's first presidential visit since Halonen in 2009) included President al-Sisi, PM Madbouly, the Grand Imam of Al-Azhar, the Arab League Secretary-General, and an address to Arab League permanent representatives, accompanied by a 19-company Finnish business delegation including Nokia, KONE, Metso, Stora Enso, and Elisa. On April 17, Stubb also participated in a Macron-convened virtual 51-country meeting on Strait of Hormuz freedom of navigation. Separately, Stubb phoned Zelenskyy on April 19 to discuss Ukraine and the Iran war's implications.
- **Domestic:** The Verian party barometer (conducted March 25–31, n=1,335) found 66% of respondents consider the Orpo government to have performed poorly — 39% 'poorly,' 27% 'fairly poorly.' Only 27% gave a positive assessment. This is the term's lowest result and the fifth-worst outcome since the series began in 1995; only the Sipilä and Alexander Stubb governments reached lower at their nadirs (20–23%). SDP received positive ratings from 46%, Centre from 45%. Kokoomus was rated most arrogant by 55% of respondents. The survey showed broad dissatisfaction with both government and opposition, with 57% rating the opposition as having performed poorly in providing alternatives. Importantly, the survey was conducted before the current week's confidence vote and Länsirata controversy.
- **Domestic:** Transport Minister Lulu Ranne (Finns Party) told Sisä-Suomen Lehti that Länsirata construction 'is stuck and won't proceed this government term,' triggering immediate contradiction from PM Orpo, who told reporters the project 'proceeds entirely normally' under the coalition programme and that 'nothing has been decided anywhere' about delay. Ranne subsequently clarified on X and to STT that the coalition remains committed to advancing the project, but the company operating Länsirata Oy acknowledged construction would unlikely begin before 2028. Orpo labelled Centre Party leader Kaikkonen's criticism 'politicking,' but acknowledged no government asset sales for the project are currently planned despite earlier commitments. Separately, Kauppalehti reported that NATO has placed infrastructure requirements on Finland including the Arctic rail corridor to Narvik, and that sources indicate some government politicians favour redirecting Länsirata's €385 million toward the militarily prioritised Arctic route.
- **Domestic:** Three opposition parties (SDP, Left Alliance, Greens) filed an interpellation accusing the government of knowingly increasing poverty and inequality. The debate was dominated by Social Security Minister Grahn-Laasonen's comment: 'The system must also be fair to those who wake up in the morning to go to a low-paid job while their neighbour stays asleep.' The left-wing opposition characterised this as 'incredibly arrogant' and implying jobseekers are lazy; both Grahn-Laasonen and Orpo disputed this reading. The government pointed to international turmoil (including the Iran war) as the primary driver of unemployment, which stands at Finland's second-highest EU rate. The government survived the confidence vote 88-63, with approximately 49 MPs absent. The Centre Party and Liike Nyt did not participate in the interpellation, indicating they declined to weaponise the poverty critique despite being opposition parties.
- **Security:** The government submitted RP 55/2026, amending the Act on the Finnish Defence Forces to raise mandatory retirement ages in line with general public sector longevity adjustments. Separately, Kauppalehti's pre-kehysriihi analysis confirmed drone defence funding is on the agenda for the April 22 framework session. The article also noted NATO has placed infrastructure improvement requirements on Finland, and that internal government sources indicate some politicians favour redirecting Länsirata capital to the Jäämeri (Arctic sea) rail link from Narvik to Tornio, which NATO considers a more critical military mobility corridor. The government security policy report supplementing the foreign and security policy review was published, though full content was not extracted.
- **Economic:** PM Orpo pledged in a Kauppalehti interview that Kokoomus would present a list of at least €8 billion in fiscal adjustments before the spring 2027 elections, encompassing spending cuts, tax increases, and structural reforms. The kehysriihi requires identifying €400 million in additional savings after Iran war-driven economic deterioration pushed the debt stabilisation target out of reach. Preliminary agreement was reached on YEL entrepreneur pension reform, though Purra cautioned that final details depend on the overall framework outcome. The Iran war's effect on growth forecasts was cited as the key external constraint, with the Finance Ministry's December 2025 1.1% GDP growth projection now effectively obsolete. Kauppalehti noted that the framework session faces a nearly impossible fiscal arithmetic — finding savings while simultaneously funding drone defence and growth-supporting measures.
- **Institutional:** On April 17, Stubb participated in a virtual multilateral meeting on Hormuz freedom of navigation convened by French President Macron involving 51 countries; the meeting produced a Macron-Starmer co-chair joint statement. The same day, Stubb met Finnish MEPs at the Presidential Palace. RP 65/2026 was submitted, amending athlete pension insurance to allow fund-linked and fixed-term alternatives and small lump-sum payouts (entry into force 1 July 2026). A government security policy report was published assessing changes in the security environment for Finland and Europe, supplementing the foreign and security policy review — full content not extracted.

- **Unexpected:** NATO-flagged tension between Länsirata high-speed rail and Arctic military logistics corridor surfaces publicly. Kauppalehti reported that NATO has placed infrastructure improvement requirements on Finland, with a key requirement being an unbroken rail connection from Norway's Narvik via Sweden's Haparanda to Finland. Sources indicate some government politicians favour redirecting Länsirata's allocated capital toward this Arctic corridor. This surfaces a previously non-public tension between Finland's civilian infrastructure investment priorities and NATO's military logistics demands — potentially making the Länsirata dispute structurally more complex than a coalition communications failure.

- **Notable absence:** Follow-on hybrid threat incidents following last week's explosive-equipped drone finds on Finnish territory — No new documented drone or hybrid threat incidents this reporting period. The acute escalation of last week has not continued, suggesting either a pause in Russian hybrid operations or successful deterrence through the military response and government signalling. The absence is analytically notable given the prior week's escalation trajectory — it lowers the probability that last week represented the beginning of a sustained escalation campaign.
- **Notable absence:** Finance Minister Purra public statements on kehysriihi priorities, given her previous high-profile fiscal communications — Purra appeared less prominently in this week's coverage than her pattern in prior weeks would suggest, given that the kehysriihi is the government's most politically consequential fiscal event. A Verkkouutiset snippet quoted her calling for moderation on wish-lists, but major Purra statements anticipated on the basis of her prior week pattern did not emerge. This may reflect deliberate restraint before the framework session outcome, or a communications strategy shift following the 66% negative approval rating.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/ee.svg" alt="Estonia flag" style={{ width: "24px", height: "18px", display: "inline", verticalAlign: "middle", marginRight: "8px" }} /> Estonia

Estonia this week maintained its foundational security reorientation (€500M procurement shift, Narva base) while managing two new complications: US HIMARS ammunition delivery delays due to Iran conflict diversion, requiring active pursuit of South Korean alternative sourcing, and a KAPO annual report that expands the threat picture beyond Russia to include Chinese influence operations for the first time at prominent institutional disclosure level. Diplomatically, Estonia produced a concentrated week of pro-NATO, pro-Ukraine alignment signals — Pevkur's Reuters reaffirmation of US trust, coordinated Baltic airspace denial to Fico, PM Michal's explicit Orbán condemnation, and the Karis Lithuania state visit — all consistent with established posture but unusually dense in a single week. Domestically, the regime stress picture has intensified: the Karis-Tsahkna dispute has entered systemic 'Orbánism' debate territory, EKRE's polling collapse to 11% is accompanied by rhetorical radicalisation (Helme on Hungary, Viljandi councillor post), the governing coalition shows visible EU policy incoherence with Eesti 200 contradicting Tsahkna, and Isamaa consolidates at 24% as the modal next government. The March 2027 election horizon is now the dominant organising pressure driving behaviour across all five categories simultaneously.

**Key developments:**

- **Domestic:** The public dispute between President Karis and Foreign Minister Tsahkna over MFA capacity (Karis criticised the ministry as lacking 'people, skills, or sometimes knowledge to take a long-term view') escalated this week. Tsahkna and Isamaa leader Reinsalu faced off in a radio debate, each accusing the other of 'Orbánism.' Tsahkna stated that second terms in high state offices 'have not justified themselves,' widely read as a signal against Karis's second-term bid. Postimees analysis confirms Karis remains personally popular but the governing coalition prefers a different candidate, and the row has not materially shifted the presidential race landscape. The debate's 'Orbánism' framing — with both sides hurling the accusation — reflects Hungary's Orbán defeat resonating immediately in Estonian political discourse.
- **Domestic:** The April 2026 Turu-uuringud poll (conducted April 2–8, n=876) shows: Isamaa 24%, Centre 23%, SDE 14%, Reform 14%, EKRE 11%, Parempoolsed 8%, Estonia 200 2%. EKRE has dropped 4 percentage points since January. The coalition parties combined at 16% (up from 14% in March), but this is historically unprecedented weakness for a governing coalition. Opposition parties combined hold 72% support — unchanged from March. Delfi notes that Isamaa and Centre theoretically have enough combined support for a parliamentary majority, though that combination is politically implausible given Centre's Russia-association toxicity. Estonia 200 at 2% is below both the 5% electoral threshold and the 2% state support threshold.
- **Domestic:** EKRE chairman Martin Helme publicly called Viktor Orbán's electoral defeat 'a dark day for nationalists and conservatives' and stated in a radio interview he would have declared a state of emergency had he been in Orbán's government. Helme also dismissed calls to respect the Hungarian election results as 'naïve' ('the game is played by different rules'). Separately, Viljandi city councillor Tõnis Tulp, an EKRE member, posted on social media under Helme's Hungary commentary calling people to 'wake up and take up arms, as nothing else will help anymore.' When contacted by Sakala, Tulp denied calling for literal armed violence but declined to rule out 'measures on or near the legal boundary.' No criminal investigation was reported.
- **Diplomatic:** Speaking to Reuters in Vilnius on April 17, Defence Minister Pevkur stated Estonia has 'no doubt' the US would come to its defence if Russia attacked, compared NATO's current strains to 'a long marriage,' and explicitly acknowledged Europe is 'not ready' to face Russia without the US. He called for NATO to help resolve the Iran conflict to allow the US to refocus on Ukraine, and noted Estonia will slightly exceed the 5% GDP defence target at 5.1%. The visit was bilateral with Lithuanian counterpart Robertas Kaunas, with both ministers citing their defence spending as a model for NATO allies. The 17-source media pickup confirms this was received internationally as a calibrated signal during alliance uncertainty, not routine commentary.
- **Diplomatic:** Foreign Minister Tsahkna confirmed Estonia will again deny airspace access to Slovak PM Fico for travel to Moscow's May 9 Victory Day parade, repeating the 2025 action. All three Baltic states acted in coordination. Tsahkna stated: 'No country may use our airspace to strengthen ties with Russia at a time when Russia continues to violate international norms and wage aggression against Ukraine.' Ukrainian FM Sybiha publicly thanked the Baltic states. Fico indicated he will again find an alternative route.
- **Diplomatic:** At the April 17 Baltic Council of Ministers meeting in Tallinn — held at the Ülemiste Rail Baltic terminal as a symbolic venue — PM Michal congratulated Hungarian opposition leader Peter Magyar on his election victory and confirmed a personal call and Tallinn invitation. In the same press conference, Michal explicitly named Orbán alongside Fico and Babiš as the three EU leaders who refused the December-agreed €90 billion Ukraine support package, singling out Orbán for then 'actively blocking it' and characterising this as 'unprecedented in European history.' He also acknowledged that Estonian foreign policy lacks 'steam' to think beyond the immediate Ukraine crisis — a notable concession echoing his MFA capacity critique from Story #3. The visit included inspection of the Rukla military base and discussion of Rail Baltica. First lady Sirje Karis visited Ukrainian refugee centres.
- **Security:** KAPO's 2025 annual report, released April 14, disclosed that 16 individuals were detained for cooperating with Russian intelligence agencies — a record. Nine were formally designated as 'agents'; residency permits of Russian Orthodox Church clergy were revoked on security grounds. The report prominently flagged Chinese influence operations: sponsored trips to China targeting local government politicians, media figures, journalists, and content creators (one specific October 2024 trip involving Harju Elu journalist Askur Alas and MK Estonia staffer Galina Streltsova is named). KAPO also identified Telegram as the primary current disinformation vector and noted non-military threats (sabotage, fake bomb reports) are growing. The Delfi article specifically reports Kaitseliit members are being offered as conduits to FSB.
- **Security:** Defence Minister Pevkur confirmed Estonia has been notified of US arms delivery pauses, primarily affecting HIMARS rocket ammunition scheduled for delivery in 2026. Former EDF commander Martin Herem stated delays of under three years are manageable, but beyond that, alternatives should be sought — notably the existing South Korean Hanwha contract. Pevkur noted theoretical alternatives (third-party HIMARS-compatible ammunition) require Lockheed Martin and US government approval. A call with US Defence Minister Pete Hegseth was scheduled for Monday. Herem also noted that Ukraine faces similar HIMARS shortfalls, as NATO allies have been purchasing US-origin weapons for Ukraine.

Economy Minister Keldo confirmed Estonia may accept infringement fines if Brussels refuses. Postimees immediately published evidence that Tsahkna personally approved the directive three years earlier. Eesti 200 party leader Kristina Kallas subsequently issued a contradictory statement saying 'Estonia must adopt measures that reduce the gender pay gap.' SDE leader Läänemets called it a step away from 'European values.' Eesti 200 advisor Daniel Kõiv publicly called it a 'short-sighted' decision. Estonia has one of the EU's widest gender pay gaps.
- **Economic:** The Estonian parliament passed legislation on April 12–14 cancelling planned increases to excise duties on alcohol, tobacco, fuel, and electricity, which had been scheduled for May 1. Finance committee chair Annely Akkermann (Reform) attributed the action explicitly to rising energy prices from the Middle East conflict. The bill passed both readings during the week.

- **Unexpected:** KAPO 2025 report explicitly flags Chinese influence operations as a significant and growing national security concern. Prior KAPO annual reports and Estonian security discourse have been almost exclusively Russia-focused. The explicit and prominent inclusion of Chinese influence operations — with named individuals and sponsored trip details — represents a doctrinal expansion of Estonia's publicly stated threat perception. This is an early signal that Estonian security institutions are beginning to treat China as a co-vector of hybrid influence, not merely a peripheral concern. It has implications for both domestic policy (media figures accepting Chinese-sponsored trips) and for Estonia's positioning within EU debates on China policy.
- **Unexpected:** Eesti 200 party internally contradicts coalition partner Tsahkna's EU pay transparency directive position at joint government press conference. The public contradiction of Tsahkna's position by Eesti 200's own party leader (Kallas) and party advisor (Kõiv) on the same day as the joint government press conference is an unusual degree of public coalition incoherence. The governing coalition already operates on a 52/101 majority; visible ministerial contradictions within the junior coalition partner on EU policy — a domain where Tsahkna personally reversed his own prior approval — add a new fracture line to a coalition already under severe polling pressure.

### <img src="https://flagcdn.com/lt.svg" alt="Lithuania flag" style={{ width: "24px", height: "18px", display: "inline", verticalAlign: "middle", marginRight: "8px" }} /> Lithuania

Lithuania enters a period of compounding institutional pressure across its domestic and security dimensions simultaneously. The coalition governing architecture is in genuine structural uncertainty: the LSDP has publicly lost confidence in Dawn of Nemunas as a partner but defers resolution to the May 1 party congress, while the Skvernelis bribery indictment simultaneously degrades DSVL as an alternative coalition option. The May congress is now the single most important near-term domestic political event. On the security dimension, Lithuania faces two converging challenges: a confirmed US supply chain disruption (Pentagon notification of possible HIMARS and Javelin delivery delays due to the Iran war) and a political-military transparency dispute that has escalated to the State Defence Council. Neither development reverses Lithuania's foundational Western alignment or procurement modernization trajectory, but together they mark the first week in which structural US dependency has produced a concrete near-term operational consequence. The Baltic trilateral (Tallinn PM summit) continues to function as the primary multilateral coordination mechanism and reinforces the alignment posture. Lithuania's institutional architecture is working as designed — opposition uses the State Defence Council, prosecutors pursue former PMs, the constitutional court remains independent — but the simultaneity of pressures tests the current governing structure's capacity to manage them.

**Key developments:**

- **Security:** Pentagon representatives informed the Lithuanian Defense Ministry of possible delays in delivering previously contracted US military equipment due to inventory drawdown from the Iran conflict. Lithuania's outstanding US orders total approximately \$640M and include HIMARS rocket artillery systems (8 units), Black Hawk helicopters (2 units), and Javelin anti-tank missiles. Neither Lithuania nor Estonia (also informed) specified which contracted deliveries were affected or by how much. PM Ruginienė stated: 'We do not see significant problems for now. We understand that some timelines are changing, but today this does not create difficulties for Lithuania.' Latvia stated it had not been officially informed. TV3 reporting confirms the delay notice covers ammunition specifically alongside the HIMARS units. Lithuanian Armed Forces also use the US-produced Javelin system.
- **Security:** Following PM Ruginienė's January statement that Lithuania has 'Plan B' and 'Plan C' for national security in a NATO-collapse scenario, the Seimas National Security and Defence Committee (NSGK) held a closed-door session. Opposition NSGK Vice Chair Kasčiūnas (TS-LKD) stated the committee received only a presentation on standard NATO planning frameworks and 'no discussion of Plans B or C, or how to use regional alliances including cooperation with Poland.' Liberal opposition leader Čmilytė-Nielsen: 'The prime minister said the least. In my view, our questions were not answered.' Ruginienė did not comment to reporters after the meeting. Lithuanian Armed Forces Commander General Vaikšnoras said plans are continuously updated but could not discuss specific scenarios due to classification. Defence Minister Kaunas argued alternative plans were presented but questioned why anyone would 'undermine treaties' — framing the opposition's questions as politically motivated. TS-LKD subsequently announced it would appeal to the State Defence Council. President Nausėda and Estonian President Karis both stated NATO is 'certainly not coming to an end.'
- **Domestic:** General Prosecutor Nida Grunskienė applied to the Seimas to lift Seimas member Saulius Skvernelis's immunity, alleging he accepted at least €51,000 in cash bribes between January and May 2025 via former advisor Agnė Silickienė, paid from proceeds of an organized corruption scheme within the State Plant Service. Former Seimas member Kazys Starkevičius was also charged. Fifteen persons total face charges. Skvernelis proactively requested the simplified immunity-lifting procedure, suspended party membership (DSVL), and moved to the Mixed Seimas Members group. President Nausėda called the charges 'serious' and said DSVL must 'clearly distance itself and prove to voters' it is not involved. PM Ruginienė said 'let's not rush to conclusions, let's wait for the investigation.' LSDP leader Sinkevičius denied any LSDP-DSVL vote coordination agreements. The case involves an organized criminal scheme that reportedly laundered over €1.1 million in bribes through the State Plant Service over multiple years.
- **Domestic:** Following a 2+ hour LSDP board and council session, acting party leader Mindaugas Sinkevičius announced no immediate coalition decision. Three scenarios were presented: status quo (rejected), coalition reconstruction (not chosen), and deferral to new leadership elected at the May 1 LSDP congress (selected). LSDP internal polling shows 42% support continuing the current coalition, 41% favor change, ~17% undecided. Sinkevičius explicitly stated the coalition with Dawn of Nemunas 'may harm the state' because 'important decisions are taken without one partner's votes.' He identified the problem as PPNA leader Žemaitaitis personally rather than the party organization. Sinkevičius also noted that if PPNA is being considered for removal due to not voting on the Kapčiamiestis training range, a 'second partner' (LVŽS-KŠS faction) also didn't vote — complicating any clean expulsion rationale. Coalition options explicitly excluded: no coalition with TS-LKD or Liberal Movement. All other combinations described as 'worth considering.' Sinkevičius confirmed 'minimal conversations' with unnamed parties, and separately 'minimal conversations' with DSVL were mentioned — though the Skvernelis case now complicates that option. Analyst commentary (vz.lt, Urbonaite): 'nothing good' for the coalition's future.
- **Domestic:** A pen containing a hidden audio recorder was discovered at the LSDP party council session where coalition future scenarios were being discussed in closed session. Acting LSDP chair Sinkevičius publicly accused LRT journalist Eglė Samoškaitė of leaving the device. Samoškaitė denied the allegations and subsequently withdrew from a separate journalists' protest initiative. Police were contacted. The incident occurred on the same day as the coalition deferral announcement, at the same session where the three coalition scenarios were discussed.
- **Diplomatic:** PM Ruginienė joined Estonian PM Michal and Latvian PM Siliņa in Tallinn for the Baltic Ministerial Council PM meeting. Ruginienė stated at the joint press conference that air defense is 'no longer optional, it is mandatory,' called for NATO to strengthen air and missile defense (including counter-drone and balloon response), and declared the transatlantic link 'vitally important.' The three PMs confirmed continued support for Ukraine and called for Europe to be fully involved in shaping any future peace agreements. Meeting opened at the Rail Baltica Ülemiste terminal, where 43% of mainline construction is underway. Ruginienė pressed for 'political will' to meet the 2030 deadline and noted EU budget proposals for Rail Baltica funding are 'quite positive.' Latvia's Siliņa and Estonia's Michal subsequently joined a French-UK-hosted video call on Hormuz freedom of navigation; Ruginienė's participation in that call was not reported.
- **Economic:** Rail Baltica CEO Kivila reported 43% of corridor mainline construction started. Total project cost has been revised down to ~€15B by eliminating some stations and limiting to a single track. Latvia's PM Siliņa acknowledged Latvia faces the most difficult situation — most construction concentrated near Riga, main line funding still needed. New EU multi-year budget proposals described as 'quite positive' but must still be agreed with all member states. RB Rail identified three 2026 priorities: CCS contract alignment, rolling stock firm orders, and closing the EU financing gap. Ruginienė called for 'political will' to meet the 2030 deadline.
- **Institutional:** Baltic PMs confirmed continued military, air defense, and financial support for Ukraine. They assessed hybrid threats including drone and balloon incidents in the region, noting such events are direct consequences of Russia's war of aggression and may recur. The meeting reinforced the Baltic states' joint position that Europe must be 'fully involved' in shaping any future peace agreements. The format (Baltic Ministerial Council at PM level) is a structured trilateral mechanism, not a one-off summit.

- **Unexpected:** Latvia not officially informed of US arms delivery delays despite sharing Baltic security architecture. The asymmetry in US notification — Lithuania and Estonia informed, Latvia not yet — is analytically unexpected. All three Baltic states are in joint procurement programs (CV90) and share the same threat environment. The asymmetry may reflect differences in direct US bilateral defense cooperation depth (Lithuania and Estonia both have larger direct US procurement footprints), but it also raises a question about whether US-Baltic security coordination is operating as seamlessly as the trilateral format implies. Worth monitoring for whether Latvia is subsequently informed or whether the gap persists.

- **Notable absence:** LRT reform legislation vote or further procedural advancement, given prior weeks showed the coalition fast-tracking amendments before Venice Commission opposition — No LRT reform vote or committee advancement was reported this week despite prior pressure. The absence may reflect deliberate LSDP decision to avoid inflaming the situation during the Skvernelis crisis week, or parliamentary schedule limitations. The recording device incident involving an LRT journalist introduces a new variable — public accusations against LRT staff while the LRT governance reform is pending may complicate the legislative push. The absence is likely a tactical pause rather than a policy retreat, but given the Venice Commission and RSF condemnations, monitoring whether the pause extends through the May congress is analytically significant.
- **Notable absence:** German 45th Panzer Brigade deployment milestone update, given the brigade activated April 1, 2025 and is on a path to 4,800 troops by 2027 — No Bundeswehr or Lithuanian Defence Ministry update on brigade deployment progress this week. Given this is listed as Watch Indicator 1 in the dossier (§19), absence of reporting is baseline rather than significant — brigade milestone updates are quarterly, not weekly. No analytical inference drawn from this specific absence.

Latvia has entered the pre-election campaign period (October 3, 2026) under acute domestic political stress from converging accountability investigations and a near-coalition-collapse, while maintaining proactive and expanding external positioning within Western security frameworks. The governing coalition survived its most serious crisis since the Istanbul Convention vote — PM Siliņa publicly declaring willingness to sacrifice the coalition over airBaltic support before ZZS backed down — but accumulated simultaneous scandals (VIP lounge KNAB review, Lake Como seminar scrutiny, party ratings decline) are generating measurable 'privilege aura' political damage to New Unity. KNAB, presidential oversight, and independent media are functioning as institutional accountability mechanisms; the system is working, but the governing coalition's political capital is depleting ahead of elections. Externally, Latvia's alignment posture shows modest scope extension with Siliņa's expression of support for a UK-France-led Hormuz freedom-of-navigation coalition — the first out-of-area security initiative engagement — and continued Baltic trilateral Rail Baltica coordination. The Fiscal Discipline Council's formal warning about structural budget deterioration (fiscal space potentially negative from 2028, defence spending above 5% GDP from 2027 driving 3-5% deficits) confirms the defense-pension fiscal impossible trinity as an active structural constraint, not merely a theoretical risk. A new Fuel Traders Solidarity Payment Law adds an interventionist domestic price management tool. The EPPO IT procurement corruption case has now escalated to directly implicate election infrastructure legitimacy, prompting presidential intervention on the electronic voter register ahead of the October elections.

**Key developments:**

- **Domestic:** ZZS issued a three-point ultimatum demanding Transport Minister Švinka's (Progressives) resignation before it would support the €30 million state loan to airBaltic. PM Siliņa publicly declared she was ready to let the coalition collapse rather than concede. After crisis meetings on April 16, coalition partners agreed to continue; the Saeima approved the loan. Under the resolution, Švinka must deliver a path to airBaltic profitability or face accountability. Siliņa ordered a Transport Ministry reorganization. Economics Ministry impact assessment (produced per Siliņa directive of April 11) showed airBaltic accounts for 57% of Riga Airport volume, €779.3M 2025 revenue (net loss €44.3M). President Rinkēvičs called for serious restructuring and said he was not prepared to comment on the loan decision.
- **Domestic:** Former State Chancellery Director Citskovskis — himself facing a criminal case — alleged Siliņa's 2024 New York visit incurred a €4,184 state bill for VIP lounge use at Amsterdam Schiphol, which Citskovskis claimed he refused to sign and was subsequently suspended. He also alleged former PM Kariņš improperly sought reimbursement for his daughter's VIP lounge use (costs later deducted from Kariņš's salary). KNAB announced it would assess the allegations. Siliņa threatened a defamation lawsuit against Citskovskis. President Rinkēvičs defended VIP use by top officials on security grounds but said Siliņa should explain her specific case herself.
- **Domestic:** Multiple New Unity ministers including Siliņa took personal vacation time to attend a three-day seminar at the Konrad Adenauer Foundation's luxury Villa La Collina on Lake Como, Italy. KNAB is examining whether the trip's costs constitute an impermissible in-kind contribution from an NGO to a political party. Political analyst Iveta Kažoka assessed in LSM polling analysis that this 'governing style indelibly associated with privileges' is damaging both JV and ZZS support, with more than a quarter of voters undecided and multiple new 'change message' parties benefiting. She assessed that ZZS is performing worse than JV and risks losing its established voter base to alternatives.

A court has issued an arrest warrant and is seeking Rosļikovs internationally.

Siliņa acknowledged Latvia's funding shortfalls, the need for cost reductions, and said construction would span several governments. Construction is underway on 43% of the corridor. Separately, following the Baltic summit, Siliņa and Michal joined a video call hosted by Macron and Starmer on Strait of Hormuz freedom of navigation; Siliņa expressed Latvia's support for an international coalition and emphasized the need to work with the United States on the issue.
