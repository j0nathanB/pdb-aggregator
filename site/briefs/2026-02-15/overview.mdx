## Week of February 09, 2026


America's allies are taking control from Washington, and they're doing it everywhere at once. NATO is handing command jobs from America to Europe, with Poland taking over a NATO command. Nordic countries are running Arctic operations without American oversight. In the Pacific, Taiwan, Japan and Australia are building new security infrastructure without asking Washington first. This isn't routine burden-sharing — it's permanent transfers of control that may not reverse even if American policy changes.

At the same time, Western governments are being hit with political scandals that all trace back to Jeffrey Epstein. France has a diplomat implicated, Britain's Mandelson-Epstein scandal is tearing apart Starmer's inner circle, and Norway has filed corruption charges against former Prime Minister Jagland. The timing suggests these revelations were released together rather than discovered separately, though evidence of direct coordination remains thin. If this is a planned operation, it shows sophisticated targeting of senior Western politicians when their governments are already weak.

Meanwhile, middle powers are giving up on playing all sides. Turkey, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and India all face visible contradictions in trying to hedge between great powers, while Canada and Mexico are pursuing completely different approaches to managing their American relationships. Rather than work together as middle powers traditionally have, each country is going it alone with strategies that expose the fundamental conflicts between different great power relationships.

These developments show the international system is breaking apart on multiple fronts — alliance structures adapting to reduced American leadership, Western political establishments under information attack, and middle power coordination collapsing. The breadth of stress hitting institutions at once indicates that great power competition is intensifying faster than the mechanisms designed to manage it can adapt.
