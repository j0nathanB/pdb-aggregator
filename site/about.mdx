The Middle Powers Monitor is a weekly intelligence publication tracking 28 countries across five regions. It analyzes state positioning through five analytical dimensions: diplomatic alignment, security posture, economic statecraft, institutional engagement, and domestic constraints.

## Coverage

The Monitor tracks countries that occupy the strategic space between great powers and smaller states — nations whose positioning decisions carry outsized consequences for regional and global order.

## Analytical Framework

Each country is assessed weekly across five signal categories:

- **Diplomatic alignment** — bilateral and multilateral positioning, alliance management, hedging behavior
- **Security & defense** — military posture, procurement, operational deployments, intelligence cooperation
- **Economic statecraft** — trade policy, sanctions compliance, investment flows, energy strategy
- **Institutional engagement** — participation in international organizations, treaty behavior, norm-setting
- **Domestic constraints** — regime stability, public opinion, electoral dynamics, civil-military relations

## Methodology

The Monitor uses a tiered analytical pipeline. Countries are triaged weekly based on signal activity, with high-activity countries receiving full deep-dive treatment and quieter countries held at maintenance. Regional synthesis identifies cross-cutting dynamics, and an executive layer surfaces system-level patterns.

Country dossiers provide the structural context that informs weekly analysis — the institutional landscape, key actors, blind spots, and historical patterns that shape how signals should be interpreted.
