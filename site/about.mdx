## From the Editor

On [January 20, 2026](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2026/01/davos-2026-special-address-by-mark-carney-prime-minister-of-canada/), Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney named a rupture in the rules-based international order. The old world order is dead, he said, but the middle powers have the capacity to build a new one grounded in human rights, sustainable development, solidarity, and the sovereignty and territorial integrity of states.

The Middle Powers Monitor emerges from that reality. Its goal is to track the old international order as it evolves into a new one. As this shift occurs in defence commitments, trade policy, and domestic politics, no single story or outlet captures it. While a reader who subscribes to Reuters, the BBC, Foreign Affairs and a handful of regional papers will find no shortage of good reporting on these topics, they will find that there are not enough hours in the week to read the volume needed to hold the full picture together across 28 countries. So it is built on the model of an intelligence brief: wide sourcing across dozens of outlets, tight selection, and enough context to make sense of it — so that twenty minutes on a Monday morning leaves a reader with a clearer picture of the world than a week of scattered reading would.

Each country earns its place not by GDP, G20 membership or geographic balance, though the list covers 14 of 20 G20 members and spans five regions. The selection turns on a single question: is this country doing the work of sustaining the liberal international order, or merely saying that it does?

The Monitor publishes every Monday. It is free to read and will remain so. Corrections, objections and tips are welcome at editor@middlepowers.fyi.
